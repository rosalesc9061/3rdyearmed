🛡️ Active vs. Passive Immunity

Feature Active Immunity Passive Immunity
Mechanism Stimulation of body's own immune system to produce antibodies and T-cells. Administration of preformed antibodies (human or animal).
Onset & Duration Delayed onset; Long-lasting / Prolonged (years/lifetime via memory). Immediate yet transient (weeks to months).
Sources Natural infection or Vaccines/Toxoids. Endogenous: Transplacental IgG transfer.
Exogenous: Blood products (WB, PRBC, Platelets), IVIG, IMIG, RIG, HBIG, TIG, Monoclonals.
Primary Indications Routine protection prior to exposure. Emergency PEP (e.g., Rabies PEP, Tetanus wound PEP, HepB newborn of HBsAg+ mother) or B-cell immunodeficiencies.
💡 Clinical Pearl: Ideal Vaccine Attributes

An ideal vaccine must be: Safe (no severe reactions or disease induction), Immunogenic (provokes high specific antibody levels), Efficacious (reduces real-world disease rates in RCTs), Long-lasting, and Cost-effective for national health programs.

⏱️ Strict Spacing & Timing Rules

⭐ Spacing Rules Summary
  • ≥2 Inactivated antigens: May give simultaneously or at ANY interval.
  • Inactivated + Live antigens: May give simultaneously or at ANY interval.
  • ≥2 Live Parenteral antigens: Must give SIMULTANEOUSLY on same day OR separated by ≥4 WEEKS minimum interval. (e.g., MMR and Varicella).
  • ≥2 Live Oral antigens: May give simultaneously or at ANY interval (e.g., OPV + Rotavirus).
  • EXEMPTION: Tdap and MCV4 should be separated by ≥4 weeks if not given simultaneously.
⚠️ The 4-Day Grace Period Rule & Extended Intervals

Grace Period: Doses administered ≤4 days before minimum age or interval are counted as valid. Doses administered ≥5 days early are INVALID and must be repeated spaced by the recommended minimum interval.

CRITICAL EXCEPTION: The 4-day grace period DOES NOT APPLY TO RABIES VACCINE!

Extended Intervals: Interrupting or delaying a series DOES NOT decrease total potency or final antibody titers. NEVER RESTART A VACCINE SERIES due to a lapse in time!

📋 The Vaccine Visit, Algorithms & Republic Act 10152

Clinical workflow, avoiding missed opportunities, counseling frameworks, and legal mandates.

🔄 The 6-Step Vaccine Algorithm

  1. Step 1: VERIFY — Verify age, written records, and prior valid doses.
  2. Step 2: ASSESS — Assess current illness, allergies, immunocompromise, pregnancy, or recent blood products/immunoglobulins.
  3. Step 3: COMPARE — Compare patient record against national NIP / PPS-PIDSP schedules and catch-up tables.
  4. Step 4: SELECT — Select all indicated vaccines due today. Maximize co-administration!
  5. Step 5: ADMINISTER — Administer correct product, dose, route, anatomical site, and needle size.
  6. Step 6: DOCUMENT — Document lot #, expiration, site, date, set next due date, and counsel on AEFI.
⚠️ Biggest Preventable Error: Missed Opportunity

Failing to vaccinate an eligible child during a healthcare encounter. Mild acute illnesses (low-grade fever, URI, otitis media, mild diarrhea) are NOT valid contraindications!

⚖️ Republic Act No. 10152 & NIP Evolution

"Mandatory Infants and Children Health Immunization Act of 2011" (Enacted June 21, 2011). Mandates free basic immunization at public health facilities.

Timeline Vaccine / Policy Integration
1976 (EPI) Original 6 VPDs: Tuberculosis, Diphtheria, Pertussis, Tetanus, Poliomyelitis, Measles.
2011 (RA 10152) Added Hepatitis B, Hib, Mumps, Rubella to mandatory basic list.
2012–2016 Rotavirus (2012), PCV & HPV (2013), Japanese Encephalitis (2016).
2022–2025 2nd IPV dose added at 9 months (2022). Rabies Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP) added for children 2–10 years (2025).
🧠 Vaccine Counseling: 3-Step Strategy

1. Presume: Use a confident, presumptive recommendation ("Today, your child is due for...").
2. Listen: Address specific parental concerns with empathy and factual evidence.
3. Close the Loop: Explain normal post-vaccine reactions, red flags, and confirm next due date.

📚 Comprehensive Vaccine Directory & Special Considerations

Detailed annotations for all childhood vaccines from BCG to Typhoid, plus Immunoglobulin preparations.

💉 BCG (Bacille Calmette-Guérin) ▼

Type: Live-attenuated bacterial (bovine M. bovis). Min Age: Birth. Route: ID (10–15° angle).

Dose: <12 months = 0.05 mL; ≥12 months = 0.10 mL.

Special Considerations: For infants >2 months without BCG birth dose, perform PPD/TST prior. If induration ≥5 mm or clinical/CXR TB features present → Do NOT give BCG (treat for TB).

PLHIV: Contraindicated if severely immunocompromised. Given if clinically well & CD4 >25% (<5y).

Normal Reaction Progression: Erythema (Wks 1–2) → Papule/Pus formation (Wks 2–3) → Rupture/Ulceration → Scar formation/Keloid.

💉 Hepatitis B Vaccine (HBV) & HBIG ▼

Type: Inactivated subunit. Min Age: Birth. Route: IM.

Perinatal Prophylaxis (HBsAg+ Mother): Administer HBV + HBIG (0.5 mL) within 12 hours of birth (HBIG up to 7 days max). Inject at different anatomical sites!

Mother with Unknown HBsAg: If BW ≥2kg → give HBV within 12h, test mother. If BW <2kg → give HBV + HBIG within 12h. Note: Dose 1 given at <2kg does NOT count toward 3-dose series!

💉 DTP / Td / Tdap (Diphtheria, Tetanus, Pertussis) ▼

Type: Inactivated Toxoid + Acellular/Whole-cell Pertussis. Route: IM. Min Age: DTaP/DTwP = 6 weeks; Td/Tdap = 7 years.

Schedule: 3 primary doses (6, 10, 14 wks), boosters at 12–18 mos, 4–6 yrs, and 10–18 yrs (Tdap). Td boosters q10 years.

Pregnant Adolescents: Give 1 dose Tdap during EVERY pregnancy (preferably 27–36 weeks AOG) to protect newborn against pertussis.

💉 Poliovirus Vaccines (OPV vs. IPV) ▼

OPV: Live-attenuated oral (Sabin). Mucosal IgA. Risk of cVDPV. Contraindicated in PLHIV/Immunocompromised.

IPV: Inactivated injectable (Salk). IM route. Safe for immunocompromised children & household contacts.

NIP 2026 Combined Schedule: 3 OPV doses (6, 10, 14 wks) + 2 IPV doses (14 wks and 9 mos).

💉 Pneumococcal Vaccines (PCV vs. PPSV23) ▼

PCV (Conjugate - PCV10, PCV13, PCV15, PCV20): T-cell dependent immunity, memory, effective in <2 years old. Min age 6 wks. IM route.

PPSV23 (Polysaccharide): T-cell independent immunity, NO memory, ineffective in <2 years old. Min age 2 YEARS old. Reserved for high-risk conditions (asplenia, nephrotic, HIV, chronic heart/lung disease).

Rule: Do NOT co-administer PCV and PPSV23 on same visit! Minimum interval between PCV and PPSV23 is 8 WEEKS.

💉 Measles, MMR, MMRV & Blood Product Timing ▼

Type: Live attenuated viral. Route: SC (45° angle). Min Age: Monovalent Measles/JEV = 9 mos; MMR/MMRV = 12 mos.

Outbreak Rule: Measles vaccine can be given as early as 6 months. (Does not count as valid routine dose; repeat at 9 or 12 months).

Interference with Immunoglobulins/Blood Products:

  • High-Dose IVIG (2 g/kg for Kawasaki): Delay MMR / Varicella vaccine for 11 to 12 MONTHS!
  • Rabies IG (RIG) or TIG: Delay MMR / Varicella for 4 MONTHS!
  • Packed Red Blood Cells (PRBC): Delay live vaccines for 6 months.
💉 Rabies Vaccine (PrEP vs. PEP) ▼

Type: Inactivated (PVRV or PCECV). Route: IM or ID.

2025 NIP Addition (PrEP): Routine PrEP recommended for children 2–10 years old (Days 0, 7, 21/28). High burden in PH.

CRITICAL PITFALL: NEVER inject Rabies vaccine in the GLUTEAL AREA (unpredictable tissue absorption & vaccine failure)! Inject in Deltoid or Thigh.

🛡️ Appendix: Major Indications for Immunoglobulins & Antisera

Product & Route Major Clinical Indications
IVIG (Intravenous) Kawasaki Disease (2 g/kg), Primary B-cell Immunodeficiencies, Pediatric HIV, Severe Toxic Shock Syndrome, Post-exposure Measles PEP in immunocompromised.
HBIG (Hepatitis B IG, IM) Perinatal prevention in infants born to HBsAg+ mothers; post-exposure prophylaxis.
RIG (Rabies IG, IM/Infiltration) Category III Rabies animal bite post-exposure prophylaxis (infiltrated around wound).
TIG (Tetanus IG, IM) Tetanus-prone wound prophylaxis in incomplete vaccination (<3 doses); clinical Tetanus treatment.
VariZIG (Varicella-Zoster IG, IM) PEP for high-risk susceptible individuals (immunocompromised, pregnant, neonates exposed to maternal varicella).

❄️ Vaccine Storage, Cold Chain & Post-Vaccination Care

Cold chain logistics, refrigerator organization, AEFI identification, and post-injection patient care.

🧊 Cold Chain Management & Storage

Figure 3: Proper Vaccine Refrigerator Organization (Water bottles buffer temperature fluctuations).

  • Refrigerator Temperature: Maintain strictly between 2°C to 8°C at all times.
  • Freezer Temperature: Maintain strictly between -50°C to -15°C.
  • Water Bottles Placement: Place on top shelf, bottom shelf, and door racks to stabilize internal temperature during door openings or power failures.
  • CRITICAL RULE: NEVER store food, drinks, or clinical specimens in the vaccine refrigerator!

🩹 Post-Vaccination Patient Care & AEFI Advice

💡 Standard Care Protocol

First 24 Hours: Apply COLD COMPRESS over injection site 3x a day to diminish local swelling and pain.

After 24 Hours: Apply WARM COMPRESS thereafter if induration or lump persists.

Fever & Irritability: Administer Paracetamol for post-vaccination fever and consolable fussiness.

⚠️ BCG Normal Reaction Counseling vs. AEFI

Reassure parents that a BCG papule/pus lesion at 2–3 weeks post-vaccination is expected. Do NOT squeeze or apply ointment! Wash gently with clean water.

AEFI Reporting: Report anaphylaxis, severe high fever, persistent inconsolable screaming (>3 hrs), or suppurative lymphadenitis to DOH / VAERS.

⚖️ Side-by-Side Comparison Tables

Direct, high-yield comparison tables of easily confused topic pairs for board exam mastery.

1. Live Attenuated vs. Inactivated Vaccines

Feature Live Attenuated Vaccines Inactivated Vaccines
Organism Status Weakened live microbe; capable of limited replication. Killed microbe, toxoid, subunit, or recombinant protein.
Immune Response Humoral + Strong Cell-Mediated (T-cell) Immunity. Mostly Humoral (Antibody-mediated); little/no cell-mediated.
Interference by Antibodies High (Maternal IgG or IVIG neutralizes live vaccine). Minimal/None (Can be given in presence of maternal antibodies).
Dosing Need Usually 1 or 2 doses confer lifelong protection. Requires primary multi-dose series + periodic boosters.
Immunocompromised Patients Generally CONTRAINDICATED (Risk of disease). SAFE to administer (though immunogenicity may be reduced).

2. OPV (Sabin) vs. IPV (Salk)

Feature Oral Poliovirus Vaccine (OPV) Inactivated Poliovirus Vaccine (IPV)
Type & Route Live-attenuated; Oral drops (PO). Inactivated killed virus; Intramuscular (IM) injection.
Mucosal Immunity (IgA) Excellent intestinal IgA (Interrupts community transmission). Low intestinal IgA; protects individual CNS/paralysis.
Outbreak Risk Risk of circulating Vaccine-Derived Poliovirus (cVDPV). Zero risk of VDPV or vaccine-associated paralysis.
Immunocompromised Use CONTRAINDICATED in PLHIV & contacts. SAFE & REQUIRED for immunocompromised.

3. PCV (Conjugate) vs. PPSV23 (Polysaccharide)

Feature Pneumococcal Conjugate (PCV10, 13, 15, 20) Pneumococcal Polysaccharide (PPSV23)
Structure Polysaccharide conjugated to protein carrier. Pure polysaccharide cell wall antigens.
Immune Mechanism T-cell Dependent (Induces T-helper response & Memory). T-cell Independent (B-cell activation only; No Memory).
Efficacy in <2 Years Old HIGHLY EFFICACIOUS (Starts at 6 weeks). INEFFECTIVE (Immunological immaturity <2 yrs).
Nonspecific Booster Response Yes (Antibody titers rise with booster doses). No booster effect (Hyporesponsiveness with repeated doses).

🚨 "If You See X → Think Y" Clinical Chains

Clinical Scenario → Immediate Action / Diagnosis

  • Infant born to HBsAg(+) Mother: Give HepB vaccine + HBIG (0.5 mL) within 12h of birth at separate sites.
  • Infant <2kg born to HBsAg(+) Mother: Give HepB + HBIG within 12h; birth dose does NOT count toward 3-dose series.
  • Kawasaki child received high-dose IVIG (2 g/kg): Defer MMR and Varicella vaccines for 11 to 12 months.
  • Child bitten by rabid dog and received Rabies IG (RIG): Defer live parenteral vaccines (MMR/Varicella) for 4 months.
  • Unvaccinated child receives live parenteral vaccine (MMR): Must wait ≥4 weeks before giving another live parenteral vaccine (Varicella) if not given on same day.
  • Vaccine dose given 3 days before minimum age: VALID (Within 4-day grace period).
  • Vaccine dose given 6 days before minimum age: INVALID (Must repeat dose spaced by minimum interval).
  • Rabies vaccine given 3 days before minimum interval: INVALID (Grace period NEVER applies to Rabies!).
  • Rabies vaccine accidentally injected in gluteus: INVALID (Must repeat dose in Deltoid/Thigh; gluteal absorption is unpredictable).

🔢 Must-Know High-Yield Numbers & Ages

Ages & Intervals Summary

  • BCG Dose: <12 months = 0.05 mL | ≥12 months = 0.10 mL.
  • Minimum Age for DTP / PCV / Hib / OPV / Rotavirus: 6 weeks.
  • Minimum Age for Measles (Monovalent/JEV): 9 months.
  • Minimum Age for MMR / Varicella / HAV (Inactivated): 12 months.
  • Minimum Age for Rabies PrEP / PPSV23 / Typhoid Vi: 2 years.
  • Minimum Age for HPV Vaccine: 9 years (2 doses at 0, 6–12 mos if <15y; 3 doses at 0, 1, 6 mos if ≥15y or immunocompromised).
  • Cold Chain Temps: Refrigerator = 2°C to 8°C | Freezer = -50°C to -15°C.
  • Injection Site Spacing (Same Limb): Separate sites by ≥2.5 cm (1 inch).
