Intracranial Dynamics & Monro-Kellie Doctrine

Fundamental pathophysiology governing mass effect, ICP-volume curves, and cerebral perfusion.

Core Physiology

The rigid skull forms an unyielding container holding three non-compressible components: Brain Tissue (~80%), Cerebrospinal Fluid (~10%), and Blood (~10%). According to the Monro-Kellie Doctrine, because the cranial vault volume is fixed, any increase in the volume of one component must be compensated by an equal reduction in the volume of the others.

Compensatory Cascade in Expanding Intracranial Mass
Expanding Tumor Mass
➔
1. CSF Displacement into Spinal Axis
➔
2. Venous Blood Compression
➔
3. Decompensation (~220 mmHg Tipping Point)

Cerebral Perfusion Pressure (CPP)

The brain's oxygenation relies directly on maintaining adequate CPP. Calculated as:

CPP = MAP - ICP

where Mean Arterial Pressure $\text{MAP} = \frac{\text{SBP} + 2(\text{DBP})}{3}$.

  • Target Normal CPP: 70 to 120 mmHg.
  • CPP < 70 mmHg: Induces severe ischemic/hypoxic neuronal damage.
  • CPP > 120 mmHg: Causes "breakthrough" hyperperfusion, worsening cerebral edema.

Cushing's Triad (Terminal High ICP)

⚠️
Decompensation Indicator: Signifies impending brainstem herniation!
  1. Hypertension (with widening pulse pressure)
  2. Bradycardia
  3. Irregular/Cheyne-Stokes Respirations
⭐
Must Know Epidemiology (CBTRUS Data):
  • Adults (>20 y/o): Most common primary overall = Meningioma (~36-40%). Most common primary malignant = Glioma (Glioblastoma). However, overall most common brain tumor in adults is Metastatic (Secondary).
  • Pediatrics (0-14 y/o): CNS tumors are the #1 cause of cancer mortality and the #1 overall cancer in this age bracket. Most common malignant histologies = Gliomas and Embryonal tumors (Medulloblastoma).
  • Anatomic Predilection: 70% of adult brain tumors are Supratentorial; 70% of pediatric brain tumors are Infratentorial.

Vasogenic Brain Edema & Herniation Syndromes

Pathophysiology of BBB breakdown, edema management, and mechanical displacement syndromes.

High Yield Emergency

Vasogenic vs Cytotoxic Edema

Brain tumor edema is predominantly Vasogenic (not cytotoxic). Occurs due to breakdown of tight endothelial junctions, tumor secretion of vascular transudative factors, active vesicular transport across endothelial cells, and tumor proteases disrupting the BBB. Characteristically confined to the cerebral white matter (appears hyperintense on T2/FLAIR MRI).

Medical Management of Raised ICP & Edema:
  • Corticosteroids: Dexamethasone 4 mg IV/PO every 6 hours (reduces BBB permeability).
  • Osmotherapy: Mannitol 0.5–1.0 g/kg BW (25% solution) over 2–10 mins OR Hypertonic Saline (3%, 7%, or 23%).
  • ER Acute Control: Controlled hyperventilation (induces cerebral vasoconstriction).

💡
Clinical Pearl — Histology Buzzword Key:
  • "Fried Egg" cells + Chicken-wire vessels: Oligodendroglioma
  • Pseudopalisading Necrosis + Microvascular Proliferation: Glioblastoma
  • Cyst with a Mural Nodule: Pilocytic Astrocytoma (Childhood Grade 1; Rosenthal fibers + eosinophilic granular bodies).

Ependymal, Embryonal & Glioneural Neoplasms

Key pediatric and young-adult brain tumors, histopathology, and molecular subgroups.

Pediatric Focus

Ependymoma

Derived from ependymal cells lining ventricles and central canal. Most common intracranial site = Floor of 4th Ventricle (children); Most common spinal site = Filum terminale (Adults, Myxopapillomatous variant).

  • Histology: True Ependymal Rosettes (lumens) and Perivascular Pseudorosettes (cells arranged around blood vessels with anuclear zones).
  • Molecular Note: RELA fusion positive in supratentorial pediatric cases.
  • Management: Maximal surgical resection + Craniospinal RT (if CSF seeding present). Chemotherapy response is poor/suboptimal.

Medulloblastoma

Highly malignant (WHO Grade 4) embryonal tumor arising from cerebellar vermis in children (ages 4-8). Propensity for CSF drop metastases along the neuraxis.

  • Histology: Small round blue cells, dense cellularity, Homer-Wright Rosettes (cells around central neuropil).
  • Associated Syndromes: Gorlin (PTCH1), Turcot (mismatch repair), Li-Fraumeni (TP53), Rubinstein-Taybi, Fanconi Anemia.

Glioneural

Lhermitte-Duclos Disease

Dysplastic Gangliocytoma of Cerebellum. Benign, slow-growing mass of disorganized granule and Purkinje cells.
MRI Hallmark: Pathognomonic "Tiger Stripe" appearance in cerebellum. Associated with Cowden syndrome (PTEN).

Glioneural

DNET

Dysembryoplastic Neuroepithelial Tumor. Cortical lesion in temporal lobe causing intractable childhood seizures.
MRI Hallmark: Cluster of small cysts ("soap-bubble" appearance). Surgical resection is curative.

Astrocytic

SEGA

Subependymal Giant Cell Astrocytoma. Arises at the Foramen of Monro in up to 20% of Tuberous Sclerosis patients.
Treatment: Surgical excision or targeted Oral Everolimus (mTOR inhibitor).

Meningioma & Vestibular Schwannoma

Extra-axial neoplasms of the meninges and cranial nerves.

Extra-Axial Dominance

Meningioma Overview

Most common primary non-glial intracranial tumor in adults (36-40%). Peak incidence 6th–7th decades; Female predominance (2:1 to 3:1). Arises from Arachnoid Cap Cells. Strongly associated with NF2 gene (merlin) mutations on Chromosome 22q.

  • Histopathology: Concentric cellular whorls, Psammoma bodies (laminated calcific concretions), and "Orphan Annie eyes" nuclei (central clearing).
  • Neuroimaging: Extra-axial, dural-based mass with "Dural Tail" sign, intense homogeneous contrast enhancement, hyperostosis of adjacent skull.
🧠
WHO 2021 Meningioma Grading:
  • Grade 1 (80.8%): Meningothelial, fibroblastic, transitional, psammomatous.
  • Grade 2 (18.3%): Atypical, clear cell, choroid. Brain invasion OR 4–19 mitoses/10 HPF.
  • Grade 3 (1.6%): Anaplastic/Malignant, papillary, rhabdoid. $\ge20$ mitoses/10 HPF OR TERT promoter mutation or CDKN2A/B homozygous deletion.

Vestibular Schwannoma (Acoustic Neuroma)

Benign nerve sheath tumor arising from the vestibular division of CN VIII in the Cerebellopontine Angle (CPA).

  • Presentation: Asymmetric sensorineural hearing loss, progressive unilateral tinnitus, unsteady gait. Large masses compress CN V (facial numbness) and CN VII (facial weakness).
  • Bilateral Acoustic Neuromas: Pathognomonic diagnostic criterion for Neurofibromatosis Type 2 (NF2) in patients <21 y/o.

Biphasic Histology Patterns

1. Antoni A Pattern: Hypercellular, compact spindle cells arranged in fascicles with nuclear palisading surrounding acellular eosinophilic zones known as Verocay Bodies.

2. Antoni B Pattern: Hypocellular, loose myxoid stroma with stellate cells and sparse reticulin fibers.

Sellar, Ventricular & Primary CNS Lymphoma

Endocrine-active tumors, acute ventricular cysts, and primary hematologic malignancies.

Deep Midline Tumors

Pituitary Adenoma & Apoplexy

Classified as Microadenomas (<10 mm) or Macroadenomas (>10 mm). Non-functional tumors present with mass effect (compression of optic chiasm $\rightarrow$ Bitemporal Hemianopia). Functional tumors secrete excess hormones:

  • Prolactinoma (60-70%): Amenorrhea, galactorrhea, gynecomastia, hypogonadism. First-line rx: Medical (Bromocriptine / Cabergoline).
  • GH-Secreting (10-15%): Acromegaly / Gigantism. Rx: Octreotide / Surgery.
  • ACTH-Secreting: Cushing's Disease.
🚨
PITUITARY APOPLEXY EMERGENCY: Acute hemorrhage/infarction into an adenoma. Presents with sudden severe retro-orbital headache, rapid bilateral visual loss, ophthalmoplegia, and collapse.
STAT Management: High-dose Hydrocortisone (treat acute Addisonian crisis!) followed by urgent transsphenoidal sellar decompression.

Craniopharyngioma

Benign suprasellar mass derived from remnants of Rathke's Pouch. Bimodal age peak (children ~10 y/o; adults 50-60 y/o). Compresses optic chiasm from above/behind $\rightarrow$ Bitemporal Lower Quadrantanopia.

Feature Adamantinomatous Papillary
Demographics Children (Predominant) Adults Almost Exclusively
Histology Stellate reticulum, "Wet Keratin" (ghost cells), peripheral palisading. Well-differentiated non-keratinizing squamous pseudopapillae.
Cyst Fluid Viscous, cholesterol-rich "Motor Oil" fluid; Rim calcifications. Viscous fluid; Calcification and wet keratin are ABSENT.
Genetics CTNNB1 mutations BRAF V600E mutations (responds to BRAF inhibitors).

Colloid Cyst of 3rd Ventricle

Benign cyst derived from ependymal cells of the paraphysis located at the Foramen of Monro.

⭐
Classic Presentation: Intermittent severe positional headaches modified by head posture ("ball-valve" mechanism) and sudden "Drop Attacks" without loss of consciousness. Can cause sudden fatal acute obstructive hydrocephalus.

Primary CNS Lymphoma (PCNSL)

Malignant Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma (DLBCL). Strongly associated with EBV and immunosuppression (HIV/AIDS, transplants), though incidence in immunocompetent elderly is rising. Deep periventricular, perivascular cuffing histology.

⚠️
HIGH-YIELD EXAMINATION PITFALL: DO NOT give Corticosteroids (Dexamethasone) before diagnostic stereotactic biopsy! Corticosteroids induce rapid tumor lysis ("ghost tumor"), causing transient disappearance on imaging and leading to false-negative biopsy and delayed care.

Brain Metastases, Leptomeningeal & Paraneoplastic Syndromes

Secondary CNS involvement, autoantibody profiles, and spinal axis tumors.

Secondary & Systemic

Brain Metastases

Most common overall intracranial tumor in adults. Typically settle hematogenously at the Gray-White Matter Junction due to abrupt blood vessel caliber change. Well-circumscribed, intense ring-enhancement with disproportionate surrounding vasogenic edema.

  • Most Common Primary Sources: Non-Small Cell Lung Ca ($>50\%$) > Breast Ca > Melanoma > Renal Cell Ca > GI.
  • Highest Propensity to Met to Brain: Malignant Melanoma ($75\%$).
  • Most Prone to Hemorrhage: Melanoma, Choriocarcinoma, Renal Cell Ca, Thyroid, Lung Adenocarcinoma.

Leptomeningeal Carcinomatosis

Widespread tumor seeding across meninges and ventricles ("sugar coating"). Presents with multi-level neurological deficits: headache, back pain, polyradiculopathies, and multiple cranial neuropathies.

Diagnosis: Gadolinium MRI shows nodular basilar leptomeningeal enhancement. Confirmed by CSF Cytology / Flow Cytometry.

Spinal Cord Tumor Classification

80% of Spine Tumors

Extradural Tumors

Located outside the dura. Almost exclusively Metastatic Carcinoma (prostate, breast, lung, renal, myeloma) via Batson's venous plexus. Causes bony destruction and spinal cord compression.

15% of Spine Tumors

Intradural-Extramedullary (IDEM)

Inside dura, outside cord parenchyma. Most common = Schwannoma and Meningioma. Compresses cord from periphery.

5% of Spine Tumors

Intramedullary (IMSCT)

Inside spinal cord parenchyma. Overall most common in adults = Ependymoma (esp filum terminale); Most common in children = Astrocytoma.

10-Minute Last Minute Board Exam Cram Sheet

Ultra-condensed rapid review: high-yield buzzwords, "If you see X $\rightarrow$ Think Y", and core facts.

PLE Ultra-Rapid

If You See X $\rightarrow$ Think Y Lines

  • Fried egg cells + chicken-wire vessels: Oligodendroglioma (check IDH & 1p/19q co-deletion).
  • Pseudopalisading necrosis + vascular proliferation: Glioblastoma (GBM).
  • Psammoma bodies + whorls + dural tail: Meningioma.
  • Rosenthal fibers + eosinophilic granular bodies + cyst with mural nodule: Pilocytic Astrocytoma.
  • Verocay bodies + Antoni A/B patterns: Vestibular Schwannoma.
  • Homer-Wright rosettes in pediatric vermis: Medulloblastoma.
  • True rosettes + perivascular pseudorosettes in 4th ventricle: Ependymoma.
  • "Tiger stripe" MRI pattern in cerebellum: Lhermitte-Duclos disease.
  • Soap-bubble cortical temporal lobe cysts + childhood seizures: DNET.
  • Subependymal nodule at Foramen of Monro + Tuberous Sclerosis: SEGA.
  • Positional "ball-valve" headaches + drop attacks: Colloid Cyst of 3rd Ventricle.
  • Bitemporal lower quadrantanopia + wet keratin + motor oil fluid: Adamantinomatous Craniopharyngioma.
  • DO NOT give steroids prior to biopsy: Primary CNS Lymphoma.
  • Multiple ring-enhancing lesions at gray-white junction: Brain Metastases.
  • Opsoclonus-Myoclonus-Ataxia ("dancing eyes, dancing feet"): Anti-Ri antibody (Breast/SCLC).

Critical Numerical Values & Formulas

  • CPP Formula: $\text{CPP} = \text{MAP} - \text{ICP}$. Normal range = 70–120 mmHg.
  • Monro-Kellie Tipping Point: Sudden ICP spike occurs at ~220 mmHg volume equivalent decompensation.
  • Dexamethasone Edema Dose: 4 mg IV/PO every 6 hours.
  • Mannitol Dosing: 0.5–1.0 g/kg BW (25% solution) over 2–10 mins.
  • Meningioma Prevalence: #1 primary adult brain tumor (~36–40%). Grade 1 = 80.8%, Grade 2 = 18.3%, Grade 3 = 1.6%.
  • GBM Median Survival: 14.6 months with Stupp Protocol (Surgery + RT + Temozolomide).
  • Oligodendroglioma Survival: 13.8 years median survival with PCV + RT.

Diagnostic Buzzword Cheat Sheet

  • Kernohan's Notch: False-localizing sign in uncal herniation causing ipsilateral hemiplegia via compression of contralateral cerebral peduncle.
  • AMEN Histology Criteria (GBM): Atypia, Mitoses, Endovascular proliferation, Necrosis.
  • Orphan Annie Eye Nuclei: Central nuclear clearing seen in Meningioma.
  • Motor Oil Cyst Fluid: Viscous cholesterol-rich fluid in Adamantinomatous Craniopharyngioma.
  • Batson's Venous Plexus: Valveless venous system mediating spinal extradural metastases from prostate/breast.
  • BRAF V600E Mutation: Targetable mutation in Papillary Craniopharyngioma and Pleomorphic Xanthoastrocytoma.
  • RELA Fusion: Supratentorial pediatric Ependymoma driver.
