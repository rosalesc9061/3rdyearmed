📖 High-Yield Executive Overview

Welcome to the interactive board-exam reviewer for Connective Tissue Disorders & Vasculitis Syndrome. This module synthesizes core rheumatologic concepts from internal medicine lectures and board guidelines into interactive study cards, diagnostic flowcharts, side-by-side comparison matrices, active-recall flashcards, and board-style self-assessment questions.

🎯 Primary Learning Objectives

  • Recognize multisystem autoimmune connective tissue diseases (SLE, APS, SSc, IM, Sjögren's) based on clinical presentations and lab markers.
  • Differentiate systemic vasculitides according to predominantly affected vessel size (Large, Medium, Small) and clinicopathologic patterns.
  • Interpret complex autoantibody panels (ANA patterns, anti-dsDNA, anti-Sm, anti-Scl-70, anti-centromere, ANCA, anti-Ro/SSA, anti-La/SSB).
  • Formulate evidence-based diagnostic algorithms, renal biopsy interpretations, and risk-adapted therapeutic plans.
  • Avoid critical diagnostic traps (e.g., steroid-induced scleroderma renal crisis, mistaking steroid myopathy for myositis relapse).

🔑 Core Autoimmune Terminology

Autoimmune Triad: Pathogenic convergence of genetic susceptibility, environmental/microenvironmental triggers, and defective immune tolerance leading to autoantibody deposition and end-organ damage.

Leukocytoclastic Vasculitis (LCV): Histopathologic neutrophil fragmentation (nuclear dust) within small blood vessel walls, typical of immune complex vasculitides.

Panniculitis: Inflammation of subcutaneous fat lobules or septa (e.g., in chronic cutaneous lupus).

🦋 Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)

Core Topic 1

Definition: SLE is a prototypical multisystem autoimmune rheumatologic disease characterized by autoantibody production, complement activation, and immune complex deposition leading to widespread systemic inflammation and organ destruction.

Epidemiology: Predominantly affects females of childbearing age (F:M ratio of 9:1). Disease course is relapsing-remitting or continuously active. Early mortality is driven by active disease, infection, and thrombosis; late mortality is heavily driven by atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and organ failure.

Triggers Detail: Ultraviolet exposure increases skin cell apoptosis and alters DNA structure, rendering it immunogenic. Tobacco smoking increases risk (HR 1.5). Environmental silica exposure and drugs (hydralazine, procainamide, isoniazid) also trigger autoantibodies.

🩺 Organ System Clinical Manifestations

1. Musculoskeletal & Cutaneous System ▼

Musculoskeletal: Polyarthritis in hands, wrists, and knees. Soft tissue swelling/tenderness without bone erosion (distinguishes it from Rheumatoid Arthritis). Hand deformities (Jaccoud's arthropathy) occur in 10% and are reducible.

Cutaneous:

  • Malar / "Butterfly" Rash: Erythematous, photosensitive, slightly raised rash over cheeks and nasal bridge, strictly sparing the nasolabial folds. Worsening signals systemic flare.
  • Discoid Lupus Erythematosus (DLE): Chronic dermatitis with circular hyperpigmented erythematous rims and depigmented atrophic centers destroying dermal appendages permanently. 5% of DLE have SLE; 20% of SLE have DLE.
  • Oral Ulcers: 3rd most common cutaneous sign; typically painless, located on the upper hard palate.
  • Alopecia: Non-scarring generalized hair thinning.
2. Renal Manifestation: Lupus Nephritis (LN) ▼

Nephritis occurs in up to 50% of patients and is a leading cause of early mortality alongside infection. Biopsy is indicated for unexplained creatinine elevation, confirmed proteinuria ≥ 1.0 g/24h, or proteinuria ≥ 0.5 g/24h with hematuria/cellular casts.

LN Class Histopathologic Description Clinical / Lab Picture Prognosis & Therapy
Class I Minimal Mesangial Bland sediment, proteinuria <200 mg Excellent prognosis; no specific immunosuppression.
Class II Mesangial Proliferative Mild hematuria/proteinuria (200-500 mg) Excellent prognosis; no active immunosuppression.
Class III Focal Lupus Nephritis (<50% glomeruli) Active sediment, proteinuria 500-3500 mg, high anti-dsDNA, low C3/C4 High risk for ESRD; aggressive immunosuppression required.
Class IV Diffuse Lupus Nephritis (>50% glomeruli) Most frequent & severe; heavy proteinuria, RBC casts, HTN, low C3/C4 Poor prognosis without therapy. Immunosuppressive induction (MMF / Cyclophosphamide) mandatory!
Class V Membranous Lupus Nephritis Nephrotic-range proteinuria (>3000 mg), bland sediment, normal complement Treated with immunosuppression if mixed with Class III/IV or progressive.
Class VI Advanced Sclerotic (≥90% sclerotic) Renal failure, chronic elevated creatinine Irreversible damage; NO immunosuppression recommended (prepare for dialysis/transplant).
3. Hematologic, Neurologic, Cardiopulmonary & Other Systems ▼

Hematologic: Normochromic normocytic anemia (most common). Lymphopenia (almost always present; rarely predisposing to infection alone). Thrombocytopenia (therapy generally not required unless <40,000/μL or bleeding).

Neurologic: 19 neuropsychiatric syndromes. Most common diffuse manifestation: cognitive dysfunction and severe headaches (excruciating headaches herald a flare).

Cardiopulmonary: Pericarditis is the most common cardiac feature. 2.7-fold increased risk of Heart Failure. Premature coronary atherosclerosis is a major late mortality driver.

Ocular: Sicca syndrome & conjunctivitis are common. Retinal vasculitis and optic neuritis are vision-threatening emergencies!

🧪 SLE Autoantibody Profile

Autoantibody Sensitivity Specificity Clinical & Diagnostic Utility
ANA (Antinuclear Ab) >95% Low Best screening test. Titer ≥ 1:80 is obligatory entry criterion for 2019 EULAR/ACR. IF method preferred over ELISA.
Anti-dsDNA 70% High Correlates directly with disease flares & lupus nephritis activity. Rising titers + declining C3/C4 indicate imminent renal flare.
Anti-Sm (Smith) 30% Very High Pathognomonic/specific for SLE. Does NOT fluctuate with disease activity.
Anti-Histone 70% Moderate Associated with Drug-Induced Lupus (Procainamide, Hydralazine, Isoniazid, Lithium, TNF inhibitors). Spares kidneys and CNS.
Anti-Ro / SS-A 30-40% Moderate Associated with Neonatal Lupus (congenital heart block), Sicca syndrome, and Subacute Cutaneous LE. Screening essential in pregnancy!
Antiphospholipid (aPL) 20-30% Moderate Identifies risk for venous/arterial thrombosis, recurrent fetal loss, and thrombocytopenia.

📋 SLE Classification Criteria

2012 SLICC Criteria: Sensitivity 97%, Specificity 84%. Requires ≥ 4 of 17 criteria (with ≥ 1 clinical AND ≥ 1 immunologic criteria) OR biopsy-proven nephritis compatible with SLE plus positive ANA or Anti-dsDNA.

2019 EULAR/ACR Criteria: Sensitivity 96.1%, Specificity 93.4%. Mandatory Entry Criterion: Positive ANA ≥ 1:80. Requires score ≥ 10 weighted points across 7 clinical and 3 immunologic domains. Biopsy-proven Class III or IV nephritis awards 10 points automatically!

💊 Therapeutic Strategy & Pharmacotherapy

Goal: Remission/low disease activity, organ damage prevention, drug toxicity minimization.

  • Antimalarials (Hydroxychloroquine - HCQ): Indicated for ALL SLE patients regardless of activity. Dose ≤ 5.0 mg/kg actual weight/day. Reduces flares by 50%, improves survival, protects against thrombosis and organ damage. Adverse Effect: Retinal toxicity (requires baseline, 5-year, and annual ophthalmologic screening).
  • Glucocorticoids: Mainstay for life/organ-threatening flares (Prednisone 0.5-1.0 mg/kg/day or IV Methylprednisolone pulses 500-1000 mg x 3 days). Taper to ≤ 7.5 mg/day within 6 months to avoid steroid toxicity.
  • Immunosuppressive Agents (MMF & Cyclophosphamide): First-line induction for Class III/IV Lupus Nephritis. MMF (2-3 g/d) is preferred in women wanting fertility protection. Cyclophosphamide carries risks of hemorrhagic cystitis (prevented with Mesna) and premature ovarian failure.
  • Maintenance Agents: Mycophenolate Mofetil or Azathioprine (safer long-term options).
  • Biologics: Belimumab (anti-BLyS antibody) for refractory extrarenal disease; Rituximab (anti-CD20) for severe refractory disease or severe autoimmune thrombocytopenia.
⭐
MUST KNOW: All SLE patients should receive Hydroxychloroquine unless contraindicated. Broad-spectrum SPF ≥ 30 sunscreen is mandatory for photosensitive dermatitis.
⚠️
COMMON PITFALL: NSAIDs must be used with extreme caution due to elevated risks of NSAID-induced aseptic meningitis, elevated transaminases, hypertension, and acute renal insufficiency.

🩸 Antiphospholipid Syndrome (APS)

Core Topic 2

Definition: Autoantibody-mediated acquired thrombophilia characterized by recurrent venous or arterial thrombosis and/or pregnancy morbidity.

Subtypes:

  • Primary APS: Occurs in isolation without underlying autoimmune disease.
  • Secondary APS: Occurs in association with autoimmune diseases, predominantly SLE.
  • Catastrophic APS (CAPS): Rapidly progressive, life-threatening variant with simultaneous thrombotic occlusion of ≥ 3 organ systems over days.

🧪 Autoantibodies & Laboratory Testing

APS antibodies target negatively charged phospholipids and plasma phospholipid-binding proteins:

Target Marker Assay Used Pathopathologic Mechanism & Utility
Anticardiolipin Antibodies (aCL) ELISA (IgG or IgM isotype) Recognizes β2GPI bound to cardiolipin. Titer > 40 GPL/MPL or >99th percentile.
Anti-β2 Glycoprotein 1 (anti-β2GPI) ELISA (IgG or IgM isotype) Recognizes β2GPI bound to oxidized polystyrene surface. Titers >99th percentile.
Lupus Anticoagulant (LA) Coagulation Tests: aPTT, KCT, Dilute Russell Viper Venom Test (dRVVT) Prolongs clotting time in vitro by interfering with thrombin generation, but induces thrombosis in vivo! Most predictive marker for thrombosis.

🩺 Clinical Features & Sapporo Diagnostic Criteria

Clinical Spectrum:

  • Venous Thrombosis: Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) in lower extremities is the most common presentation, frequently leading to Pulmonary Embolism (PE).
  • Arterial Thrombosis: Brain is most common site (Stroke, TIA, Multi-infarct dementia, Migraines). Libman-Sacks endocarditis (valvular thickening/vegetations). Visceral/extremity gangrene.
  • Cutaneous: Livedo reticularis (mottled purple lace-like vascular discolouration due to capillary thrombi), ischemic leg ulcers.
  • Obstetric Morbidity: Late fetal loss (≥10 weeks of normal fetus), severe preeclampsia/eclampsia requiring premature delivery before 34 weeks, or ≥3 unexplained consecutive spontaneous abortions before 10th week.
⭐
REVISED SAPPORO CRITERIA FOR DEFINITE APS: Requires at least 1 Clinical Criterion (vascular thrombosis or defined pregnancy morbidity) AND 1 Laboratory Criterion (LA, aCL, or anti-β2GPI present on 2 or more occasions at least 12 weeks apart). Tests must be within 5 years of clinical event.

💊 Management Guidelines

Clinical Scenario Recommended Management
Asymptomatic or High-risk aPL profile in SLE Prophylactic Low-Dose Aspirin (80-100 mg/day).
First Venous Thrombosis Lifelong Vitamin K Antagonist (Warfarin) with target INR 2.0 - 3.0.
Arterial Thrombosis Warfarin with high-target INR 3.0 - 4.0 OR Warfarin (INR 2.0 - 3.0) PLUS low-dose aspirin.
Catastrophic APS (CAPS) Triple Therapy: High-dose Glucocorticoids + Heparin + Plasma Exchange (PEX) or IVIG + treating underlying triggers (infections).

🧬 Systemic Sclerosis (Scleroderma / SSc)

Core Topic 3

Definition: SSc is a chronic orphan connective tissue disease characterized by a pathogenic triad: 1) Autoimmunity & Inflammation, 2) Widespread Obliterative Microvasculopathy, and 3) Progressive Interstitial & Vascular Fibrosis of skin and internal organs.

⚖️ Limited vs. Diffuse Phenotypes Comparison

Feature Limited Cutaneous SSc (lcSSc / CREST) Diffuse Cutaneous SSc (dcSSc)
Skin Distribution Distal to elbows and knees; facial involvement possible; spares trunk. Proximal to elbows/knees, involving trunk, chest, and abdomen.
Onset & Course Indolent; Raynaud's precedes skin changes by years/decades. Rapid onset & progression; Raynaud's occurs concurrently with skin edema/induration.
Internal Organs Late pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH); low risk of early renal crisis. Early severe Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD) & Scleroderma Renal Crisis (within first 4 years).
Autoantibody Anti-Centromere Antibodies (ACA) Anti-Topoisomerase I (Anti-Scl-70) & Anti-RNA Polymerase III
CREST Features Calcinosis, Raynaud's, Esophageal dysmotility, Sclerodactyly, Telangiectasia. Tendon friction rubs, carpal tunnel, severe joint contractures.

🩺 Systemic Manifestations & Radiography

Organ Systems Involved:

  • Skin: Edema → Sclerodactyly → Atrophy; "Salt-and-pepper" hyper/hypopigmentation; Calcinosis cutis; Digital ischemic ulcers & necrosis.
  • Gastrointestinal: Hypomotility (esophageal dysmotility, GERD, Barrett's), Gastric Antral Vascular Ectasia (GAVE / "watermelon stomach"), small bowel bacterial overgrowth, intestinal pseudo-obstruction.
  • Cardiopulmonary: Leading cause of mortality! ILD (frequent in dcSSc) and PAH (frequent in lcSSc). Diastolic dysfunction and pericarditis.
  • Scleroderma Renal Crisis (SRC): Occurs in 15% of dcSSc patients. Manifests as malignant accelerated hypertension, rapidly progressive oliguric renal failure, and microangiopathic hemolytic anemia. Risk linked to Anti-RNA Polymerase III.

Radiographic Hallmark: Acro-osteolysis — Resorption and dissolution of distal terminal phalanges of fingers due to long-standing ischemic injury.

⚠️ Diagnostic Criteria & Steroid Warning

2013 ACR/EULAR Classification Criteria: Total score ≥ 9 fulfills definite SSc (90% sensitivity & specificity). Note: Bilateral skin thickening of fingers extending proximal to MCP joints awards 9 points automatically! Other scoring items: Sclerodactyly (2), Digital tip ulcers (2), Pitting scars (3), Telangiectasia (2), Abnormal nailfold capillaries (2), PAH/ILD (2), Raynaud's (3), SSc autoantibodies (3).

⚠️
CRITICAL BOARD PITFALL: Glucocorticoids are NOT first-line in Scleroderma! Moderate-to-high dose corticosteroids (>15 mg/day prednisone) trigger or precipitate Scleroderma Renal Crisis. If necessary, use lowest dose for brief periods only. Management of SRC is ACE Inhibitors (Captopril).

💪 Idiopathic Inflammatory Myopathies (IIM)

Core Topic 4

A heterogenous group of autoimmune muscle disorders characterized by muscle weakness, elevated serum muscle enzymes, abnormal electromyography (EMG), and inflammatory cell infiltration on muscle biopsy.

📊 Inflammatory Myopathy Subtypes Comparison

Disorder Weakness Pattern Skin Rash Muscle Biopsy & Cellular Infiltrate Malignancy Risk
Dermatomyositis (DM) Symmetric, Proximal > Distal Yes (Heliotrope, Gottron's, V/Shawl sign) Perifascicular atrophy; Perivascular/perimysial CD4+ T cells, B cells, plasmacytoid dendritic cells High (15% in adult-onset within 2-3 years)
Polymyositis (PM) Symmetric, Proximal > Distal No Endomysial infiltration; CD8+ T cells & macrophages surrounding MHC-1 expressing myofibers Moderate (lower than DM)
Immune-Mediated Necrotizing Myopathy (IMNM) Severe Proximal Weakness No Necrotic muscle fibers with sparse/minimal inflammatory infiltrate; Anti-SRP or Anti-HMGCR (statin-triggered) Variable
Inclusion Body Myositis (IBM) Asymmetric; Distal & Proximal (wrist/finger flexors, quadriceps) No Rimmed vacuoles, amyloid/p62/TDP-43 deposits, 15-18 nm tubulofilaments; poorly responsive to therapy Not associated; males > 50yo

🎨 Pathognomonic Cutaneous Signs of Dermatomyositis

  • Heliotrope Rash: Violaceous/erythematous eruption on upper eyelids, often accompanied by periorbital edema.
  • Gottron Papules: Pathognomonic raised erythematous scaly papules over extensor surfaces of MCP and IP joints.
  • Gottron Sign: Macular erythematous rash over elbows, knees, or ankles.
  • V-Sign: Erythematous rash over sun-exposed anterior neck and upper chest.
  • Shawl Sign: Erythematous rash over upper back, posterior neck, and shoulders.
  • Nailbed Changes: Periungual telangiectasias with capillary loop dilation and cuticular hypertrophy.

💊 Management & Differential Diagnosis Traps

First-Line Therapy: High-dose Glucocorticoids (Prednisone 0.75-1.0 mg/kg/day). Maintained until strength normalizes or plateaus (3-6 months).

Second-Line / Steroid-Sparing Agents: Methotrexate, Azathioprine, MMF, Rituximab (for severe weakness, cardiac/ILD involvement, or steroid toxicity).

Clinical Feature Steroid Myopathy Myositis Relapse
Timing of Weakness Develops while on HIGH-dose prednisone. Develops during prednisone TAPER or reduction.
Serum Creatine Kinase (CK) NORMAL ELEVATED / Increasing
Electromyography (EMG) Normal (absence of membrane irritability). Abnormal spontaneous activity (fibrillations, sharp waves).
Cushingoid Features Present (moon facies, ecchymoses). Variable.

👁️ Sjögren's Syndrome

Core Topic 5

Definition: Chronic autoimmune disease characterized by progressive lymphocytic infiltration of exocrine glands (lacrimal and salivary), leading to glandular destruction and dryness.

Epidemiology: Predominantly middle-aged females (F:M ratio 10:1 to 20:1). May be Primary or Secondary (associated with RA, SLE, SSc).

🩺 Clinical Hallmarks & Diagnostic Algorithm

Classic Triad: 1) Keratoconjunctivitis Sicca (dry eyes), 2) Xerostomia (dry mouth), 3) Major salivary (parotid) gland enlargement.

Screening History Questions (Must answer YES to ≥ 1):

  • Persistent dry eyes > 3 months?
  • Recurrent sensation of sand or gravel in eyes?
  • Use tear substitutes > 3 times daily?
  • Daily feeling of dry mouth > 3 months?
  • Frequently drink liquids to aid swallowing dry food?
Diagnostic Modality Objective Positive Threshold
Schirmer Test ≤ 5 mm wetting in 5 minutes (measures tear production).
Unstimulated Salivary Flow ≤ 1.5 mL in 15 minutes.
Ocular Staining Score ≥ 5 using lissamine green / fluorescein.
Minor Salivary Gland Biopsy Focal lymphocytic sialadenitis with Focus Score ≥ 1 (≥50 lymphocytes per 4 mm²).
Serology Anti-Ro/SS-A antibodies (most important serologic marker).

⚠️ Extraglandular Manifestations & Malignancy Risk

Extraglandular Features: Non-erosive polyarthritis (Jaccoud's-like), Raynaud's phenomenon (37%), Lymphocytic Interstitial Pneumonitis (LIP), Renal Tubular Acidosis (Type 1 distal RTA), Small vessel vasculitis (palpable purpura), Peripheral neuropathy.

⚠️
HIGH-YIELD MALIGNANCY RISK: Sjögren's patients have a 44-fold increased risk (approx 6% incidence) of developing Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma, specifically MALT Lymphoma (Mucosa-Associated Lymphoid Tissue) in persistent swollen parotid glands!

💊 Management Workflow

  • Dry Eyes: Avoid dry environments, artificial tear supplements, ophthalmic Cyclosporine drops, punctal occlusion.
  • Dry Mouth: Muscarinic receptor agonists (Pilocarpine, Cevimeline) to stimulate salivary flow; topical fluorides; oral sugarless gums.
  • Extraglandular Disease: NSAIDs/Hydroxychloroquine for mild arthralgias; Glucocorticoids & Immunosuppressives (MTX, AZA, MMF, Rituximab) for severe vasculitis, nephritis, or pulmonary involvement.

🫀 Systemic Vasculitis Syndrome

Core Topic 6

Definition: Heterogeneous clinicopathologic disorders characterized by inflammation and necrosis of blood vessel walls, leading to luminal stenosis, ischemia, thrombosis, or aneurysm formation.

📐 Vessel Size Categorization Framework

Category Predominant Vessels Involved Typical Clinical Features Representative Disorders
Large Vessel Vasculitis Aorta and its major primary branches Limb claudication, absent/decreased pulses, asymmetric arm BP (>10 mmHg), bruits, aortic dilation Takayasu Arteritis, Giant Cell Arteritis (GCA)
Medium Vessel Vasculitis Main visceral/renal arteries and branches Cutaneous ulcers, subcutaneous nodules, livedo reticularis, digital gangrene, mononeuritis multiplex, microaneurysms Polyarteritis Nodosa (PAN), Kawasaki Disease
Small Vessel Vasculitis Intraparenchymal arterioles, capillaries, venules Palpable purpura, vesicular lesions, glomerulonephritis, alveolar hemorrhage, scleritis/episcleritis Immune Complex: IgA Vasculitis (HSP), Cryoglobulinemic
ANCA-Associated: MPA, GPA, EGPA

🩺 Core Vasculitic Entities

1. Takayasu Arteritis ("Pulseless Disease") ▼

Demographics: Young Asian females (<40 years old, median 25yo).

Features: Granulomatous inflammation of aorta and branches. Limb claudication, weak/absent brachial pulses, asymmetric blood pressure (>10 mmHg diff), subclavian/aortic bruits, renovascular HTN.

Diagnosis: Arteriography showing stenosis, vessel wall irregularity, post-stenotic dilatation, aneurysms, and collateral flow.

2. Giant Cell Arteritis (GCA / Temporal Arteritis) ▼

Demographics: Adults > 50 years old (females > males). Most common systemic vasculitis in adults.

Features: Affects extracranial branches of carotid (temporal artery). Tender, indurated, nodular temporal artery, new localized headache, jaw claudication. Strongly linked to Polymyalgia Rheumatica (PMR) (neck/shoulder/hip stiffness).

Most Feared Complication: Irreversible blindness due to Anterior Ischemic Optic Neuropathy (AION).

Diagnosis & Tx: ESR ≥ 50 mm/hr, Temporal artery biopsy (granulomatous with giant cells). Start Immediate High-Dose Steroids before waiting for biopsy results if clinically suspected!

3. Polyarteritis Nodosa (PAN) ▼

Demographics: Adults 40-60yo; strongly associated with Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) infection.

Features: Necrotizing vasculitis of medium muscular arteries. Livedo reticularis, digital gangrene, testicular tenderness/pain, mononeuritis multiplex, hypertension. NO Glomerulonephritis! (spares capillaries).

Diagnosis: Biopsy showing transmural fibrinoid necrosis with PMN infiltration; Arteriography showing renal/visceral microaneurysms.

4. IgA Vasculitis (Henoch-Schönlein Purpura / HSP) ▼

Demographics: Most common vasculitis in children (<5 years old).

Trigger & Classic Tetrad: Occurs 1-3 weeks following an Upper Respiratory Tract Infection (URTI).

  1. Palpable Purpura: Non-blanching raised rash over buttocks and lower extremities.
  2. Arthralgias / Arthritis: Transient knees and ankles swelling.
  3. Abdominal Pain / Bowel Angina: Colicky abdominal pain, GI bleeding, risk of intussusception.
  4. Renal Involvement: Hematuria, proteinuria (IgA nephropathy pattern).

Histology: Leukocytoclastic vasculitis with prominent IgA and C3 deposition on immunofluorescence.

⚠️ Conditions Mimicking Vasculitis

Category Mimicking Condition
Infections Infective Endocarditis, Syphilis, RMSF, Lyme Disease, Disseminated Gonococcal.
Coagulopathies / Emboli Antiphospholipid Syndrome, TTP, Atheroembolic Disease, Atrial Myxoma.
Drug Toxicity Cocaine, Levamisole, Amphetamines, Ergot alkaloids.

Clinical Presentation / Trigger (If You See X...) Diagnosis / Association (...Think Y) Pathopathologic / Clinical Mechanism
Young female with malar rash sparing nasolabial folds + non-erosive arthritis Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE) Immune complex-mediated type III hypersensitivity.
High anti-dsDNA + declining serum complement C3/C4 levels Active SLE Flare / Lupus Nephritis Complement consumption via glomerular immune deposition.
Lupus symptoms + positive Anti-Histone antibodies Drug-Induced Lupus (DIL) Triggered by Hydralazine, Procainamide, or Isoniazid; spares kidneys/CNS.
Recurrent deep vein thrombosis or fetal loss + prolonged aPTT Antiphospholipid Syndrome (APS) In vitro prolonged aPTT paradoxically causes in vivo arterial/venous thrombosis.
Mottled purplish lace-like skin discoloration on legs Livedo Reticularis (APS / PAN) Venule swelling due to capillary microthrombi occlusion.
Rapidly progressive thrombosis in ≥ 3 organ systems simultaneously Catastrophic APS (CAPS) Requires urgent triple therapy (Steroids + Heparin + Plasma Exchange/IVIG).
Bilateral skin thickening proximal to MCP joints Diffuse Cutaneous Scleroderma (dcSSc) Fulfills 2013 ACR criteria automatically (score = 9). Risk for early severe ILD.
Scleroderma patient given high-dose prednisone presenting with severe HTN Scleroderma Renal Crisis (SRC) Steroid-induced hyperreninemic renal crisis; treat immediately with ACE Inhibitors.
Distal digital tip resorption on hand radiographs Acro-osteolysis Terminal phalanx bone resorption secondary to chronic Raynaud's ischemia.
Violaceous eyelid edema (Heliotrope) + knuckles scaly papules (Gottron's) Dermatomyositis (DM) Perifascicular atrophy; perimysial CD4+ / plasmacytoid dendritic cell infiltration.
Proximal muscle weakness developing while on high-dose steroids with NORMAL CK Steroid Myopathy Distinguish from myositis relapse (which shows elevated CK & EMG irritability).
Older male (>50) with wrist/finger flexor weakness & rimmed vacuoles on biopsy Inclusion Body Myositis (IBM) Poorly responsive to immunosuppressive therapy.
Dry eyes, dry mouth, parotid enlargement + positive Anti-Ro/SSA Sjögren's Syndrome Lymphocytic exocrine gland destruction; 44x increased risk for MALT Lymphoma.
Young Asian female with asymmetric arm BP (>10 mmHg diff) and bruits Takayasu Arteritis ("Pulseless Disease") Granulomatous large-vessel arteritis of aorta and primary branches.
Adult >50yo with new headache, jaw claudication, tender temporal artery Giant Cell Arteritis (GCA) Start high-dose steroids IMMEDIATELY to prevent irreversible blindness (AION).
Medium-vessel necrotizing vasculitis + Hepatitis B + livedo + testicular pain Polyarteritis Nodosa (PAN) Visceral microaneurysms on arteriography; NO glomerulonephritis.
Child with post-URTI palpable purpura on buttocks, abdominal pain, & hematuria IgA Vasculitis (Henoch-Schönlein Purpura) Small vessel leukocytoclastic vasculitis with IgA + C3 immune deposition.
