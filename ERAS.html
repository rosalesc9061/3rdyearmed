1. Overview: Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS)

⭐ Core Concept

Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) is also known as "Fast Track Surgery" or "Outpatient Day Surgery". It involves evidence-based interventions spanning three phases of surgery (Preoperative, Intraoperative, and Postoperative) to optimize patient outcomes.

Goals of ERAS

  • Surgical efficacy: Reduce physiological stress during the operation.
  • Metabolism: Early return to anabolic homeostasis (balanced state).
  • Economics: Shorter hospital stay leading to fewer expenses.
  • Recovery: Prompt return to normal daily activity.

Surgical Metabolism

⚠️ Pathophysiology

Following surgical injury or stress, the body enters a hypermetabolic state characterized by:

  • Increased total body expenditure of macromolecules.
  • Urinary nitrogen washing.
  • Collective alteration in the metabolism of amino acids (protein), carbohydrates, and fats.

2. ERAS of Cancer Patients & Cachexia

⭐ Definition

Cancer Cachexia: A complex metabolic syndrome characterized by muscle and fat wasting, commonly seen in advanced cancer. It results in a severe loss of Lean Body Mass (LBM) which is intrinsically tied to immunity, recovery, and wound healing.

3. Pre-admission Period

Health Condition Optimization

  • Diabetes Mellitus: Maintain blood sugar at 140-180 mg/dL (7.8-11 mmol/L).
    ⚠️ Uncontrolled DM = Gastroparesis
  • Hypertension & CV: Control BP, optimize cardiovascular function.
  • Respiratory (Asthma, COPD, TB): Improve respiratory function (crucial for anesthesia application).
  • Renal disease: Optimize function, remove waste products.

Social and Behavioral Factors

Tobacco/Smoking/Vaping Leads to hypersecretion (aspiration risk), prolonged intubation, poor wound healing.
Intervention: Cease smoking 4 to 8 weeks prior.
Alcohol dependency May decrease immunity and ↑ post-op infections.
Intervention: Limit to max 2 units (e.g., ≤150mL of 13% wine, ≤500mL of 4% beer).
Illicit drug use Strongly discouraged.

Nutritional Status

Most underweight surgical patients have GI problems (large/small bowel, esophagus, stomach). Malnutrition leads to worse outcomes, cardiac cachexia, and higher infection rates.

  • Mild to Moderate Malnutrition: Operation may proceed immediately.
  • Severe Malnutrition: 💡 Management: Build up nutrition for at least 1 week if surgery can be delayed. Monitor intake to avoid Refeeding Syndrome (electrolyte/acid-base shifts).

Anemia Management

Blood is essential for nutrient, O2, and IV med delivery.

  • WHO Criteria: Men Hb < 130 g/L; Women Hb < 120 g/L (Note: Trans says mg/L but standard is g/L or g/dL; faithfully transcribing exact text: Men Hb < 130 mg/L, Women Hb < 120 mg/L - likely a typo in source for g/L).
  • Diagnostics: Serum ferritin and transferrin saturation.
    Without inflammation: < 30 µg/L. With inflammation: < 100 µg/L.
  • Management:
    • Oral Fe: 40-60 mg daily for a week (often tolerated poorly).
    • Standard Treatment: IV ferric carboxymaltose at 15 mg/kg (max 1000 mg) over 15 mins for a week.
    • ⚠️ Avoid Blood Transfusion/EPO: Has LOW recommendation due to complication risks (reactions, blood-borne diseases). Transfuse only if Hb ≤ 7 g/dL.
    • Autotransfusion must be done at least 1 week prior.

4. Pre- and Intra-operative Period

Premedication & Antibiotics

  • Premedication: Keep minimal to zero. Avoid long-acting agents.
    Avoid Benzodiazepines (prolonged sedation). Avoid excess Opioids (PONV, ileus, resp depression).
  • Bowel Prep: Low recommendation (leads to dehydration/electrolyte imbalance).
  • Antibiotics: Administer 30 to 60 minutes before skin incision for max tissue concentration.
    💡 Redosing: Give an additional dose if the operating time exceeds 4 hours.

Thromboprophylaxis & Anesthesia

  • Thrombus Prevention: Use LMW Heparin. Should NOT exceed 28 days if ambulatory. Use graduated compression stockings (arch support, gentle compression).
  • Anesthesia: Favor short-acting agents (e.g., Propofol) for easy reversal and early ambulation.
    ⚠️ Avoid: Long-acting anesthesia and long-acting neuromuscular blocking agents (NMBAs).

Fluid Management & Overload (Fig 10)

Goal: Restrictive fluid therapy with near zero balance input/output. Correct fluid deficit pre-op. Usual post-op deficit is ±500 mL.

Pathophysiology of Fluid Overload:
Salt/water overload ➔ ↑ Intra-abdominal pressure ➔ ↓ Mesentery blood flow AND Intestinal Edema ➔ Ileus & poor wound healing (↓ OH-proline) ➔ Dehiscence.

⭐ Rule: Daily accumulated fluid balance should NOT go beyond 75% of interstitial fluid volume (or 12% of patient's weight).

Fasting & Carbohydrate Loading

⭐ NO SOLID FOOD for at least 6 hours.
💡 Clear Liquids: Can be given up to 2 hours prior to OR (prevents dehydration/hypoglycemia).

CHO Loading Benefits: Shifts fasting to fed state, ↓ post-op insulin resistance, ↓ post-op weight loss, preserves LBM.
⚠️ Caution: Low recommendation for diabetic patients (risk of gastroparesis / poor emptying).

Temperature, Glycemic Control, & Surgery Type

  • Temperature: Prevent hypothermia (shivering ↑ muscle activity, causes coagulopathy and SSI).
    ⚠️ Triad of Death: Hypothermia (<35°C), Acidosis (pH <7.1), Coagulopathy (INR >1.5).
  • Glycemic Control: Maintain 140-180 mg/dL.
    Acute hypo = fast emptying. Chronic hyper + ox stress = fast emptying.
    Chronic hyper + inflamm stress = slow emptying. Uncontrolled = delayed motility / PONV.
  • Surgery Type: Laparoscopic favored (smaller incision, ↓ pro-inflammatory cytokines, better pulmonary care).
    If open surgery: use Transversus Abdominis Plane (TAP) Block. Avoid epidural (causes postural hypotension/delays ambulation).

5. Post-operative Period

Pain & Fluid Management

  • Pain Management: Multimodal approach. Paracetamol 1g QID + NSAID (Ketorolac 15-30mg OD) OR COX2 Inhibitor (200mg BID). COX2 is preferred as it doesn't affect platelet aggregation. Reduces stress, allows early ambulation.
  • Fluid Management: Balanced salt solution (Lactated Ringer's) at 50 mL/hour. Boluses of 100-150 mL if needed. Monitor via Urine Output (target: 0.5-1 cc/kg/hr). Restrictive intraoperative hydration significantly lowers complication rates.

Postoperative Nausea and Vomiting (PONV)

Prophylaxis: Dexamethasone 8-10 mg IV (start of OR, promotes appetite) + 5-HT3 antagonist (Ondansetron 4 mg IV at end of OR). Avoid Dopamine inhibitors (delays motility).

Risk Factor CategorySpecific Factors (Traditional Approach)
PatientFemale gender, Non-smoking status, Hx of PONV, Hx of motion sickness
AnestheticVolatile agents, Nitrous oxide, Intraop opioids, Neostigmine (>2.5mg)
SurgicalLengthy surgical procedures (MOST IMPORTANT), Intra-abdominal, Gynecological, Laparoscopic, Breast, Neuro, ENT

Infective Complications & Tubes

  • Predictor of Infection: C-reactive Protein (CRP). A Day 3 CRP > 150 mg/L or persistent elevation predicts infective complication in colorectal ops.
  • Drainage & NGT: Avoid routine use. Remove ASAP.
    Peritoneal drainage does NOT prevent intestinal leak. NGT/foley restricts breathing, delays oral intake, risks pneumonia/UTI.
⭐ Early Feeding & Ambulation

Early Feeding: Done within 1st 24 hours. Prevents negative protein balance, maintains intestinal mucosal integrity/immunity, prevents bacterial translocation.

⚠️ Common Myth: Bowel sounds and passage of flatus are NOT absolute prerequisites for initiating early feeding! Adequate resuscitation (urine output) is key.

Early Ambulation: Promotes faster recovery and shorter hospital stay.

9. Cram Sheet (Last-Minute Review)

  • IF YOU SEE "Fast Track Surgery" ➔ THINK ERAS.
  • IF YOU SEE Cancer patient with muscle wasting ➔ THINK Cachexia (Tumor=PIF/LMF, Body=Cytokines/Inflammation).
  • IF YOU SEE Severe malnutrition pre-op ➔ THINK Delay surgery 1 week for nutrition build-up (watch out for refeeding syndrome).
  • IF YOU SEE Pre-op diabetic patient ➔ THINK Target BS 140-180 mg/dL. Caution with CHO loading due to Gastroparesis.
  • IF YOU SEE Smoking patient ➔ THINK Stop 4-8 weeks prior to reduce hypersecretion/aspiration.
  • IF YOU SEE Pre-op Anemia ➔ THINK IV ferric carboxymaltose (Blood transfusion is LOW recommendation unless Hb ≤ 7).
  • IF YOU SEE Prolonged surgery (>4 hrs) ➔ THINK Give additional dose of prophylactic antibiotics.
  • IF YOU SEE NPO guidelines ➔ THINK Solid food = 6 hours max fasting. Clear liquids = up to 2 hours before OR.
  • IF YOU SEE Fluid overload intra-op ➔ THINK Bowel edema ➔ Ileus ➔ Anastomotic dehiscence. Target Near-Zero Balance.
  • IF YOU SEE Triad of Death ➔ THINK Hypothermia (<35°C), Acidosis (pH <7.1), Coagulopathy (INR >1.5).
  • IF YOU SEE Open abdominal surgery pain control ➔ THINK TAP block (avoid Epidural due to postural hypotension).
  • IF YOU SEE PONV Prophylaxis ➔ THINK Dexamethasone (start of OR) + Ondansetron (end of OR). Lengthy surgery is biggest risk factor.
  • IF YOU SEE Day 3 post-op CRP > 150 mg/L ➔ THINK Infective complication (colorectal).
  • IF YOU SEE Post-op Feeding ➔ THINK Start within 24 hours. Bowel sounds/flatus are NOT required!
  • IF YOU SEE Drains/NGT ➔ THINK Remove ASAP. They do not prevent leaks; they delay recovery and cause infections.
