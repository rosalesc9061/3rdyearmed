1. OSTEOARTHRITIS (OA) — DEGENERATIVE JOINT DISEASE ▼

A. Pathophysiology & Pathology

Osteoarthritis is a mechanically-driven organ failure of the diarthrodial joint. The pathology of OA is primarily IN the hyaline cartilage (non-uniform loss of cartilage), accompanied by subchondral bone sclerosis, cyst formation, and marginal osteophyte (bone spur) formation—which represents bone's repair response to lost cartilage.

  • Cartilage Breakdown: Lysosomal enzymes, plasmin, cathepsins, and Matrix Metalloproteinases (MMPs) degrade matrix proteoglycans and collagen. Cartilage initially undergoes fibrillation and surface irregularity, followed by focal erosions.
  • Joint Protective Mechanisms Failure: Normal joints are protected by joint capsules/ligaments (limit excursion), synovial fluid (lubrication via hyaluronic acid and lubricin secreted by synovial fibroblasts), mechanoreceptors (anticipate joint loading), bridging muscles/tendons (decelerate joint impact), and cartilage compressibility. Failure of these protectors accelerates OA (e.g., Charcot's arthropathy in peripheral neuropathy; rapidly developing OA after sensory nerve sectioning or meniscectomy).
  • Load Allocation & Compartments: In the knee, the medial compartment degenerates first. Thinning of medial joint space leads to frontal plane malalignment resulting in varus deformity ("bow-leggedness"). Lateral compartment loss leads to valgus deformity ("knock-knees"). 3 to 6 times body weight is transmitted across the knee during single-leg stance.
⭐
MUST KNOW: Sources of Pain in OA
Healthy articular hyaline cartilage is Aneural and Avascular. Cartilage loss alone does NOT cause pain! Pain in OA originates from extra-cartilaginous innervated structures: synovial inflammation/synovitis, joint effusion (capsular stretch), bone marrow edema (from subchondral stress), periarticular bursitis (anserine bursitis, IT-band syndrome), and peripheral/central sensitization. Adipokines from obesity also enhance pain sensitivity.

B. Etiology & Risk Factors

  • Systemic Factors: Age (most potent risk factor overall); Female gender (more prone, earlier onset); Genetic susceptibility (GDF5 gene mutation; hand/hip heritability ~50%, knee ~30%); Race/ethnicity.
  • Local / Joint Vulnerability: History of trauma, prior meniscectomy or ligamentous tears; intrinsic muscle weakness; developmental hip abnormalities in utero/childhood (Congenital Dysplasia, Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease, Slipped Capital Femoral Epiphysis [SCFE]); Avascular Necrosis (AVN).
  • Use / Loading Factors: Obesity (strongest modifiable factor for knees/hips/hands); high-impact athletic pursuits; occupations requiring prolonged standing/heavy lifting (teachers, security guards, salespeople, farmers).

C. Clinical Manifestations & Physical Exam

  • Pain Pattern: Mechanical pain—worsened by joint use/weight-bearing (e.g., walking down stairs, standing up after sitting), relieved by rest. Advanced disease causes rest and nocturnal pain.
  • Morning Stiffness: Brief stiffness lasting <30 minutes (often <5 minutes), termed the "gel phenomenon".
  • Joint Involvement: Cervical and lower lumbar spine, Hips, Knees, 1st CMC (thumb base), 1st MTP (bunions), PIPs, and DIPs. Spares the wrist, elbow, and ankle (unless secondary to major trauma/AVN).
  • Physical Findings: Bony enlargement, quadriceps atrophy, limping ("penguin walk"), varus/valgus deformity, limited ROM, and coarse joint crepitus.
    • Heberden's Nodes: Bony enlargement of Distal Interphalangeal (DIP) joints.
    • Bouchard's Nodes: Bony enlargement of Proximal Interphalangeal (PIP) joints.
⚠️
COMMON PITFALL: Radiograph vs. Pain Severity
The severity of radiographic changes in OA (joint space narrowing, osteophytes, subchondral sclerosis) does NOT correlate well with the presence or severity of pain! Routine X-rays or MRIs are NOT indicated for uncomplicated OA presenting with classic symptoms.

2. RHEUMATOID ARTHRITIS (RA) — SYSTEMIC AUTOIMMUNE DISEASE ▼

A. Pathophysiology & Pathogenesis

Rheumatoid Arthritis is a chronic, systemic autoimmune inflammatory arthritis characterized by persistent inflammatory synovitis. Pathology resides primarily in the synovium (unlike OA which is in cartilage).

  • Synovial Pathology: Microvascular injury, B/T cell infiltration, and synovial lining cell hyperplasia form an invasive, hypervascularized, proliferative tissue called Pannus. Pannus erodes underlying articular cartilage and subchondral bone.
  • Cytokine Cascade: Driven by TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6, and IL-8. TNF-α activates synovial macrophages. B cells produce autoantibodies (RF and anti-CCP) and secrete pro-inflammatory cytokines.
  • Environment & Citrullination: Cigarette smoking is the strongest known environmental risk factor (1.5–3.5x risk). Periodontal disease caused by Porphyromonas gingivalis plays a unique role: P. gingivalis harbors peptidyl arginine deiminase (PAD) enzyme, which citrullinates arginine residues, breaking self-tolerance and inducing Anti-Citrullinated Protein Antibodies (ACPA / Anti-CCP).
💡
CLINICAL PEARL: Anti-CCP vs. Rheumatoid Factor (RF)
RF (IgM antibody against Fc portion of IgG) is positive in 75–80% of RA cases but is non-specific (positive in Sjögren's, SLE, Endocarditis, Hepatitis). Anti-CCP (ACPA) has equivalent sensitivity (~75%) but superior specificity (~95%). Positive Anti-CCP in early arthritis strongly predicts rapid disease progression, severe joint erosions, and extra-articular manifestations!

B. Clinical Features & Characteristic Deformities

  • Symmetrical Polyarthritis: Involves small peripheral joints: MCPs, PIPs, Wrists (uniform feature), and MTPs. Spares DIP joints, 1st CMC, and axial skeleton (except C1-C2).
  • Morning Stiffness: Severe, lasting >1 hour (often all morning), easing with physical movement.
  • Irreversible Chronic Deformities:
    • Boutonnière Deformity: Hyperflexion of PIP joint with hyperextension of DIP joint.
    • Swan-Neck Deformity: Hyperextension of PIP joint with hyperflexion of DIP joint.
    • Z-Deformity of Thumb: Subluxation of 1st MCP joint with hyperextension of 1st IP joint.
    • Ulnar Deviation: Subluxation of MCP joints causing fingers to pull ulnar-ward.
    • Piano-Key Sign: Dorsal subluxation of the ulnar styloid.
    • Pes Planovalgus: Flat feet secondary to midtarsal/ankle chronic inflammation.
    • Atlantoaxial (C1–C2) Subluxation: Cervical spine involvement occurring in <10% of cases. Crucial risk during endotracheal intubation for general anesthesia!

C. Systemic & Extra-Articular Manifestations

RA affects ~0.5–1% of adults (Female-to-Male ratio in PH = 9:1; onset 4th–5th decades). Overall mortality is 2x higher than general population, with Ischemic Heart Disease / Cardiovascular Disease being the #1 cause of death (2–4x increased risk of MI and stroke).

D. 2010 ACR-EULAR Classification Criteria for RA

A score of ≥6 / 10 classifies definite RA (in patients with ≥1 joint with definite clinical synovitis not explained by another disease):

  • Joint Involvement: 1 large joint (0); 2–10 large joints (1); 1–3 small joints (2); 4–10 small joints (3); >10 joints with ≥1 small joint (5).
  • Serology: Negative RF & ACPA (0); Low-positive RF or ACPA [≤3x ULN] (2); High-positive RF or ACPA [>3x ULN] (3).
  • Acute-Phase Reactants: Normal CRP & ESR (0); Abnormal CRP or ESR (1).
  • Duration of Symptoms: <6 weeks (0); ≥6 weeks (1).

3. GOUTY ARTHRITIS & HYPERURICEMIA ▼

A. Etiology & Pathogenesis

Gout is a metabolic disease characterized by monosodium urate (MSU) crystal deposition in articular and periarticular tissues due to chronic hyperuricemia (serum urate saturation threshold >6.85 mg/dL). Humans develop gout because we lack uricase, an enzyme present in other mammals that converts urate into soluble allantoin.

  • Urate Kinetics: Renal Underexcretion accounts for ~90% of gout patients; Overproduction accounts for ~10%.
  • Risk Factors: Male sex (men >> postmenopausal women; premenopausal women rarely develop gout); Obesity and insulin resistance; Chronic kidney disease; Hypertension; Dyslipidemia.
  • Medication Triggers: Thiazide and loop diuretics, low-dose aspirin, pyrazinamide, cyclosporine.
  • Dietary Triggers: Alcohol (beer, hard liquor, wine), red meats, organ meats, seafood, high-fructose corn syrup beverages. (Note: Vegetables, legumes, nuts, plant oils are unrestricted!).

B. Clinical Stages & Features

  1. Asymptomatic Hyperuricemia: High urate without joint attacks (Guidelines state DO NOT treat unless SUA >9 mg/dL or high risk).
  2. Acute Gouty Arthritis: Excruciating, sudden-onset monoarthritis peaking within 24 hours. Erythema, swelling, and extreme warmth mimicking cellulitis.
    • Podagra: Involvement of the 1st Metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint—the most common initial site (>50%). Other common sites: ankle, knee, tarsal joints.
  3. Intercritical Gout: Symptom-free intervals between acute attacks.
  4. Chronic Tophaceous Gout: Develops 10+ years after uncontrolled hyperuricemia. Tophi are firm, yellow-white nodular MSU deposits discharging chalky material. Locations: helix of ear, olecranon bursa, Achilles tendon, prepatellar bursa, hands/feet.
  5. Renal Complications: Urolithiasis (uric acid kidney stones) and Uric Acid Nephropathy / Chronic Interstitial Nephritis.
⭐
MUST KNOW: Gold Standard Diagnosis of Gout
Gold Standard = Arthrocentesis and Synovial Fluid Analysis demonstrating Intracellular & Extracellular Needle-Shaped Monosodium Urate (MSU) Crystals showing Strong Negative Birefringence under polarized light microscopy (crystals appear bright yellow when parallel to the slow-wave axis of the red compensator).
Note: Serum uric acid levels can be normal or falsely low during an acute flare (due to inflammatory cytokine-induced uricosuria). Always recheck serum urate 2–4 weeks after the flare resolves!

4. PSEUDOGOUT / CALCIUM PYROPHOSPHATE DEPOSITION (CPPD) ▼

A. Clinical & Pathologic Features

CPPD is a microcrystalline arthritis predominantly affecting the elderly (prevalence doubles each decade >60 years). It results from elevated inorganic pyrophosphate in the cartilage matrix, leading to calcium pyrophosphate dihydrate crystal formation and release into the joint space, triggering inflammasome activation in macrophages.

🧠
EXAM TIP: CPPD Under Age 50
If a patient presents with CPPD / Pseudogout under 50 years of age, you MUST search for underlying metabolic disorders: Primary Hyperparathyroidism, Hemochromatosis ("Bronze Diabetes"), Hypomagnesemia, Hypophosphatasia, or Gitelman's Syndrome!

B. Diagnostic Characteristics

  • Synovial Fluid Microscopy: Demonstrates Rhomboid or Rod-shaped Calcium Pyrophosphate Crystals displaying Weak Positive Birefringence (appear blue when parallel to slow wave axis) under polarized light.
  • Radiographic Hallmark: Chondrocalcinosis—punctate and linear radiodense deposits within fibrocartilage (menisci of knee, triangular fibrocartilage of wrist) or hyaline articular cartilage.
  • Common Joint Sites: Knee (#1), Wrist, Shoulder, Elbow, Ankle. Acute attacks mimic gout flares and can be precipitated by trauma, severe illness, or surgery (e.g., parathyroidectomy rapid serum Ca/Mg shift).
  • Treatment: Aspiration, IA glucocorticoids, oral NSAIDs, or Colchicine. Note: There are NO disease-modifying maintenance drugs to dissolve CPP cartilage deposits!
5. ANKYLOSING SPONDYLITIS (AS) & AXIAL SPONDYLOARTHRITIS ▼

A. Pathophysiology & Clinical Features

Ankylosing Spondylitis is a chronic inflammatory disorder affecting the axial skeleton. Predominantly affects young males (Male:Female ratio = 3:1; onset in 2nd to 3rd decades [age 20–30]). Highly associated with HLA-B27 (>90% of cases).

  • Primary Lesion: Starts as Sacroiliitis (inflammatory destruction of sacroiliac joints). In the spine, inflammatory granulation tissue at the junction of the annulus fibrosus cartilage and vertebral bone erodes bone, forming bony excrescences called Syndesmophytes. Ascending progression leads to complete spinal fusion—the "Bamboo Spine".
  • Inflammatory Back Pain Criteria: Insidious onset of dull lower back/gluteal pain, age at onset <45 years, duration >3 months, worse in the morning or after rest, IMPROVES WITH EXERCISE/ACTIVITY, accompanied by several hours of morning stiffness. Alternating deep gluteal pain.
  • Extra-Articular Manifestations:
    • Anterior Uveitis / Iritis: Most common extra-articular feature (unilateral, painful red eye, photophobia).
    • Enthesitis: Inflammation at tendon/ligament insertion sites on bone (e.g., Achilles tendinitis, plantar fasciitis).
    • Apical Pulmonary Fibrosis, Aortic Insufficiency, Secondary Amyloidosis.
⭐
MUST KNOW: Therapeutic Modalities in AS
• Physical Therapy & Daily Exercise: Essential non-pharmacologic intervention to maintain spinal extension and chest expansion!
• NSAIDs: 1st-line pharmacotherapy for back pain and stiffness.
• Biologic Therapies (Anti-TNF-α & Anti-IL-17A): The ONLY agents proven to slow axial disease progression and prevent spinal fusion!
• Pitfall: Conventional DMARDs (Methotrexate, Sulfasalazine) only benefit peripheral joint involvement and have NO efficacy for axial spinal disease. Systemic Corticosteroids have NO ROLE in AS!
6. ACUTE RHEUMATIC FEVER (ARF) & RHEUMATIC HEART DISEASE ▼

A. Etiology & Diagnosis

ARF is a non-suppurative autoimmune sequela occurring 2–3 weeks after Group A Beta-Hemolytic Streptococcal (GAS) pharyngitis. Predominantly affects children aged 5–14 years. Pathology is driven by molecular mimicry between GAS M-protein antigens and human tissue cardiac myosin/valvular proteins.

💡
CLINICAL PEARL: Arthritis in ARF vs. RA
In RA, joint involvement is Symmetrical, Additive, and Persistent Polyarthritis of small joints.
In ARF, joint involvement is a Migratory Polyarthritis—it "jumps" from one large joint (knee) to another (ankle) as the initial joint improves. At physical exam, only 1 or 2 joints are inflamed at a time!

B. Secondary Antibiotic Prophylaxis Duration

Secondary prophylaxis prevents GAS reinfection and worsening Rheumatic Heart Disease (RHD). Regimen: Depot Benzathine Penicillin G 1.2 Million Units IM every 21 days (or 3-4 weeks):

  • ARF WITHOUT Carditis: Prophylaxis for 5 years after last attack OR until age 21 (whichever is longer).
  • ARF WITH Carditis but NO residual valvular disease: Prophylaxis for 10 years OR until age 21 (whichever is longer).
  • ARF WITH Carditis AND Persistent Valvular Disease (RHD): Prophylaxis for 10 years OR until age 40 (sometimes lifelong!).
7. ACUTE BACTERIAL / SEPTIC ARTHRITIS ▼

A. Pathogenesis & Microbiology

Septic arthritis is a rheumatologic emergency! Bacteria enter the joint primarily via Hematogenous spread (synovial capillaries lack a basement membrane, allowing rapid bacterial escape). Cartilage destruction begins within 48 hours due to bacterial toxins, chondrocyte proteases, and increased intra-articular pressure.

  • Staphylococcus aureus: The #1 overall cause of non-gonococcal septic arthritis across all age groups and adults. Also #1 in RA patients and diabetics.
  • Neisseria gonorrhoeae: The most common cause in sexually active young adults (<40 years; females 2-3x > males, especially during menses or pregnancy).
  • Group B Streptococci / Gram-negative Bacilli: Neonates, elderly, IV drug users, immunocompromised.

💊 Rapid High-Yield Drug Monitoring & Safety Rules

  • Methotrexate (MTX): Anchor csDMARD. Co-prescribe Folic acid 1 mg/day. Watch for Hepatotoxicity, Myelosuppression, and Interstitial Pneumonitis. Monitor CBC & LFTs q2-3 months. Teratogenic (Category X).
  • Hydroxychloroquine: Not a true DMARD alone. Watch for Irreversible Retinal Toxicity. Mandates annual OCT eye exams!
  • Allopurinol: 1st-line ULT. Titrate dose. Mandatory screening for HLA-B*58:01 in Southeast Asians (prevents Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis!).
  • Acute Gout Flare Rules: Colchicine (1.2 mg then 0.6 mg 1h later; stop at diarrhea!). NEVER START OR STOP ULT DURING AN ACUTE FLARE!
  • Anti-TNF-α Biologics: Mandatory screening for Latent TB (PPD/IGRA + CXR) and Hepatitis B prior to therapy!
  • Ankylosing Spondylitis: Only Anti-TNF-α & Anti-IL-17A prevent structural axial fusion. Systemic steroids have NO ROLE!
