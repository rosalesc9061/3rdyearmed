1. Hematopoietic Stem Cells (HSCs) vs. Leukemic Stem Cells (LSCs)

Stem Cell Biology

Normal Hematopoietic Stem Cells (HSCs)

HSCs possess the unique capacity for perpetual self-renewal and multilineage differentiation into all cellular components of the blood and immune system throughout lifetime.

  • Quiescence: Reside in a deep $G_0$ phase under steady state. Low metabolic rate with few mitochondria protects HSCs from oxidative stress, reactive oxygen species (ROS), and DNA damage.
  • BM Niche: Maintained in specialized microenvironments comprising Mesenchymal Stem Cells (MSCs), osteoblasts, endothelial cells, and extracellular matrix (ECM).
  • Surface Phenotype: The most primitive human HSCs are CD34+ / CD38- (comprising ~0.1% of total BM cells).
  • Stress Response: Activated to leave $G_0$ and proliferate during tissue stress (e.g., severe bleeding, systemic infection, post-chemotherapy myelosuppression). Reversible switch between dormancy and self-renewal.

Leukemic Stem Cells (LSCs)

Leukemia is fundamentally a stem cell disease. LSCs arise when HSCs acquire serial genetic and epigenetic mutations that disrupt steady-state growth control.

  • Loss of Quiescence: LSCs gain proliferative autonomy while preserving self-renewal.
  • Block in Differentiation: Accumulation of immature precursor blasts in the bone marrow.
  • Evasion of Apoptosis & Immunity: Downregulation of intrinsic apoptotic pathways and loss of MHC antigen presentation to evade CD8+ T-cell immunosurveillance.
  • Epigenetic Drivers: Mutations in genes controlling DNA methylation (DNMT3A, TET2) and chromatin remodeling (ASXL1) create "pre-leukemic" clones.

Steps in Clonal Evolution (Normal HSC to Overt Leukemia)

Stage Genetic & Biological Feature Clinical & Diagnostic Relevance
1. Normal HSC Tightly regulated self-renewal, deep quiescence, active DNA repair. Maintains lifelong balanced hematopoiesis.
2. ARCH / CHIP Age-Related Clonal Hematopoiesis / Clonal Hematopoiesis of Indeterminate Potential (somatic hits in DNMT3A, TET2, ASXL1). Asymptomatic, normal blood counts; increased risk of hematologic malignancy and cardiovascular disease.
3. Pre-Leukemic Stem Cell Epigenetic lesions perturb self-renewal/differentiation balance, but cell differentiation is preserved. Pre-leukemic clone persists in remission and can cause late disease relapse.
4. Leukemia Stem Cell (LSC) Cooperating "proliferative" hits acquired (e.g., NPM1, FLT3-ITD, RAS, BCR::ABL1, PML::RARA). Induces differentiation arrest and uncontrolled clone expansion. Basis for targeted drugs (e.g., FLT3 inhibitors, Menin inhibitors).
5. Overt Leukemia Massive BM accumulation of blasts (>20%), suppression of normal hematopoiesis causing pancytopenia. Clinical presentation with fever, anemia, bleeding; requires urgent intensive induction or targeted therapy.
⭐ Must Know

The surface phenotype defining the most primitive human HSCs in the BM is CD34+ / CD38-. CD34 is the primary target marker used during stem cell harvesting for autologous or allogeneic transplantation.

💡 Clinical Pearl

Founder mutations (e.g., DNMT3A, TET2) occur early in pre-LSCs and often persist during complete morphologic remission. In contrast, late cooperating mutations like NPM1 are unique to the leukemic clone and serve as excellent markers for Measurable Residual Disease (MRD) tracking via qPCR.

2. Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML)

BCR::ABL1 Positive MPN

CML is a myeloproliferative neoplasm originating from a pluripotent hematopoietic stem cell, defined molecularly by the Philadelphia chromosome.

Pathophysiology & Cytogenetics

Arises from reciprocal translocation between chromosomes 9 and 22: t(9;22)(q34.1;q11.2).

  • The ABL1 proto-oncogene on Chr 9 is fused to the BCR gene on Chr 22, forming the truncated der(22) or Philadelphia (Ph) chromosome.
  • Encodes a constitutively active BCR::ABL1 tyrosine kinase, activating downstream JAK-STAT, RAS/MAPK, and PI3K/AKT pathways.
  • Biological consequences: Uncontrolled granulocytic proliferation, reduced apoptosis, loss of stromal adhesion, and progressive genomic instability.

Clinical Pearl: Constitutive phosphorylation drives downstream survival pathways. Targeted TKI therapy binds the ATP-binding pocket or allosteric site to induce deep molecular remission.

Disease Phases & Clinical Presentation

  • Chronic Phase (CP): Preserved cell differentiation. Often asymptomatic (incidentally diagnosed on routine CBC). Symptomatic patients exhibit fatigue, weight loss, night sweats, low-grade fever, bone pain, gout (high purine turnover), and splenomegaly (causing left upper quadrant fullness and early satiety).
  • Blast Phase (BP): Defined by WHO 2022 as ≥20% blasts in peripheral blood or marrow, or extramedullary blast proliferation. CML can transform into either Myeloid BP (~70%) or Lymphoid BP (~30%) because CML arises in a multipotent stem cell.
⚠️ Common Pitfall (WHO 2022 Update)

The WHO 5th Edition (2022) officially eliminated the separate "Accelerated Phase" (AP) category, classifying CML into Chronic Phase and Blast Phase, emphasizing high-risk genetic features instead. However, ICC 2022 still retains AP criteria (10–19% blasts, ≥20% basophils).

3. Classic BCR::ABL1-Negative Myeloproliferative Neoplasms

PV | ET | PMF

Clonal HSC disorders characterized by panmyelosis, absence of BCR::ABL1, and driver mutations in JAK2, CALR, or MPL that constitutively activate the JAK-STAT signaling pathway.

Polycythemia Vera (PV)

WHO Major Criteria:

  1. Hgb >16.5 g/dL (men) / >16.0 g/dL (women) OR Hct >49% (men) / >48% (women).
  2. BM: Trilineage proliferation (panmyelosis) with mature pleomorphic megakaryocytes.
  3. Presence of JAK2 V617F or exon 12 mutation.

Minor Criterion: Subnormal serum EPO.

Treatment Goal: Maintain Hct <45% in ALL patients via phlebotomy + low-dose aspirin.
• High-risk (Age ≥60 or prior thrombosis): Hydroxyurea or Ropeginterferon alfa-2b (Besremi).
• 2026 Update: Rusfertide (hepcidin mimetic) for phlebotomy-dependent PV.

Essential Thrombocythemia (ET)

WHO Major Criteria:

  1. Platelets ≥450 × 10⁹/L.
  2. BM: Proliferation predominantly of megakaryocytic lineage; large, mature megakaryocytes with hyperlobulated nuclei ("staghorn"). No left shift.
  3. Exclude CML, PV, PMF, MDS.
  4. JAK2, CALR, or MPL mutation.
Pitfall: In low-risk CALR-mutated ET or extreme thrombocytosis (plts >1,000 × 10⁹/L), aspirin may increase bleeding risk due to Acquired von Willebrand Disease!

Primary Myelofibrosis (PMF)

Key Clinical Features:

  • Atypical megakaryocyte proliferation with reticulin/collagen fibrosis (Grades 2–3).
  • Peripheral Blood Smear: Teardrop red cells (dacrocytes) and leukoerythroblastosis (immature granulocytes + nucleated RBCs).
  • Massive, painful splenomegaly & severe constitutional symptoms.
Management:
• Symptomatic/Splenomegaly: JAK2 inhibitors (Ruxolitinib, Fedratinib, Pacritinib if plts <50k, Momelotinib if severe anemia).
• Only Curative Option: Allogeneic HSCT.

4. Acute Myelogenous Leukemia (AML) & APL Emergency

Acute Myeloid Clones

AML is a aggressive malignancy of myeloid progenitor cells characterized by rapid accumulation of leukemic blasts in the bone marrow, leading to severe hematopoietic failure.

Hallmark Diagnostic Features:

  • High Nuclear:Cytoplasmic (N:C) ratio.
  • Prominent reticular chromatin with multiple distinct nucleoli.
  • Basophilic cytoplasm containing azurophilic granules.
  • Auer Rods: Pathognomonic crystalline aggregates of fused primary lysosomes containing myeloperoxidase. Diagnostic of AML!
  • Faggot Cells: Bundles of numerous Auer rods in cytoplasm — characteristic of Acute Promyelocytic Leukemia (APL).

5. Myelodysplastic Neoplasms (MDS)

Ineffective Hematopoiesis

MDS represents a heterogeneous group of clonal stem cell disorders characterized by cytopenias, ineffective hematopoiesis, morphological dysplasia in ≥1 lineage, and variable risk of transformation to AML (Note: MDS transforms strictly to myeloid leukemia, never lymphoid!).

Spectrum of Indolent Myeloid Disorders

CHIP Absent Absent Present (e.g., DNMT3A, TET2) ~0.5 – 1% / year
ICUS Present Absent Absent Low (~0.1% / year)
CCUS Present Absent Present Intermediate-High
MDS Present Present (≥10% of lineage) Present Variable (IPSS-M defined)

WHO 2022 Classification of MDS

MDS, Genetically Defined

  • MDS-5q: Low blasts + isolated del(5q). Excellent response to Lenalidomide.
  • MDS-SF3B1: Low blasts + SF3B1 mutation (replaces RARS). Characterized by ringed sideroblasts (mitochondrial iron accumulation).
  • MDS-biTP53: Biallelic TP53 inactivation (mutation + deletion or 2 mutations). Extremely dismal prognosis.

MDS, Morphologically Defined

  • MDS-LB (Low Blasts): <5% BM blasts and <2% PB blasts.
  • MDS-h (Hypoplastic): Marrow cellularity <30%; overlaps with Aplastic Anemia.
  • MDS-IB1: 5–9% BM blasts or 2–4% PB blasts.
  • MDS-IB2: 10–19% BM blasts or 5–19% PB blasts, OR presence of Auer rods.

Risk-Stratified Management (2026 Updates)

💡 Lower-Risk MDS (Anemia Management)

• Erythropoiesis-Stimulating Agents (ESAs): First-line if serum EPO <500 IU/L.
• Luspatercept: Recombinant fusion protein targeting TGF-β signaling. First-line standard for lower-risk MDS with ring sideroblasts/SF3B1 (COMMANDS trial).
• Imetelstat: Telomerase inhibitor approved in 2024 for transfusion-dependent lower-risk MDS failing ESAs.
• Lenalidomide: Targeted for isolated del(5q).

⚠️ Higher-Risk MDS Standard & Trial Failure

• Standard First-Line: Hypomethylating Agents (HMAs) — Azacitidine, Decitabine, or oral Decitabine-cedazuridine.
• Curative Intent: Allogeneic HSCT for eligible patients.
• VERONA Trial Update (2025): Adding Venetoclax to Azacitidine FAILED to show overall survival benefit in higher-risk MDS. HMA monotherapy remains the standard!

6. Lymphomas (Hodgkin vs. Non-Hodgkin) & Mature Lymphoid Malignancies

Nodal & Extranodal Clones

Lymphomas are clonal malignant neoplasms of lymphocytes originating in lymph nodes or extranodal lymphoid tissues. Divided into Hodgkin Lymphoma (HL) and Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma (NHL).

Indolent Lymphomas (Low Grade)

Examples: Follicular Lymphoma, CLL/SLL, Marginal Zone Lymphoma.

  • Slow growth rate; often advanced stage (III/IV) at initial diagnosis.
  • Incurable with standard chemotherapy, but survival spans decades.
  • Management: "Watch-and-wait" approach for asymptomatic, low-tumor-burden disease.

Aggressive Lymphomas (High Grade)

Examples: DLBCL (most common NHL, ~31%), Burkitt, Mantle Cell, Lymphoblastic.

  • Rapidly growing mass, severe B symptoms; fatal in months if untreated.
  • Potentially curable with combination chemoimmunotherapy. High risk of Tumor Lysis Syndrome (TLS).

Pediatric & Adult Lymphoblasts

ALL is an aggressive acute leukemia originating from B-lineage (~85%) or T-lineage (~15%) lymphoid progenitors. Most common cancer in children (peak age 2–5 years), with a second incidence peak after age 50.

Cytogenetic & Molecular Risk Groups in B-ALL

Standard / Favorable Risk

  • High Hyperdiploidy (51–65 chromosomes): Trisomies of chromosomes 4, 10, and 17 have the most favorable outcome.
  • t(12;21)(p13;q22) / ETV6::RUNX1 (most common in children).
  • DUX4-rearranged; PAX5 p.P80R.

High / Poor Risk

  • Hypodiploidy (<44 chromosomes).
  • t(9;22)(q34;q11) / BCR::ABL1 (Ph+ ALL; predominant transcript is p190).
  • BCR::ABL1-like (Ph-like) ALL (CRLF2, JAK-STAT alterations).
  • KMT2A-rearranged (t(4;11)); TP53 mutation; IKZF1 deletion.

2026 ALL Treatment Breakthroughs

💡 Blinatumomab in Front-Line Consolidation

Blinatumomab (CD19×CD3 bispecific T-cell engager / BiTE) added to consolidation chemo significantly improves Overall Survival in Ph-negative B-ALL (E1910 trial: 3-yr OS 85% vs 68%; FDA approved 2024 regardless of MRD status!).

⭐ Philadelphia-Positive (Ph+) ALL

TKI backbone (Ponatinib or Dasatinib) + low-intensity chemotherapy or chemo-free Ponatinib + Blinatumomab achieves high rate of deep molecular response, avoiding intensive toxic chemotherapy in adults!

Clonal Plasma Cells

CRAB-SLiM Diagnostic Criteria for Active Myeloma

CRAB Criteria (End-Organ Damage)

  • C — Hypercalcemia: Serum Calcium >11.0 mg/dL (>2.75 mmol/L).
  • R — Renal Insufficiency: Creatinine Clearance <40 mL/min or Serum Creatinine >2.0 mg/dL.
  • A — Anemia: Hemoglobin <10.0 g/dL or >2.0 g/dL below lower limit of normal.
  • B — Bone Lesions: ≥1 osteolytic lesion on CT, PET-CT, or MRI (Plain radiography is obsolete due to low sensitivity).

SLiM Biomarkers (Treat Before CRAB)

  • S — Sixty Percent: ≥60% clonal plasma cells in bone marrow aspirate/biopsy.
  • Li — Light Chain Ratio: Involved/Uninvolved serum free light chain (FLC) ratio ≥100 (involved FLC must be ≥100 mg/L).
  • M — MRI: >1 focal bone marrow lesion (≥5 mm in size) on MRI.

2026 Myeloma Treatment Algorithm

💡 Newly Diagnosed — Transplant Eligible

• Induction: Quadruplet therapy D-VRd (Daratumumab + Bortezomib + Lenalidomide + Dexamethasone) x 4 cycles.
• Consolidation: High-dose Melphalan (200 mg/m²) + Autologous HSCT.
• Maintenance: Lenalidomide ± Daratumumab (PERSEUS trial).

🧠 Relapsed / Refractory (R/R) Myeloma Breakthroughs

• CAR-T Cell Therapies: Ciltacabtagene autoleucel (Cilta-cel) (approved after ≥1 prior line) & Ide-cel.
• Bispecific Antibodies: BCMA-directed (Teclistamab, Elranatamab, Linvoseltamab) and GPRC5D-directed (Talquetamab).
• Antibody-Drug Conjugates: Belantamab mafodotin + Vd (Oct 2025).

9. Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation (HSCT)

Transplant & Cellular Immunotherapy

Transplantation of multipotent HSCs derived from bone marrow, peripheral blood (apheresis), or umbilical cord blood. Serves as stem cell rescue or allogeneic immunotherapy.

Types & Primary Indications

  • Autologous HSCT: Patient's own harvested stem cells. Purpose: Rescue bone marrow from lethal myelosuppression following high-dose chemotherapy (e.g., Melphalan in Myeloma, BEAM in Lymphoma). No GVL effect, zero GVHD risk.
  • Allogeneic HSCT: Stem cells from HLA-matched sibling, matched unrelated donor (MUD), or haploidentical family donor. Purpose: Eradicate host malignancy via Graft-versus-Leukemia (GVL) immune response and reset hematopoiesis.

2026 Practice Breakthroughs

💡 Haploidentical HSCT + PTCy Revolution

Administering Post-Transplant Cyclophosphamide (PTCy) on days +3 and +4 selectively destroys rapidly proliferating, highly alloreactive donor T-cells while sparing non-proliferative memory T-cells and regulatory T-cells. This makes haploidentical family donor transplant outcomes equal to matched sibling donors!

GVHD Management & Philippine Landscape

Clinical Domain Current Practice Standards & Philippine Realities
GVHD Prophylaxis PTCy + Tacrolimus + Mycophenolate Mofetil (MMF) (BMT CTN 1703 trial proved superiority over Tacrolimus/Methotrexate).
Steroid-Refractory GVHD Ruxolitinib (JAK1/2 inhibitor approved for acute/chronic GVHD); Belumosudil (ROCK2 inhibitor) for chronic GVHD.

Side-by-Side Confusable Concept Comparisons

High-Yield Differential Diagnosis

Mastering easily confused entity pairs is essential for Philippine Physician Licensure Examination (PLE) and internal medicine specialty exams.

1. Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML) vs. Reactive Leukemoid Reaction

Feature Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML) Leukemoid Reaction (Severe Infection)
Leukocyte Alkaline Phosphatase (LAP) Low or Zero High / Elevated
Basophilia & Eosinophilia Prominent (>20% basophils in advanced) Absent
Splenomegaly Massive / Palpable Absent or mild
Molecular Marker BCR::ABL1 translocation t(9;22) positive Negative
Toxic Granulation / Dohle Bodies Absent Present in neutrophils

2. Polycythemia Vera (PV) vs. Secondary Polycythemia

Diagnostic Parameter Polycythemia Vera (PV) Secondary Erythrocytosis (Hypoxia/RCC)
Serum Erythropoietin (EPO) Subnormal / Low Elevated or Normal
JAK2 Mutation (V617F) Positive in ~95% Negative
White Cell & Platelet Counts Elevated (Panmyelosis: Leukocytosis + Thrombocytosis) Normal
Splenomegaly Present in 75% Absent
Aquagenic Pruritus Characteristic (itching after warm shower) Absent

3. Hodgkin Lymphoma (HL) vs. Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma (NHL)

Feature Hodgkin Lymphoma (HL) Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma (NHL)
Nodal Spread Pattern Contiguous, orderly spread along lymphatic chains Non-contiguous, erratic dissemination
Extranodal Involvement Rare (<10%) Common (~40%) (GI tract, CNS, skin)
Diagnostic Cell Reed-Sternberg Cells (CD15+ / CD30+) Monoclonal proliferation of B or T cells
Mesenteric Node / Waldeyer Ring Rarely involved Commonly involved
Associated Symptoms B symptoms + Severe Eosinophilic Pruritus B symptoms
