⭐ MUST KNOW: AIDS Definition
AIDS is defined by either: 1) CD4+ T-cell count < 200 cells/µL (or <14%), OR 2) Presence of any CDC Category C AIDS-defining illness regardless of CD4 count!
🧠 EXAM TIP: Advanced HIV Disease
WHO defines Advanced HIV Disease as CD4 count < 200 cells/mm³ OR a WHO Stage 3/4 event. In these patients, do NOT delay care; implement the advanced HIV disease package (OI screening, prophylaxis, rapid ART).
🧬 Topic 1: Virology, Structure & Replication Cycle
Etiologic Agent & Taxonomy

HIV belongs to the family Retroviridae and subfamily Lentiviruses ("slow viruses"). It is an enveloped, single-stranded RNA (ssRNA) virus with an icosahedral structure containing surface spikes.

Figure 1. Structure of the HIV Virion
Plain-Language Explanation: The HIV virion consists of an outer lipid membrane studded with gp120 (outer attachment) and gp41 (transmembrane fusion) glycoprotein spikes. Inside is a bullet-shaped p24 protein capsid housing 2 identical copies of single-stranded positive-sense RNA and key enzymes: Reverse Transcriptase, Integrase, and Protease.
Clinical Significance: gp120 and gp41 are target sites for attachment and fusion inhibitors; p24 antigen is measured in 4th-generation screening tests for early detection during the window period.
HIV Types & Subgroups
Type Epidemiology Groups / Subtypes Clinical Features
HIV-1 Worldwide; primary cause of global AIDS pandemic • Group M (Major): Drives the pandemic
• Groups N, O, P: Minor groups 		High transmissibility, higher viral loads, rapid CD4 progression if untreated.
HIV-2 First identified 1986; originally confined to West Africa Groups A through H Lower transmissibility, lower viral load, slower progression to AIDS, intrinsically resistant to NNRTIs.
🔄 The 7-Step HIV Replication Cycle & Drug Targets
1. Attachment & Binding: gp120 binds primary CD4 receptor on host cell → conformational change allowing binding to co-receptors CCR5 (macrophage-tropic / R5) or CXCR4 (T-tropic / X4).
Target: CCR5 Antagonists (Maraviroc), Attachment Inhibitors (Fostemsavir)
2. Fusion & Entry: gp41 mediates fusion of viral lipid envelope with host cell membrane, releasing viral core into cytoplasm.
Target: Fusion Inhibitors (Enfuvirtide)
3. Reverse Transcription: Viral single-stranded RNA is transcribed into double-stranded complementary DNA (cDNA) by viral Reverse Transcriptase.
Target: NRTIs (Tenofovir, Lamivudine, Abacavir) & NNRTIs (Efavirenz, Doravirine)
4. Integration: Viral cDNA enters host cell nucleus; Integrase inserts viral DNA into host genome → forming a Provirus (permanent infection).
Target: INSTIs (Dolutegravir, Bictegravir)
5. Transcription & Translation: Host RNA polymerase transcribes proviral DNA into viral genomic RNA and mRNA to synthesize long viral polyproteins.
6. Assembly & Budding: New viral RNA and precursor polyproteins assemble at host cell inner membrane and bud off as immature, non-infectious virions.
Target: Capsid Inhibitors (Lenacapavir)
7. Maturation: Viral Protease cleaves long precursor polyproteins into functional structural proteins and enzymes, transforming immature particle into infectious mature virion.
Target: Protease Inhibitors (Darunavir, Atazanavir)
⚠️ COMMON PITFALL: Protease Inhibitor Mechanism
Protease inhibitors do NOT prevent viral budding or assembly—they block viral cleavage/maturation. Uninhibited budding produces immature, non-infectious viral particles.
💡 CLINICAL PEARL: Provirus Permanence
Once integrase inserts viral DNA into the host genome to form a provirus, the infection becomes lifelong. Active viral replication can be suppressed by ART, but the provirus cannot be eradicated from resting latent cells.

🔍 Why Receptive Anal Intercourse Has the Highest Sexual Risk
  • Mucosal Fragility: Rectal mucosa is thin, single-layered columnar epithelium with abundant susceptible immune target cells (Langerhans and CD4+ T cells) immediately beneath.
  • Microtrauma: Friction causes microtrauma, creating direct entry portals for infected semen. In contrast, vaginal epithelium is thick, non-keratinized stratified squamous tissue providing a superior physical barrier.

👶 Mother-to-Child Transmission (MTCT) Breakdown

Without intervention, MTCT rates range from 15% to 45%. Transmission timing:

Timing Proportion of MTCT Key Determinants & Prophylaxis
In Utero (Pregnancy) 23%–30% Occurs in 1st/2nd trimesters across placental barrier.
Perinatal / Intrapartum (Delivery) 50%–65% (Most Common) Direct exposure to maternal blood and vaginal secretions in birth canal. Strongest predictor: Maternal plasma viremia!
Postpartum (Breastfeeding) 12%–20% Driven by viral load in milk, presence of mastitis, low maternal CD4, vitamin A deficiency.
⭐ MUST KNOW: Maternal Viremia Thresholds
Vertical transmission risk increases linearly with maternal viral load:
• < 1,000 copies/mL: Transmission very unlikely.
• < 50 copies/mL: Transmission extremely unlikely.
💡 CLINICAL PEARL: Breastfeeding Recommendations
In developed countries, breastfeeding is strictly contraindicated because safe infant formula is available. In developing countries, maternal ART continuation during breastfeeding dramatically reduces transmission and is recommended to prevent infant starvation/diarrheal mortality.
🧠 EXAM TIP: Male Circumcision
Male circumcision reduces female-to-male HIV acquisition risk by ~60% because the inner foreskin is rich in HIV target cells (Langerhans cells) and susceptible to microtrauma and inflammation.
⚠️ COMMON PITFALL: Non-Transmissible Fluids
Saliva, tears, sweat, and urine do NOT transmit HIV. Saliva contains natural antiviral factors (hypotonicity, secretory leukocyte protease inhibitor) that neutralize HIV. Kissing carries zero risk.
🌍 Global Burden & Philippine Epidemiology
  • Global Burden (2023): 39.9 million PLHIV globally; ~53% female; 1.4 million children <15 years. Total deaths since pandemic start: 42.3 million.
  • Epidemiologic Transition: While global new infections peak passed in 1990s and AIDS deaths dropped 65% since 2004 (due to ART), overall prevalence (PLHIV) continues to rise because treated individuals are living full lifespans.
  • Key Populations: Account for 55% of global new infections in 2022 (Sex workers, PWID, Transgender women, Prisoners, MSM).
  • Philippine Situation: While countries like Thailand and Vietnam show declining trends, the Philippines is experiencing one of the fastest-growing HIV epidemics in the world with steep increases in new cases among young MSM and key populations.
🔥 Topic 3: Pathogenesis, GALT Destruction & The Latent Reservoir
Chronological Pathogenesis Timeline
Figure 2. Classic Natural History of Untreated HIV Infection
Plain-Language Explanation: Following infection, acute viremia spikes exponentially alongside a sharp CD4 drop. Cytotoxic CD8+ T-cells partially suppress viremia down to a stable Viral Set Point, entering clinical latency. Over 8–10 years, CD4 counts steadily decline until falling below 200 cells/µL, leading to opportunistic infections, AIDS, and death.
Clinical Significance: Higher viral set point in untreated patients predicts faster progression to AIDS.
🌒 The Eclipse Phase

The Eclipse Phase is the very early window after mucosal exposure during which the virus replicates locally in mucosal tissue and seeds draining lymph nodes, but plasma HIV RNA remains undetectable. Undetectable HIV RNA in blood during this phase does NOT mean absence of virus; blood tests lag behind tissue events!

🩸 Gut-Associated Lymphoid Tissue (GALT) Destruction

GALT contains the majority of the body's total lymphocytes, specifically enriched in CD4+ Th17 cells. Primary HIV infection causes massive, selective depletion of GALT mucosal CD4+ T-cells before any significant drop in peripheral blood CD4 counts.

1. Mucosal Infection & GALT Seeding: Rapid local viral replication in GALT leads to massive destruction of Th17 CD4+ T-cells.
2. Mucosal Barrier Breakdown: Loss of mucosal immune integrity impairs tight junctions and epithelial defense.
3. Microbial Translocation: Bacterial products (e.g., Lipopolysaccharide / LPS) leak into systemic circulation.
4. Chronic Immune Activation: Persistent LPS and HIV antigens continuously drive systemic inflammation, lymphocyte turnover, and immune exhaustion.
🏰 Establishment of the Lifelong Latent Reservoir

Persistent infection is maintained primarily in resting CD4+ memory T-cells containing integrated provirus (post-integration latency). Because these cells produce no viral proteins, they remain invisible to host cytotoxic CD8+ T-cells and are unaffected by antiretroviral drugs. This reservoir is established within days of exposure—which is why ART suppresses viral replication but cannot cure infection.

⚔️ Mechanisms of CD4+ T-Cell Depletion
Direct Viral Mechanisms Indirect / Immune-Mediated Mechanisms
• Syncytium Formation: Fusion of infected CD4 cells with non-infected neighbors forming multinucleated giant cells that die.
• Direct cell lysis during viral budding.
• Disruption of cellular RNA processing.
• Accumulation of unintegrated viral DNA.
• gp120 membrane toxicity. 		• Bystander Apoptosis & Pyroptosis: Inflammatory cell death of uninfected bystander cells.
• Immune-mediated killing by HIV-specific CTLs.
• Autoimmune destruction.
• Impaired lymphopoiesis/production in bone marrow and thymus.
• Chronic immune exhaustion (PD-1, LAG-3, TIM-3 upregulation).
🎮 Long-Term Nonprogressors (LTNP) vs. Elite Controllers
Feature Long-Term Nonprogressor (LTNP) Elite Controller
ART Status No ART No ART
Viral Load Low; may be detectable Extremely low / Undetectable (<50 copies/mL)
CD4 Count Relatively preserved for years Usually normal / completely preserved

⭐ MUST KNOW: LTNP vs Long-Term Survivor
All LTNPs are long-term survivors, but NOT all long-term survivors are nonprogressors! Long-term survival today is expected with effective ART; LTNP refers specifically to rare untreated individuals who resist progression.
💡 CLINICAL PEARL: Elite Controllers Aren't Cured
Elite controllers still harbor integrated latent proviral DNA reservoirs and can experience chronic inflammatory complications (cardiovascular, renal disease). They are NOT cured.
🧪 Topic 4: Diagnostic Algorithm, Baseline Workup & Staging
🔄 CDC Recommended HIV Diagnostic Algorithm

Diagnosis relies on a sequential 3-step paradigm: Screen → Differentiate → Resolve.

Figure 3. Official CDC HIV Diagnostic Algorithm Flowchart
STEP 1: Initial Screen
Perform 4th-Generation HIV-1/2 Ag/Ab Combination Immunoassay (detects p24 antigen + HIV-1/HIV-2 antibodies).
If Nonreactive: Negative for HIV-1 and HIV-2. (If recent exposure within 3-month window period, retest).
If Reactive: Proceed immediately to Step 2! (Never diagnose from a single screening test).
STEP 2: Differentiation
Perform HIV-1/HIV-2 Antibody Differentiation Immunoassay.
If Positive for HIV-1 or HIV-2 Ab: Confirmed HIV-1 or HIV-2 Infection! Link to care.
If Negative or Indeterminate: Suspect Acute Infection! Proceed to Step 3.
STEP 3: Resolution
Perform HIV-1 Nucleic Acid Test (NAT / Viral Load).
(+) NAT: Confirmed Acute HIV-1 Infection (viremia present before antibody maturation).
(-) NAT: False-positive 4th-Gen screen. Consider HIV-2 NAT if clinical suspicion remains.
Plain-Language Explanation: 4th-gen immunoassay detects p24 antigen and antibodies. If positive, differentiation confirms antibody type. If differentiation is negative, NAT detects early viral RNA in acute HIV before seroconversion.
Clinical Significance: Western Blot is completely retired and no longer used in standard diagnostic algorithms.
⚖️ Clinical Utility: CD4+ Count vs. HIV RNA Viral Load
Parameter CD4+ T-Lymphocyte Count Plasma HIV RNA (Viral Load)
What It Measures Current immune status / damage Magnitude of active viral replication
Analogy Damage to the castle walls "Virologic Thermometer" / Speed of approaching train
Primary Clinical Use Determines OI risk, guides primary prophylaxis, identifies advanced HIV disease. Primary marker of ART response, therapeutic success, and transmission risk (U=U).
Response to ART Slow recovery over months/years Rapid exponential decline within weeks
📋 Baseline Laboratory Evaluation Prior to ART
  • Virologic/Immunologic: HIV RNA baseline, CD4 count, HIV Drug Resistance Testing.
  • Safety/Organ Function: CBC, Creatinine/eGFR (guides NRTI dosing), AST/ALT/Bilirubin.
  • Coinfections & Screening: Hepatitis B (HBsAg, anti-HBs, anti-HBc) & Hepatitis C screening, Syphilis (RPR/VDRL), Gonorrhea/Chlamydia nucleic acid tests.
  • Genetic Biomarker: HLA-B*57:01 screening prior to starting Abacavir (ABC) to prevent severe, life-threatening hypersensitivity reaction.
⚠️ COMMON PITFALL: Diagnostic Delay
Do NOT delay starting ART or necessary clinical care while waiting for baseline laboratory results (e.g., resistance test or CD4 count). ART can be rapidly initiated while pending baseline labs.
🧠 EXAM TIP: Window Period
HIV antibodies generally appear 3 to 12 weeks post-exposure. 4th-gen 24 antigen detection narrows the diagnostic window period to ~14–20 days.

⚠️ Immune Reconstitution Inflammatory Syndrome (IRIS) & ART Timing

IRIS occurs when rapid immune recovery following ART initiation triggers an exaggerated hyper-inflammatory reaction against underlying opportunistic antigens. It presents as paradoxical clinical worsening.

Opportunistic Infection CD4 Count Recommended ART Start Timing Rationale
Pulmonary Tuberculosis CD4 < 50 / µL Start ART ASAP (within 2 weeks) Mortality risk from severe immunosuppression outweighs IRIS risk.
CD4 ≥ 50 / µL Delay ART by 2 to 8 weeks Reduces risk and severity of debilitating pulmonary IRIS.
Cryptococcal Meningitis Any CD4 Delay ART by 2 to 6 weeks CNS IRIS causes severe intracranial hypertension, brain herniation, and mortality.
TB Meningitis Any CD4 Delay ART until intensive TB phase ends Prevent fatal neuro-IRIS and severe cerebral edema.
⚠️ COMMON PITFALL: PML Clinical Clue
If a patient with progressive neurological deficits presents with prominent fever or severe headache, rule out PML! PML lesions cause demyelination without significant mass effect or systemic inflammation, so fever/headache are characteristically ABSENT.
💡 CLINICAL PEARL: Toxoplasmosis vs Lymphoma
When multiple ring-enhancing lesions are seen on MRI, initiate empiric anti-toxoplasma therapy. If there is no clinical or radiological improvement after 10 to 14 days, proceed to brain biopsy or CSF EBV PCR to evaluate for Primary CNS Lymphoma.
💊 Topic 6: Antiretroviral Therapy (ART) & Prevention Strategies
🎯 Principles & Goals of Antiretroviral Therapy

ART is recommended for ALL individuals with confirmed HIV infection regardless of CD4 cell count. The primary treatment goal is durable virologic suppression (HIV RNA < 20 copies/mL), which restores immune function, reduces morbidity/mortality, and eliminates sexual transmission risk.

🇵🇭 Preferred Initial ART Regimen (Philippine Standard)

Modern guidelines favor an INSTI-based 3-drug regimen containing 2 NRTIs + 1 second-generation INSTI due to high potency, high genetic barrier to resistance, and excellent tolerability.

⭐ MUST KNOW: The Philippine "LTD" Regimen
In the Philippines HIV National Program, the standard preferred first-line single-tablet regimen is LTD:
• Lamivudine (3TC) - 300 mg
• Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate (TDF) - 300 mg
• Dolutegravir (DTG) - 50 mg
🧠 EXAM TIP: Rapid ART Initiation
WHO 2025 guidelines recommend Rapid ART initiation within 7 days of confirmed diagnosis (including Same-Day initiation for ready patients). Exceptions: Delay ART in Cryptococcal Meningitis, TB Meningitis, or TB with CD4 ≥ 50!
⚙️ Clinical Management in Special Populations
  • Hepatitis B (HBV) Co-infection: The ART regimen MUST include two drugs active against HBV (Tenofovir [TDF or TAF] + Lamivudine [3TC] or Emtricitabine [FTC]). Pitfall: Discontinuing HBV-active NRTIs causes severe, potentially fatal hepatic flares!
  • Renal Dysfunction (eGFR < 50 mL/min): Avoid TDF due to nephrotoxicity. Switch to TAF or adjust 3TC/TDF dosing intervals.
  • Pregnancy: Dolutegravir-based regimens are preferred throughout all trimesters of pregnancy. Viral suppression prevents perinatal transmission.

⚡ Last-Minute Ultra-Condensed Cram Sheet

Rapid 10-minute review before exams. Covers high-yield triggers, buzzwords, and diagnostic shortcuts.

🎯 "If You See X → Think Y" Clinical Triggers
  • Subacute dry cough + exertional dyspnea + CD4 < 200: Think Pneumocystis jirovecii Pneumonia (PJP). Give High-dose TMP-SMX + Steroids.
  • Multiple ring-enhancing brain lesions + CD4 < 200: Think Cerebral Toxoplasmosis. Give Pyrimethamine + Sulfadiazine + Leucovorin.
  • Solitary periventricular brain lesion + EBV DNA in CSF: Think Primary CNS Lymphoma.
  • Subacute headache + CD4 < 100 + India Ink clear halos: Think Cryptococcal Meningitis. Give Ampho B + Flucytosine. Delay ART 2–6 weeks!
  • "Pizza pie" fundoscopy (retinal hemorrhage + exudates) + CD4 < 50: Think CMV Retinitis. Give Oral Valganciclovir.
  • Progressive focal neurological deficits WITHOUT fever or headache: Think PML (JC Virus). MRI shows non-enhancing white matter lesions. Start ART immediately.
  • Violaceous cutaneous/oral macules/plaques + HHV-8: Think Kaposi Sarcoma. Start ART.
  • SE Asian traveler + fever + umbilicated papular skin lesions: Think Talaromycosis (Talaromyces marneffei).
  • Hypersensitivity reaction + fever/rash + starting Abacavir: HLA-B*57:01 positive.
💊 Primary Prophylaxis Rapid Summary
  • CD4 < 200 / µL: Start TMP-SMX (1 DS or SS tab daily) for PJP.
  • CD4 < 100 / µL + Toxo IgG(+): TMP-SMX 1 DS tab daily covers BOTH PJP and Toxoplasmosis.
  • TB Exposure / Positive TST (≥5mm) / IGRA(+): Give IPT (3HP: Weekly INH + Rifapentine x 12 wks) regardless of CD4.
  • Discontinuation Rule: Stop PJP/Toxo primary prophylaxis when CD4 > 200 / µL for ≥3 consecutive months on ART.
⏳ Rapid ART Exceptions (When to DELAY ART)
  • Cryptococcal Meningitis: Delay ART by 2 to 6 weeks to prevent fatal intracranial IRIS.
  • TB Meningitis: Delay ART until intensive anti-TB therapy completes to prevent neuro-IRIS.
  • Pulmonary TB + CD4 ≥ 50: Delay ART by 2 to 8 weeks.
  • Pulmonary TB + CD4 < 50: Start ART ASAP (within 2 weeks)! Benefits outweigh IRIS risk.
🔬 Diagnostic Algorithm Shortcuts
  • Initial Screen: 4th-Gen HIV-1/2 Ag/Ab Immunoassay (p24 Ag + Abs).
  • If Screen(+): Do HIV-1/HIV-2 Antibody Differentiation Assay.
  • If Diff(-/Indeterminate): Perform HIV-1 NAT (RNA) to diagnose Acute HIV-1.
  • Retired Test: Western Blot is NO LONGER used.
  • U = U Threshold: Plasma HIV RNA < 20 copies/mL = Zero sexual transmission.
