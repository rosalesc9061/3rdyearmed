Fatty Liver and Cirrhosis (Alcohol, NAFLD, Cirrhosis)

🎯 Learning Objectives
  • Describe the spectrum of fatty liver disease.
  • Describe common etiologies of fatty liver, pathophysiology, clinical signs/symptoms, and treatment.
  • Describe causes of liver cirrhosis, its complications, and management.

Definition of Terms: Steatotic Liver Disease (SLD)

Steatotic Liver Disease (SLD) encompasses several disease entities based on their cause.

Disease Entity Description & Criteria
Alcohol-Associated Liver Disease (ALD) Patients consuming higher amounts of alcohol, irrespective of metabolic risk factors.
MASLD / MASH
(previously NAFLD/NASH)		 Metabolic Dysfunction-associated Steatotic Liver Disease.
Criteria: Hepatic steatosis + Drink a small amount of alcohol (Women: <20 g/day, Men: <30 g/day) + ≥1 cardiometabolic risk factor (obesity, HTN, dyslipidemia, insulin resistance/DM2).
MetALD Metabolic and Alcohol-associated Liver Disease.
Criteria: (1) Evidence of metabolic dysfunction AND (2) Consume social amounts of alcohol (Women: 20-50 g/day; Men: 30-60 g/day).

One Drink Equivalents & Binge Drinking

14g of ethanol = 1 oz of 80% spirits = 4 oz of wine = 1 bottle of beer.

  • Binge Drinking: Men: ≥5 drinks w/in 2 hours. Women: ≥4 drinks w/in 2 hours.
  • Alcohol Use Disorder: Impaired ability to stop/control alcohol use despite harmful consequences.
💡 Clinical Pearl

48% of cases of cirrhosis are attributed to alcohol. ALD presents as a spectrum ranging from fatty liver/steatohepatitis to advanced fibrosis and cirrhosis.

Alcohol-Associated Liver Disease (ALD) & AH

Pathogenesis of ALD

  • Alcohol is primarily metabolized in the liver (via portal vein).
  • ADH1 (Alcohol dehydrogenase): Oxidizes alcohol into acetaldehyde.
  • Acetaldehyde toxicity: Causes direct cellular toxicity and generation of Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) → lipid peroxidation & DNA damage.
  • ALDH (Acetaldehyde dehydrogenase): Converts acetaldehyde to acetate. Deficiency of ALDH → Acetaldehyde accumulation (nausea, cutaneous flushing).
  • Acetate → Acetyl-CoA → Fatty acid & triglyceride synthesis (Steatosis).
  • CYP2E1: Induced by chronic alcohol consumption.
  • Kupffer cell activation: Gut-derived endotoxin from increased gut permeability triggers Kupffer cells → release cytokines (TNF) → liver injury.
⚠️ Female Susceptibility

Females have a lower activity of gastric ADH. Therefore, more unmetabolized ethanol enters the bloodstream, increasing the risk of ALD at lower consumption levels.

Clinical Features

  • General: Tiredness, malnutrition, sarcopenia.
  • Abdominal: Discomfort, hepatosplenomegaly, caput medusae, ascites.
  • Skin/Eyes: Spider angiomata, palmar erythema, scleral icterus, rhinophyma.
  • Hands: Dupuytren contracture (due to microvascular injury; acetaldehyde activates fibroblasts → excess collagen → nodular thickened palmar fascia).
  • Reproductive: Gynecomastia, testicular atrophy (hypogonadism: decreased testosterone, increased estrogen).
  • Neurologic: Encephalopathy (asterixis), Wernicke-Korsakoff (B1 deficiency).
⭐ Alcohol-associated Hepatitis (AH)

Heavy alcohol consumption for >6 months, with <60 days abstinence before symptoms.
S/Sx: Rapid-onset jaundice, malaise, fever, tender hepatomegaly, hepatic decompensation. Can be accompanied by SIRS and AKI.
Lab Profile: AST and ALT < 400 IU/L, AST/ALT ratio > 1.5, Serum bilirubin > 3 mg/dL.

Diagnosis & Elastography (VCTE)

  • Exclude other diseases. Identify fat via Ultrasound, CT, MRI, MR-PDFF.
  • Vibration-Controlled Transient Elastography (VCTE): Evaluates liver stiffness.
    • <6 kPa: Normal
    • >8 kPa: Advanced fibrosis (F3)
    • >12.5 kPa: Cirrhosis (F4)
  • Biopsy Findings: Macrovesicular steatosis, hepatocyte ballooning with Mallory-Denk bodies, lobular inflammation (neutrophils).

Algorithm: Treatment of Alcoholic Hepatitis

1. Classify Severity:
Moderate AH MDF <32 or MELD ≤20 → Alcohol abstinence + Nutritional support
Severe AH MDF ≥32 or MELD >20 → Check for Steroid Contraindications

2. Treatment for Severe AH (if no contraindications):
Start Prednisolone 40 mg/day PO (or Methylprednisolone 32 mg/d IV)

3. Reassessment at Day 7 (Lille Score):
Lille <0.45 (Responder) → Continue for 28 days total.
Lille ≥0.45 (Non-responder) → Stop prednisolone → Consider early Liver Transplant or Palliative care.

Note: Enteral nutrition (35-40 kcal/kg/day) + Zinc + Vit B & K are essential.

🧠 Formulas to Know

Maddrey Discriminant Function (MDF): 4.6 x (Patient PT - Control PT) + Serum bilirubin (mg/dL)

Zieve's Syndrome: Triad of Hemolytic anemia + Hyperlipidemia + Jaundice in severe AH.

MASLD and MASH

Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease. Commonly in overweight/obese, but also in lean individuals (truncal obesity, Asian ethnicity). Diabetes is the strongest risk factor.

Histopathologic Spectrum

Stage Description Fibrosis Progression
Simple Steatosis Absence of overt liver injury/inflammation. Usually benign. 1 stage per 14 years
MASH Lipotoxicity. Accumulation of inflammatory cells and injured/dying hepatocytes. High risk for primary liver cancer. 1 stage per 7 years
Cirrhosis (F4) Hepatocytes succumb to lipotoxicity → defective regenerative response → fibrous scar. HCC incidence 1-2% per year

Staging & Risk Stratification

Targeted screening only (not for the general population). High-risk groups: T2DM, Obesity, Family Hx of Cirrhosis.

Algorithm: MASLD Staging

  1. Primary Risk Assessment: FIB-4 Score (Age, AST, ALT, Platelets)
    • <1.3 (Low Risk): PCP follow-up.
    • ≥1.3: Proceed to secondary assessment.
    • >2.67 or persistent elevated AST/ALT: Refer to Gastro/Hepatology.
  2. Secondary Assessment (Non-invasive tests):
    • VCTE (kPa): <8 (Low), 8-12 (Intermediate), >12 (High).
    • ELF Blood test: Measures hyaluronic acid, TIMP-1, PIIINP. <7.7 (Low), >9.8 (High).
⚠️ Diagnostic Pitfall

Normal liver enzymes do NOT automatically exclude MASLD. True Normal ALT is 29-33 U/L (Men) and 19-25 U/L (Women).

Treatment of MASLD

  • Weight Loss: 3-5% improves steatosis. >10% improves MASH and fibrosis.
  • Diet & Exercise: Mediterranean diet; Brewed coffee (≥3 cups daily) shows benefit. Exercise 150 mins/week.
  • Pharmacologic:
    • Pioglitazone: Improves insulin resistance. (AE: weight gain, bone loss, heart failure).
    • GLP-1 RAs (Semaglutide): Weight loss, improves lipids/glucose.
    • Dual Incretin (Tirzepatide): Significant weight loss up to 20.9%.
    • Statins: Safe across MASLD spectrum. Long term use decreases cirrhosis/HCC risk and portal pressure.
  • No Benefits: UDCA, Betaine, Silymarin.
  • Bariatric Surgery: Indicated if BMI ≥ 40, or BMI ≥ 35 with comorbidities. Improves MASH and fibrosis.

Liver Cirrhosis

Previously thought irreversible, but can be reversible if the underlying insult is removed. Categorized as compensated or decompensated (presence of ascites, encephalopathy, or variceal bleeding).

Pathogenesis of Fibrosis

Activation of Hepatic Stellate Cells (in the space of Disse). Result of epithelial injury → release of cytokines by Kupffer cells → paracrine molecules from sinusoidal endothelial cells → collagen deposition.

Causes & Specifics

Etiology Key Features & Treatment
Alcohol Micronodular. AST>ALT 2:1. Tx: Abstinence, nutrition.
Chronic Viral (HBV, HCV) Dominant cause in PH. HBV: Perinatal transmission. HCV: Blood-borne. Tx: Antivirals (Tenofovir for HBV; Direct Acting Antivirals for HCV).
Autoimmune Hepatitis (AIH) Diagnosis: ANA, ASMA (+). Tx: Immunosuppression (steroids, azathioprine).
Cardiac Cirrhosis Long-standing right-sided CHF → central venous pressure backup → hepatic vein congestion → necrosis.

General Features of Cirrhosis

  • Malnutrition: Sarcopenia. Requires 35 kcal/kg/day, 1.2-1.5g protein/kg/day. Multiple small meals including a late evening snack to prevent overnight catabolism.
  • Coagulation Abnormalities:
    • Decreased pro- and anti-coagulant factors (balanced state).
    • Deranged PT/INR does NOT predict bleeding risk.
    • FFP not routinely required unless active bleeding. Transfuse platelets if <50,000 before procedures.
  • Bone Disease: Osteoporosis (DEXA scan → Bisphosphonates).

Portal Hypertension & Complications

⭐ Portal Hypertension Basics

Elevation of Hepatic Venous Pressure Gradient (HVPG) > 5 mmHg. Clinically significant: ≥10 mmHg.
Caused by: Increased intrahepatic resistance (collagen in space of Disse) + Increased splanchnic blood flow (vasodilation driven by Nitric Oxide).

1. Gastroesophageal Varices

  • Location: Distal 2-3 cm of esophagus (left gastric/coronary vein connects to azygos vein).
  • Endoscopy signs: Red wale sign, cherry red spots, diffuse erythema.
  • Primary Prophylaxis: Medium/large varices. Use Nonselective Beta Blockers (NSBB) (Nadolol, Carvedilol). Goal HR 55-60 bpm, SBP >90. Alternative: Endoscopic Variceal Ligation (EVL).
  • Acute Hemorrhage Management:
    • Restrict transfusion (Goal Hgb 7-9 g/dL).
    • Pharmacologic: Octreotide/Somatostatin (splanchnic vasoconstriction) + Ceftriaxone (prevent SBP).
    • Endoscopy: EVL for esophageal, injection for gastric.
    • Salvage: TIPS (Transjugular Intrahepatic Portosystemic Shunt).

2. Ascites & SBP

Pathogenesis: Portal HTN → Splanchnic vasodilation → RAAS activation → Na/H2O retention. Hypoalbuminemia → fluid shifts to peritoneum.

  • Diagnosis: Paracentesis → SAAG > 1.1 g/dL indicates portal hypertension.
  • Treatment: Na restriction (80-120 mEq/day), Spironolactone + Furosemide (100mg:40mg ratio).
  • Refractory Ascites: Large-volume paracentesis (LVP) + IV Albumin (6-8 g/L for every liter removed >5L) to prevent circulatory dysfunction.
⭐ Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP)

Diagnosis: Ascitic fluid ANC > 250 / μL. Most common bug: E. coli.
Treatment: 3rd-gen cephalosporin (Ceftriaxone) for 5 days + IV Albumin (1.5g/kg day 1, 1g/kg day 3 to reduce renal failure risk).

3. Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS)

Functional renal failure without renal structural pathology. Caused by severe splanchnic vasodilation → extreme renal vasoconstriction.

  • AKI-HRS (Type 1): Rapid progression. 0.3 mg/dL rise in 48hrs or 50% rise from baseline.
  • CKD-HRS (Type 2): Slower progression, stable.
  • Treatment: Vasoconstrictor (Terlipressin or low-dose NE) + IV Albumin. Liver transplant is the only cure.

4. Hepatic Encephalopathy (HE)

Gut-derived neurotoxins (ammonia) reach the brain due to shunting. S/Sx: Asterixis, confusion, altered sleep.

  • Treatment: Identify triggers (Infection, GI bleed, constipation).
  • First-line: Lactulose (acidifies colon, catharsis). Goal: 2-3 soft stools/day.
  • Adjunctive: Rifaximin (decreases ammonia-producing bacteria).

5. Liver-Lung Syndromes

Hepatopulmonary Syndrome (HPS) Portopulmonary Hypertension (PPHT)
Intrapulmonary vascular dilations → shunting. Pulmonary vasoconstriction due to serotonin/endothelin-1.
Arterial hypoxemia. Dyspnea, platypnea. Pulmonary hypertension. Dyspnea, fatigue.
Tx: O2 supplementation, Transplant. Tx: Vasodilators, Transplant.

High-Yield Comparisons

Primary Biliary Cholangitis (PBC) vs Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis (PSC)

Feature PBC PSC
Demographics Middle-aged women (median 50) Men, highly associated with IBD (Ulcerative Colitis 60-80%)
Pathology Small/medium intrahepatic ducts Intrahepatic and extrahepatic biliary tree
Antibody Marker AMA (Antimitochondrial) in 95% pANCA positive in ~65%
Key Imaging/Skin Xanthelasma, hyperpigmentation "Beading" on MRCP/ERCP (strictures + dilation)
Treatment UDCA (Ursodeoxycholic acid) No specific medical Tx; ERCP dilation, Transplant

Cram Sheet: Last-Minute Reviewer

Buzzwords & Rapid Associations

  • AST:ALT ratio > 1.5 or 2:1 → Alcohol-associated Liver Disease.
  • MDF ≥ 32 → Severe Alcoholic Hepatitis (Start Prednisolone 40mg).
  • True normal ALT → Men 29-33, Women 19-25. Normal labs don't rule out MASLD!
  • FIB-4 Score → First-line non-invasive test to exclude advanced fibrosis in MASLD.
  • Weight Loss >10% → Required to improve MASH and fibrosis.
  • Antimitochondrial Antibody (AMA) → Primary Biliary Cholangitis (PBC).
  • pANCA + Ulcerative Colitis → Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis (PSC).
  • Beading of bile ducts on MRCP → PSC.
  • Tx for PBC → Ursodeoxycholic Acid (UDCA).
  • Dupuytren's Contracture → Microvascular injury; acetaldehyde activates fibroblasts.
  • Zieve's Syndrome → Hemolytic anemia + hyperlipidemia + jaundice (in severe AH).
  • SAAG > 1.1 → Portal Hypertension (Cirrhosis, cardiac ascites).
  • Ascitic fluid ANC > 250 → Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP). Tx: Ceftriaxone + IV Albumin.
  • Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS) → Functional renal failure (vasoconstriction). Tx: Terlipressin + Albumin.
  • Late evening snack → Recommended for cirrhotics to prevent overnight catabolism/sarcopenia.
  • Lactulose → First line for Hepatic Encephalopathy (goal 2-3 soft stools/day).
  • Goal for variceal prophylaxis (NSBB) → HR 55-60 bpm, SBP > 90.
"If You See X → Think Y"
  • If you see Asterixis → Think Hepatic Encephalopathy.
  • If you see Platypnea (dyspnea worse upright) → Think Hepatopulmonary Syndrome.
  • If you see Cost-effective, no radiation risk imaging to confirm ascites → Think Ultrasound.
  • If you see VCTE > 12.5 kPa → Think Cirrhosis (F4).
  • If you see Deranged PT in cirrhosis with bleeding → Think Transfuse FFP ONLY if actively bleeding (PT doesn't predict risk).
