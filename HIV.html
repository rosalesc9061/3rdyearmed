Welcome to the ultimate interactive study module on Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV), specifically crafted for 3rd-year Philippine medical students preparing for long examinations and the Physician Licensure Examination (PLE). This reviewer integrates 100% of the lecture source material with high-yield clinical reasoning chains, diagnostic flowcharts, and active-recall tools[cite: 1].

🎯 Key Learning Objectives

  • Master the virology, genome structure, and 7-step replication cycle of HIV-1 and HIV-2[cite: 1].
  • Detail the precise per-act probabilities of transmission across sexual, parenteral, and vertical routes[cite: 1].
  • Trace the immunopathogenesis timeline: from mucosal entry and GALT Th17 depletion to microbial translocation, chronic immune activation, and AIDS[cite: 1].
  • Execute the updated CDC/WHO HIV Diagnostic Algorithm (4th Gen Ag/Ab → Differentiation → NAT) without error[cite: 1].
  • Recognize, diagnose, and treat all major Opportunistic Infections (OIs) based on CD4+ T-cell count thresholds[cite: 1].

💡 Core High-Yield Definitions

  • Advanced HIV Disease CD4+ T-cell count < 200 cells/µL OR any WHO Stage 3/4 event[cite: 1].
  • AIDS Definition Presence of an AIDS-Defining Condition (Category C) OR CD4+ count < 200 cells/µL (or < 14%)[cite: 1].
  • U = U Undetectable = Untransmittable. Sustained viral suppression (< 20 copies/mL) eliminates sexual transmission risk[cite: 1].
  • Viral Set Point Stable plasma HIV RNA level established after acute infection, predicting rate of CD4 decline[cite: 1].
💡
Clinical Pearl for Philippine Medical Boards: Tuberculosis remains the #1 cause of illness and death among People Living with HIV (PLHIV) in the Philippines[cite: 1]. Always maintain a low threshold to evaluate for TB when presented with an HIV patient exhibiting fever, weight loss, cough, or lymphadenopathy[cite: 1].

📜 Section I & II: Historical Perspective & HIV/AIDS Classification

🕰️ Historical Timeline Milestones

  • 1981: First recognized cases of AIDS signaled a new, unexplained immunodeficiency syndrome following clusters of Pneumocystis pneumonia (PCP) and Kaposi sarcoma (KS) in young gay men[cite: 1].
  • 1983: Etiologic retrovirus identified (subsequently named Human Immunodeficiency Virus)[cite: 1].
  • 1985: First commercial screening test (ELISA) developed for large-scale blood donor screening[cite: 1].
  • Present: Transformation of HIV from a fatal terminal diagnosis into a manageable chronic condition via effective Antiretroviral Therapy (ART)[cite: 1].

📊 CDC HIV Infection Staging System

HIV staging is based on two essential criteria: CD4+ T-lymphocyte count/percentage and Clinical Category[cite: 1].

CDC Stage CD4+ Count (≥6 yrs through Adult) CD4+ Percentage Clinical Description
Stage 1 ≥ 500 cells/µL[cite: 1] ≥ 26%[cite: 1] No AIDS-defining condition[cite: 1]
Stage 2 200 – 499 cells/µL[cite: 1] 14% – 25%[cite: 1] No AIDS-defining condition[cite: 1]
Stage 3 (AIDS) < 200 cells/µL[cite: 1] < 14%[cite: 1] OR presence of any AIDS-Defining Illness[cite: 1]

Category A

Asymptomatic HIV infection, Persistent Generalized Lymphadenopathy (PGL), or Acute (primary) HIV infection syndrome[cite: 1].

Category B

Symptomatic conditions non-A, non-C (e.g., Oropharyngeal thrush, Vulvovaginal candidiasis persistent, Oral hairy leukoplakia, Herpes zoster ≥2 episodes, Peripheral neuropathy)[cite: 1].

Category C (AIDS)

AIDS-defining clinical conditions (e.g., PCP, Esophageal candidiasis, Cryptococcal meningitis, CMV retinitis, Brain Toxoplasmosis, Kaposi Sarcoma, Invasive Cervical Cancer)[cite: 1].

⭐
MUST KNOW FOR EXAMS: If a patient develops a Category C AIDS-defining illness, they are permanently classified under Stage C/Stage 3, regardless of whether their CD4 count recovers above 200 cells/µL on ART[cite: 1]. Furthermore, CD4 < 200 cells/µL defines AIDS even in a completely asymptomatic individual[cite: 1]!

🧬 Section III: Virology & The 7-Step HIV Replication Cycle

🔬 Etiologic Agent & Virion Morphology

HIV belongs to the family Retroviridae, subfamily Lentivirinae[cite: 1]. It is an enveloped, single-stranded, positive-sense RNA ($ssRNA$) virus with an icosahedral nucleocapsid surrounded by a host-derived lipid bilayer containing viral envelope spikes[cite: 1].

  • gp120 (External Envelope Glycoprotein): Binds host cell CD4 receptor and chemokine co-receptors (CCR5 or CXCR4)[cite: 1].
  • gp41 (Transmembrane Glycoprotein): Mediates viral envelope fusion with host cell plasma membrane[cite: 1].
  • p24 (Capsid Protein): Major structural protein forming the inner core; primary viral antigen detected in 4th-generation assays[cite: 1].
  • Viral Enzymes: Reverse Transcriptase (RT), Integrase, Protease[cite: 1].
  • HIV-1 vs HIV-2: HIV-1 is the primary global cause of the AIDS pandemic (Group M responsible for >90% cases)[cite: 1]. HIV-2 originated in West Africa, is less transmissible, and has lower viral loads[cite: 1].

🔬 Why Receptive Anal Risk is Highest

  • Rectal mucosa is a single layer of thin, fragile columnar epithelium with abundant target cells (Langerhans and CD4 T-cells)[cite: 1].
  • High frequency of mucosal microtrauma during anal intercourse[cite: 1].
  • In contrast, vaginal epithelium is thick, stratified non-keratinized squamous tissue offering a stronger physical barrier[cite: 1].

🛡️ Factors Reducing Transmission

  • Undetectable Viral Load (<20 copies/mL): U=U eliminates sexual transmission risk completely[cite: 1].
  • Male Circumcision: Reduces male HIV acquisition risk by removing the inner foreskin, which is enriched with Langerhans cells and susceptible to microtrauma[cite: 1].
  • Non-infectious Fluids: Saliva, tears, sweat, urine contain natural antiviral factors and DO NOT transmit HIV[cite: 1].

🌐 Epidemiology: Global vs Philippines

⚠️
Epidemiologic Contrast for Boards: While global new HIV infections and AIDS-related deaths are dropping significantly due to expanded ART coverage, the Philippines is experiencing one of the fastest-growing HIV epidemics in the Asia-Pacific region, driven primarily by male-to-male sexual transmission and key populations[cite: 1].

⚡ Section VI: HIV Pathogenesis & Integrated Model

📈 The Natural History Timeline

HIV progression involves precise phases: Primary Infection → Eclipse Phase → Acute Retroviral Syndrome → Clinical Latency → Advanced HIV / AIDS[cite: 1].

Figure 10: Classic Natural History Dynamics of Untreated HIV Infection
--- CD4 Count (cells/µL)
- - Plasma HIV RNA (Viral Load)
Key Feature: Host cytotoxic CD8+ T-cell response drops peak primary viremia down to the "Viral Set Point"[cite: 1].

🦠 Early Target: GALT & Microbial Translocation

Gut-Associated Lymphoid Tissue (GALT) contains over 60% of body lymphocytes, predominantly memory CD4+ Th17 cells[cite: 1].

  1. Rapid HIV infection causes massive, irreversible destruction of mucosal GALT Th17 cells during the acute phase[cite: 1].
  2. Breakdown of gut epithelial mucosal integrity allows intestinal microbial products (e.g., Lipopolysaccharide / LPS) to leak into circulation[cite: 1].
  3. Microbial translocation drives persistent, systemic chronic immune activation, lymphocyte turnover, and immune exhaustion[cite: 1].

Viral Load Low to moderate[cite: 1] Undetectable without ART[cite: 1]
Mechanism Heterogeneous host factors[cite: 1] Potent CD8+ CTL, HLA-B*57:01 / B*27:05[cite: 1]
Latent Reservoir Persists[cite: 1] Persists (not cured!)[cite: 1]

💡 CD4 Count vs HIV RNA (Viral Load)

  • CD4+ T-Cell Count: Reflects current immune status and opportunistic infection risk[cite: 1]. Drives decision for OI prophylaxis[cite: 1].
  • Plasma HIV RNA: Reflects active viral replication rate ("Virologic Thermometer")[cite: 1]. Primary marker for monitoring ART efficacy[cite: 1].

🩺 Essential Baseline Workup Before ART

  • HIV Viral Load & CD4 Count[cite: 1]
  • Baseline HIV Genotypic Resistance Testing[cite: 1]
  • Hepatitis B (HBsAg) & Hep C Screening: If HBV co-infected, ART must contain 2 drugs active vs both HIV & HBV (Tenofovir + 3TC/FTC)[cite: 1].
  • HLA-B*57:01 Screening: Mandatory before starting Abacavir (ABC) to prevent severe hypersensitivity reactions[cite: 1].
  • CBC, Serum Creatinine/eGFR, LFTs, Syphilis/STI Screening[cite: 1]

🦠 Section VIII: Opportunistic Infections & Malignancies

🫁 1. Respiratory Opportunistic Infections (TB & PJP) ▼

Tuberculosis (Mycobacterium tuberculosis)

  • Clinical: High CD4 → classic upper-lobe apical cavitation[cite: 1]. Low CD4 → extrapulmonary/disseminated TB (30-40% cases)[cite: 1].
  • ART Timing & IRIS:
    • CD4 ≥ 50: Delay ART 2 to 8 weeks after starting anti-TB therapy[cite: 1].
    • CD4 < 50: Start ART ASAP within 2 weeks[cite: 1].
  • LTBI Prophylaxis: Weekly INH + Rifapentine x 12 weeks OR Daily INH + Rifampin x 3 months[cite: 1].

Pneumocystis jirovecii Pneumonia (PJP)

  • Risk & Triad: CD4 < 200 cells/µL[cite: 1]. Subacute dyspnea + dry nonproductive cough + hypoxemia + elevated LDH[cite: 1].
  • CXR: Normal or bilateral diffuse interstitial infiltrates[cite: 1].
  • Prophylaxis & Tx: First-line TMP-SMX (1 DS tab daily)[cite: 1]. Add Adjunctive Steroids if $PaO_2 < 70\text{ mmHg}$ or $A-a\text{ gradient} \ge 35$[cite: 1].

👁️ 3. Ophthalmic & Skin/Malignancies (CMV, Kaposi, HPV) ▼

CMV Retinitis

Occurs at CD4 < 50 cells/µL[cite: 1]. Painless progressive vision loss with characteristic fundoscopic "Pizza pie" appearance (perivascular hemorrhages + white retinal exudates/necrosis)[cite: 1]. Tx: Oral Valganciclovir[cite: 1].

Kaposi Sarcoma (HHV-8)

Angioproliferative neoplasm caused by HHV-8 presenting with red-purple violaceous macules, papules, or nodules on skin, oral mucosa, GI tract, or lungs[cite: 1]. Initial Tx: ART induction[cite: 1].

💊 Section IX: Antiretroviral Therapy (ART)

🚀 Indications & Universal Goals of ART

ART is recommended for ALL individuals with confirmed HIV infection, regardless of CD4+ T-cell count[cite: 1]. Primary goal: Durable suppression of plasma HIV RNA below detection limit (<20 copies/mL)[cite: 1].

💊 Major Antiretroviral Drug Classes

Drug Class Mechanism of Action Key Representative Agents
NRTIs Competitive inhibition of Reverse Transcriptase; chain termination[cite: 1] Tenofovir (TDF/TAF), Lamivudine (3TC), Emtricitabine (FTC), Abacavir (ABC)[cite: 1]
NNRTIs Allosteric non-competitive inhibition of Reverse Transcriptase[cite: 1] Efavirenz (EFV), Rilpivirine (RPV), Doravirine (DOR)[cite: 1]
INSTIs Inhibits viral strand transfer into host chromosome[cite: 1] Dolutegravir (DTG), Bictegravir (BIC), Raltegravir (RAL)[cite: 1]
Protease Inhibitors Inhibits cleavage of Gag-Pol precursor polyproteins during maturation[cite: 1] Darunavir (DRV), Atazanavir (ATV) boosted with Ritonavir/Cobicistat[cite: 1]
⭐
PHILIPPINE STANDARD FIRST-LINE ART REGIMEN:
LTD Regimen: Lamivudine (3TC) + Tenofovir Disoproxil Fumarate (TDF) + Dolutegravir (DTG)[cite: 1].
Second-generation INSTI-based regimens are preferred globally due to high barrier to resistance, rapid viral suppression, and minimal adverse side effects[cite: 1].

⏱️ Rapid ART Initiation & Exceptions

Start ART within 7 days of diagnosis (or same-day if ready)[cite: 1].

CRITICAL EXCEPTIONS TO DELAY ART:

  • Cryptococcal Meningitis: Delay ART until antifungal therapy is established due to high risk of fatal intracranial IRIS[cite: 1].
  • TB Meningitis: Delay ART due to high risk of severe neuro-IRIS[cite: 1].

⚠️ Special Clinical Situations

  • HBV Co-infection: Must include 2 agents active against both HIV and HBV (Tenofovir + 3TC or FTC)[cite: 1]. Never stop abruptly to prevent acute liver failure flare[cite: 1].
  • Renal Toxicity: TDF can cause proximal renal tubular dysfunction (Fanconi syndrome)[cite: 1]. Monitor eGFR and urinalysis[cite: 1].

🛡️ Section X: HIV Prevention & Public Health

🟢 U = U (Undetectable = Untransmittable)

People living with HIV who achieve and maintain an undetectable viral load (<20 copies/mL) on ART cannot transmit HIV to their sexual partners[cite: 1]. This transforms clinical prevention and stigma reduction[cite: 1].

💊 PrEP vs PEP Strategy

  • Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP): Daily oral TDF/FTC or TAF/FTC taken by HIV-negative individuals at high ongoing risk[cite: 1].
  • Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP): Emergency 28-day 3-drug ART regimen initiated within 72 hours following high-risk exposure[cite: 1].
