Core Definitions & Terminology

BI-RADS

Breast Imaging-Reporting and Data System: Standardized risk-stratification system (Categories 0–6) guiding management from routine screening to biopsy.

ANDI

Aberrations of Normal Development and Involution: Framework conceptualizing benign breast conditions as deviations from normal hormonal physiology.

BCS

Breast Conservation Surgery: 3-part oncologic approach: Wide Local Excision + Axillary Evaluation + Post-op Radiation Therapy.

SLNB

Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsy: Intraoperative identification and harvesting of the primary draining axillary lymph node to avoid complete dissection in node-negative patients.

Surgical Anatomy & Clinical Evaluation

The breast consists of epithelial ducts and lobules embedded in subcutaneous fatty tissue and supported by fibrous Cooper's ligaments, overlying the pectoralis major muscle[cite: 3].

Breast Quadrants & Clock System

Evaluation involves 4 quadrants or the precise Clock Method[cite: 3]:

  • Tail of Spence: Extension into the axilla overlying the insertion of the pectoralis major muscle[cite: 3].
  • Clock Face: e.g., "1 o'clock position, left breast"[cite: 3].
  • Distance Concentric Zones:
    • Zone A: Periareolar (nearest nipple)[cite: 3]
    • Zone B: Middle breast[cite: 3]
    • Zone C: Outer periphery[cite: 3]
  • Example Documentation: "10C" = Upper outer quadrant, 10 o'clock position, outer periphery.

Lymphatic Drainage & Axillary Levels

Axillary nodes receive ~75% of breast lymph; internal mammary nodes receive ~25% (medial quadrant).

AXILLARY LYMPH NODE LEVELS (Ref: Pectoralis Minor)
LEVEL I: Lateral to Pectoralis Minor
(Axillary vein, External mammary, Scapular groups - Most palpable & US detectable)
↓
LEVEL II: Deep / Underneath Pectoralis Minor
(Central & Interpectoral / Rotter's groups)
↓
LEVEL III: Medial to Pectoralis Minor
(Subclavicular group - Non-palpable clinically)
⭐ Must Know: Standard Axillary Lymph Node Dissection (ALND) routinely removes Level I and Level II nodes. Level III nodes are excised only when grossly involved. Internal mammary nodes are NOT routinely removed surgically due to location beneath the chest wall (evaluated via PET-CT).

Clinical History & Physical Assessment

History Clues

  • Onset & Progression: Malignancies progress over months. A mass appearing overnight in a young female is almost always a benign breast cyst.
  • Relation to Menses: Cyclical pain/fullness suggests fibrocystic changes.
  • Systemic ROS (Metastasis): Jaundice/anicteric sclerae (liver), shortness of breath/pleural effusion (lung), severe focal bone pain (bone - most common distant site).
  • OB-Gyne & Family History: Age at menarche, first live birth, menopause, OCP/HRT use, 1st-degree relatives with breast/ovarian cancer.

Physical Exam Steps

  • Positioning: Patient MUST be examined sitting up first (inspection, axillary palpation), then supine.
  • Palpation Techniques: Radial, concentric circles, or vertical strips ("lawnmower"). Never forget the retroareolar area!
  • Mass Features: Size, consistency, mobility, skin dimpling, nipple retraction.
  • Nipple Discharge: Elicit by gentle pressure on the periareolar area.
⚠️ Common Pitfall: Nipple retraction alone without skin involvement or underlying satellite nodules is NOT T4 disease.

Breast Cancer Risk Models & Genetic Testing

Gail Model

Assesses race, age at menarche, age at 1st live birth, prior biopsies, atypical hyperplasia, and 1st-degree relatives.

  • Calculates 5-year and lifetime risk (to age 90).
  • 5-year risk ≥ 1.66%: High Risk → Intensive surveillance / chemoprevention.
  • Limitations: NOT for prior BC, DCIS, LCIS, BRCA carriers, or strong hereditary patterns.

Claus Model

Based purely on family history of breast cancer (maternal & paternal) and age of onset.

  • Best for women with strong family history with unknown BRCA status.
  • Lifetime risk in US: Newborn = 12%, 50yo = 11%, 70yo = 7%.
🧠 Exam Tip - BRCA Testing Indications: Harmful BRCA1/2 mutation in family, Ashkenazi Jewish heritage, personal/family history of BC ≤ 50yo, ovarian cancer, male breast cancer, pancreatic cancer, or high-risk prostate cancer. Chemoprevention options: Tamoxifen or Raloxifene.

Comparison of Biopsy Techniques

Technique Key Advantages Limitations / Pitfalls Primary Indications
Fine Needle Aspiration (FNAB) Easiest, rapid, low morbidity, highly accurate for fluid CANNOT perform IHC / ER/PR/HER2 status! Sampling error. Aspiration of large cysts, staging axillary LNs.
Core Needle Biopsy (CNB) GOLD STANDARD. Preserves tissue architecture for IHC & histology. Complication: Minor bleeding/hematoma. ALL solid suspicious lesions prior to treatment.
Excisional Biopsy Highest diagnostic yield; diagnostic + therapeutic. Invasive; Do NOT perform on BIRADS 4/5 T1/T2 (need mass to monitor neoadjuvant response). Dominant benign lesions (e.g., Fibroadenoma > 2-3cm).
Vacuum-Assisted (Mammotome) Large bore core, removes complete benign lesions up to 3cm. US-guided; ineffective for non-US visible microcalcifications. US-visible benign masses, minimally invasive excision.
Stereotactic Prone Biopsy High accuracy (~99%) for non-palpable microcalcifications. Contraindicated if unable to lie prone or superficial/faint lesions. Exclusively mammographic microcalcifications (e.g., DCIS).

Classification of Benign Breast Diseases & Cancer Risk

Non-Proliferative Lesions

Relative Risk = 1.0
  • Simple Cysts
  • Mild hyperplasia (usual type)
  • Duct ectasia
  • Benign coarse calcifications
  • Fibroadenoma

No increased risk for future breast cancer.

Proliferative Without Atypia

Relative Risk = 1.5 – 2.0
  • Intraductal papilloma (RR 1.0)
  • Sclerosing adenosis (RR 1.0)
  • Moderate or florid hyperplasia
  • Radial & complex sclerosing lesions

Slightly increased risk.

Atypical Hyperplasia

Relative Risk = 4.0 – 5.0
  • Atypical Ductal Hyperplasia (ADH)
  • Atypical Lobular Hyperplasia (ALH)

Requires surgical wide excision to rule out adjacent DCIS, LCIS, or invasive cancer.

Specific Benign Conditions & Clinical Management

1. Lobular Carcinoma in Situ (LCIS) ▼

Definition: Incidental microscopic finding; NCCN classifies as a non-proliferative benign marker of risk.

Cancer Risk: Relative risk 6–12 for developing invasive cancer in either breast (~1% per year).

Management: Surgical excision if diagnosed on core biopsy to rule out concurrent invasive disease. Risk reduction via chemoprophylaxis (Tamoxifen/Raloxifene) and intensive screening (Mammo/US/MRI).

2. Fibroadenoma ▼

Epidemiology: Most common benign tumor in young females (15–25 yrs). Contains epithelial and stromal components.

Characteristics: Firm, smooth, highly mobile ("breast mouse"), non-tender, well-circumscribed.

Management: Observation if small and classic on US. Excision (surgical, vacuum-assisted, cryoablation) if > 2–3 cm, growing, or equivocal.

3. Simple vs Complex Breast Cysts ▼

Simple Cysts: Anechoic, hypoechoic dark lesions with smooth borders on US. Common, fluctuate with menses, can enlarge overnight. Reassurance; aspirate if large and painful.

Complex Cysts: Internal echoes, thick septa, or solid components. Requires FNA/Core biopsy. If aspirate is bloody → send for cytopathology AND perform excisional biopsy.

4. Nipple Discharge Evaluation & Etiologies ▼
Discharge Appearance Primary Etiology Clinical Management
Milky Lactation, prolactinoma, post-lactational Unilateral: None. Bilateral: Check serum prolactin if >2 yrs post-lactation.
Serous, Green, Gray, Brown Fibrocystic changes, duct ectasia Mammogram/US; routine reassurance.
Bloody / Serosanguinous Intraductal Papilloma (most common benign) Mammo/US → Ductography → Major duct excision (microdochectomy).
5. Gynecomastia, Breast Abscess, Mastodynia ▼

Gynecomastia: Male breast tissue enlargement. Causes: Idiopathic, cirrhosis, Klinefelter's, drugs (Spironolactone, Cimetidine, Digoxin, Metoclopramide, Marijuana, Estrogens). Treatment: Rule out cancer, subareolar excision.

Breast Abscess: Common in lactating women (S. aureus). Treatment: I&D or aspiration, antibiotics, continue nursing on contralateral side. Unexplained/non-lactational abscess requires biopsy to rule out Inflammatory Breast Carcinoma!

Mastodynia (Mastalgia): >90% need only education, reassurance, fitting bra, and NSAIDs. Caffeine reduction, Vit E, evening primrose oil (anecdotal). Danazol rarely used due to severe side effects.

Breast Cancer Subtypes & Histology

In Situ Carcinomas

Ductal Carcinoma in Situ (DCIS): Non-invasive (Stage 0, Tis N0 M0) precursor confined within ducts. Presents as microcalcifications on screening mammography. High risk of progression to invasive ductal cancer.

Invasive Histologies

  • Invasive Ductal Carcinoma (IDC): 80% (Most common).
  • Invasive Lobular Carcinoma (ILC): 10% (Second most common; higher rate of multifocality/bilaterality).
  • Medullary (4%), Mucinous (2%), Papillary (2%), Tubular (2%).
  • Paget's Disease of Nipple: Eczematous nipple change; always biopsy to rule out underlying DCIS/IDC.

Anatomic & Prognostic Staging Groups

Anatomic Stage Groups

  • Stage 0: Tis N0 M0
  • Stage IA: T1 N0 M0
  • Stage IB: T0/T1 N1mi M0
  • Stage IIA: T0/T1 N1 M0, T2 N0 M0
  • Stage IIB: T2 N1 M0, T3 N0 M0
  • Stage IIIA: T0-T2 N2 M0, T3 N1/N2 M0
  • Stage IIIB: T4 N0-N2 M0
  • Stage IIIC: Any T N3 M0
  • Stage IV: Any T Any N M1

Prognostic Staging Principles

Combines Anatomic Stage + Biomarkers (ER, PR, HER2) + Histologic Grade (Scarff-Bloom-Richardson / Nottingham Score: G1=3–5, G2=6–7, G3=8–9).

💡 Essential Staging Rule:
• Low Grade + HR Positive + HER2 Positive: DOWNGRADES the stage (e.g., Anatomic IIB → Prognostic IB).
• High Grade (G3) + Triple Negative (HR-, HER2-): UPGRADES the stage (e.g., Anatomic IIB → Prognostic IIIB).

Surgical Procedures & Anatomic Boundaries

Breast Conservation Surgery (BCS)

Must satisfy ALL 3 components: Wide excision (lumpectomy) with negative margins + Axillary LN staging + Post-op Radiation Therapy.

Absolute Contraindications:

  • Diffuse malignant microcalcifications
  • Multifocal disease in multiple quadrants
  • Prior chest wall radiation
  • Contraindication to radiation (Pregnancy, active Systemic Lupus / Scleroderma)
  • Persistently positive surgical margins

Mastectomy & Surgical Flap Boundaries

Modified Radical Mastectomy (MRM): Total mastectomy + ALND (Levels I & II).

Anatomic Boundaries of Mastectomy Flap:

  • Superior: Clavicle / Subclavius muscle
  • Inferior: Rectus muscle / 2 cm below inframammary fold
  • Medial: Sternum
  • Lateral: Anterior border of Latissimus Dorsi muscle
Nerves to Preserve:
1. Thoracodorsal Nerve: Innervates Latissimus dorsi.
2. Long Thoracic Nerve of Bell: Innervates Serratus anterior (Injury causes "Winged Scapula").

Axillary Lymph Node Evaluation & Staging

Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsy (SLNB)

Gold standard for clinically node-negative (cN0) patients to avoid lymphedema. Intraoperative procedure.

Technique: Dual tracer (Blue Dye + Tc-99m Radioactive Colloid).

  • Harvest blue and/or radioactive node → Frozen section.
  • If Negative: STOP (No ALND needed).
  • If Positive: Proceed to full ALND (Levels I & II).

Neoadjuvant vs Adjuvant Systemic Therapy

Neoadjuvant (Pre-operative): MANDATORY first-line for Stage IIIB/C (Inoperable, IBC, T4, N2/N3). Preferred for HER2(+) and Triple Negative Breast Cancer (TNBC) if ≥ cT2 or ≥ cN1.

Adjuvant Regimens:

  • HER2-Negative: ACT (Adriamycin/Doxorubicin, Cyclophosphamide, Taxane).
  • HER2-Positive: ACT + Trastuzumab (+/- Pertuzumab) or TCH.
  • Endocrine Therapy (HR+): Tamoxifen (Premenopausal; check endometrial thickness/DVT) or Aromatase Inhibitors (Postmenopausal; check bone mineral density).

Breast Cancer Management in Pregnancy

Pregnancy accelerates cancer progression; intervention MUST NOT be delayed.

Trimester Surgical Staging & Treatment Adjuvant / Systemic Therapy
1st Trimester Mastectomy + Axillary Staging Adjuvant chemotherapy delayed to 2nd trimester. Radiation & Endocrine postponed postpartum.
2nd Trimester Mastectomy or BCS + Axillary Staging OR Pre-op Chemo Chemotherapy allowed (NO Paclitaxel/Taxanes). Radiation & Endocrine postponed postpartum.
3rd Trimester Mastectomy or BCS + Axillary Staging Adjuvant chemotherapy post-delivery or immediate; Radiation & Endocrine postpartum.
⚠️ STRICT CONTRAINDICATIONS IN PREGNANCY:
1. Blue Dye is STRICTLY CONTRAINDICATED for SLNB at any point in pregnancy (use radioactive tracer alone).
2. CT Scan / Radiocontrast and Radiation Therapy during pregnancy.

Top Board Exam Rules to Remember

  • Gold Standard Biopsy: Core Needle Biopsy (CNB) - needed for IHC (ER/PR/HER2).
  • FNAB Limitation: CANNOT perform IHC/breast panel.
  • BCS Requirement: Lumpectomy + Axillary Staging + Radiation Therapy.
  • LCIS is REMOVED from AJCC 8th Ed TNM: Classified as benign risk factor.
  • Most Common Metastasis Site: Bone (better prognosis than visceral organ metastasis).
  • Stage IIIB / IBC / T4 / N2-N3: Pre-operative Neoadjuvant Systemic Therapy MANDATORY before surgery.
