📌 Scope & Core Objectives

This comprehensive, board-standard reviewer summarizes Acute and Chronic Liver Diseases (Medicine 2, LE 3 Trans 5), integrating Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 22nd Edition. Designed specifically for Philippine Physician Licensure Examination (PLE) & 3rd-year medical school preparation.

💡
4-Question Clinical Framework for Any Liver Presentation:
  1. Is this hepatocellular or cholestatic (or mixed)?
  2. Is it acute (<6 months), chronic (>6 months), or acute-on-chronic?
  3. Is there impaired liver function (synthetic dysfunction, encephalopathy, coagulopathy)?
  4. What is the underlying etiology, and is it reversible?

Section 1: Evaluation of Abnormal Liver Tests
🔬 Biochemical Test Classifications & Clinical Meaning

Liver function tests (LFTs) measure cellular integrity, excretory function, and synthetic capacity:

  • Hepatocellular Injury: ALT and AST elevation. ALT is more liver-specific (AST is also present in heart, skeletal muscle, RBCs, kidney).
  • Cholestatic Pattern: Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP) and Gamma-Glutamyl Transferase (GGT). Elevated ALP + GGT confirms hepatic origin (rules out bone/placental ALP).
  • Excretory/Conjugating Function: Direct (conjugated) vs. Indirect (unconjugated) Bilirubin.
  • Synthetic Function: Albumin (chronic indicator, half-life ~20 days) and Prothrombin Time / INR (acute indicator, short half-life of factor VII ~6 hours).
🧮 The R-Ratio (Drug-Induced Liver Injury Classification)

Used to objective categorize liver injury patterns, especially in suspected Drug-Induced Liver Injury (DILI):

R = (ALT / ALT ULN) ÷ (ALP / ALP ULN)
  • R > 5: Hepatocellular Pattern
  • 2 ≤ R ≤ 5: Mixed Pattern
  • R < 2: Cholestatic Pattern
⚠️
Common Exam Pitfall: Always calculate R using multiples of Upper Limit of Normal (ULN), NOT raw values! Example: ALT 72 (ULN 36) → 2x ULN. ALP 129 (ULN 129) → 1x ULN. R = 2/1 = 2 (Mixed Pattern).
⚡ AST vs. ALT Ratios & Diagnostic Clues
  • AST:ALT > 2:1 (with AST <400 IU/L) → Strongly points to Alcohol-Associated Hepatitis. AST is elevated due to pyridoxal phosphate (Vitamin B6) deficiency altering ALT synthesis.
  • AST & ALT > 1,000 to 2,000+ IU/L: Differential diagnosis narrows to 3 main entities:
    1. Ischemic Hepatitis ("Shock Liver" - LDH is also markedly elevated)
    2. Toxin / Medication-induced (e.g., Acetaminophen)
    3. Acute Viral Hepatitis (A, B, C, D, E)
🟡 Bilirubin Handling & Approach to Jaundice

Jaundice becomes clinically apparent when serum bilirubin exceeds 3.0 mg/dL.

  • Unconjugated (Indirect) Hyperbilirubinemia: Increased production (hemolysis) or impaired uptake/conjugation (Gilbert syndrome, Crigler-Najjar). Water-insoluble; does NOT appear in urine.
  • Conjugated (Direct) Hyperbilirubinemia: Impaired excretion or biliary obstruction. Water-soluble; causes dark urine (bilirubinuria) and pale/clay-colored stools in complete obstruction.

⭐
Must Know: Persistence of HBsAg for >6 months defines Chronic Hepatitis B infection. Anti-HBc IgM is the SINGLE key marker during the "Window Period".
💊 Hepatitis C Management Workflow

1. Screen with Anti-HCV antibody (indicates lifetime exposure).

2. If positive, confirm active infection with HCV RNA quantitative test.

3. Treat with Direct-Acting Antivirals (DAAs) such as Sofosbuvir / Velpatasvir.

4. Cure is defined as Sustained Virologic Response (SVR12) = Undetectable HCV RNA 12 weeks after completion of therapy.

Section 3: Acute Liver Failure (ALF)
🚨 Definition & Red Flag Criteria for ALF

Acute Liver Failure is a life-threatening medical emergency defined by:

  1. Acute liver injury with coagulopathy (INR ≥ 1.5 or PT prolonged by >4–6s)
  2. Any degree of Hepatic Encephalopathy (altered mental status)
  3. Occurring in a patient WITHOUT preexisting cirrhosis or established chronic liver disease
🚨
Key ALF Red Flags: Rising INR, hypoglycemia, lactic acidosis, renal failure, and rapid onset of cerebral edema / intracranial hypertension. Urgent transfer to a liver transplant center is mandatory.
💊 Acetaminophen Toxicity & Ischemic Hepatitis
Acetaminophen Overdose Pathway: Early N/V → Latent Period (24-48h) → Massive AST/ALT Elevation (>5000 IU/L) & Coagulopathy/ALF.
Antidote: N-Acetylcysteine (NAC) - restores hepatic glutathione. Most effective when given within 8 hours, but provides benefit even in established ALF!
💡
Ischemic (Hypoxic) Hepatitis: Occurs secondary to shock, cardiac arrest, or severe heart failure. Marked by rapid, extreme AST/ALT & LDH elevation, followed by a dramatic drop once hemodynamic stability is restored.
🧬 Autoimmune Hepatitis & Wilson Disease Presentation
  • Autoimmune Hepatitis (AIH): Female predominance, high total IgG, positive ANA and/or Smooth Muscle Antibodies (SMA). Biopsy shows interface hepatitis. Treated with corticosteroids & azathioprine.
  • Wilson Disease: Autosomal recessive copper overload in young adults. Low serum ceruloplasmin, elevated 24-hr urinary copper, Kayser-Fleischer (KF) corneal rings, Coombs-negative hemolytic anemia accompanying liver failure.

🩸 Esophageal Varices & Acute Variceal Hemorrhage

Primary prophylaxis: Non-selective beta-blockers (Nadolol, Propranolol) or Endoscopic Variceal Ligation (EVL).

🚨
Acute Bleeding Protocol:
  1. Hemodynamic stabilization & Restrictive transfusion strategy (target Hemoglobin 7–8 g/dL to prevent worsening portal pressure).
  2. Vasoactive therapy: Octreotide or Terlipressin.
  3. Prophylactic Antibiotics: Ceftriaxone IV (reduces mortality and rebleeding).
  4. Urgent Endoscopy with Band Ligation within 12 hours.
🌊 Ascites & Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP)

Diagnosis of SBP requires diagnostic paracentesis displaying Ascitic Absolute PMN count ≥ 250 cells/mm³.

  • Treatment: 3rd Generation Cephalosporin (Ceftriaxone IV).
  • Prevention of Hepatorenal Syndrome: IV Albumin administration (1.5 g/kg at diagnosis, 1.0 g/kg on day 3) in high-risk patients.
🧠 Hepatic Encephalopathy (HE) & Hepatorenal Syndrome (HRS)
  • HE Triggers: GI bleeding (protein load), infection/SBP, constipation, hypokalemia, overdiuresis, sedatives.
  • HE Management: First line: Lactulose (titrated to 2–3 soft stools/day) + Rifaximin (minimally absorbed antibiotic reducing gut ammonia production). Do NOT restrict dietary protein! Target 1.2–1.5 g/kg/day protein.
  • HRS Definition: Functional renal failure due to severe renal vasoconstriction. Managed with Albumin + Terlipressin/Norepinephrine. Definitive cure is Liver Transplantation.
🎗️ Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC) Surveillance

Surveillance is required every 6 months with Abdominal Ultrasound (+/- Alpha-fetoprotein / AFP) for all cirrhosis patients and high-risk chronic HBV carriers.

Diagnosis is made by multiphasic CT/MRI showing classic arterial hyperenhancement with venous washout (biopsy is usually unnecessary).
