Abdominal Wall Hernias, Omentum, Mesentery & Retroperitoneum

Based on 2027 Trans, Dr. Napoleon B. Alcedo Jr.

Overview & Core Concepts

⭐ Core Definition: A hernia is defined as a protrusion of a viscus through an opening in the wall of a cavity in which it is contained[cite: 1]. The term is derived from the Latin word for "rupture"[cite: 1].

Inguinal hernias are the most common abdominal wall hernias (accounting for almost 75%), with a lifetime risk of 27% in men and 3% in women[cite: 1]. Groin hernias include both inguinal and femoral hernias[cite: 1].

Type of External Hernia Incidence Location Notes
Inguinal hernia 80% (Most common) Occurs around the groin area[cite: 1]. Incidence is 12x more common in males[cite: 1].
Incisional hernia 10% Develops in sites of previous abdominal incisions[cite: 1].
Femoral hernia 5% Occurs around the groin area, specifically below the inguinal ligament[cite: 1]. High risk of strangulation[cite: 1].
Umbilical hernia 4% Congenital or acquired due to increased intra-abdominal pressure[cite: 1].
Epigastric hernia <1% Located between umbilicus and xiphoid[cite: 1].

Anatomy of the Abdominal Wall

From outermost to innermost layers[cite: 1]:

  1. Skin[cite: 1]
  2. Subcutaneous tissue[cite: 1]
  3. Abdominal muscles (3 layers):
    • External oblique (outermost)[cite: 1]
    • Internal oblique (middle)[cite: 1]
    • Transversus abdominis (innermost)[cite: 1]
  4. Transversalis fascia[cite: 1]
  5. Peritoneum (inner lining of the abdominal cavity)[cite: 1]
  6. Small intestines / Viscera[cite: 1]
💡 Epidemiology Pearl: The incidence of inguinal hernia has a bimodal distribution with peaks before one year of age and then again after 40 years of age[cite: 1].

Inguinal Hernias: Anatomy & Pathophysiology

Inguinal Canal Anatomy

The inguinal canal is a crucial 4-6cm passage in the lower abdominal wall serving as a conduit for the spermatic cord in males and the round ligament in females[cite: 1].

  • Anterior boundary: External oblique aponeurosis[cite: 1]. (First major structure encountered in anterior approach surgeries[cite: 1]).
  • Posterior boundary: Transversalis fascia & Conjoint tendon[cite: 1]. (Often referred to as the floor by surgeons[cite: 1]).
  • Floor: Inguinal ligament[cite: 1].
Hesselbach's Triangle (Inguinal Triangle)

An area of weakness in the anterior abdominal wall where direct inguinal hernias pass[cite: 1].

  • Base: Inguinal ligament[cite: 1].
  • Lateral border: Inferior epigastric vessels[cite: 1].
  • Medial border: Lateral border of the rectus abdominis muscle[cite: 1].
🧠 Exam Tip: The Inferior Epigastric Artery is the key landmark separating direct from indirect hernias. Indirect hernias are LATERAL to it, direct hernias are MEDIAL to it[cite: 1].
Pathophysiology & Etiology

Pediatric (Indirect Hernia): It is a developmental defect caused by the failure of the processus vaginalis to close[cite: 1]. If it fails to close completely, abdominal contents can descend, causing a scrotal hernia[cite: 1]. If minimally patent, it may just allow fluid, causing a hydrocele[cite: 1].

Adults (Acquired): Caused by a combination of weakness in the abdominal wall (aging, previous surgery, genetics) and increased intra-abdominal pressure[cite: 1]. Precipitating factors include straining, chronic cough, heavy lifting, obesity, pregnancy, and ascites[cite: 1]. Loss of collagen integrity (e.g., from smoking) is also a factor[cite: 1].

Clinical Presentation & Diagnosis

Common symptoms include a visible groin lump, heaviness/discomfort, pain on exertion, and enlargement of the lump with coughing[cite: 1].

⚠️ Common Pitfall: Do not just evaluate the hernia. In adults presenting with late-onset hernias, always evaluate the GI tract (e.g., rectal cancer causing constipation), urinary tract (BPH causing straining), and pulmonary function (COPD causing chronic cough)[cite: 1]. You must treat the underlying cause of increased abdominal pressure first[cite: 1].

Physical Examination: The most important diagnostic tool. A good clinician can diagnose without imaging[cite: 1].

  • Insert the index finger through the scrotum towards the external inguinal ring and ask the patient to cough/Valsalva[cite: 1].
  • If you feel the pulse at the tip of your finger = Indirect Hernia[cite: 1].
  • If you feel the pulse at the base/side of your finger = Direct Hernia[cite: 1].

Imaging: Used only when diagnosis is ambiguous (e.g., obese patients, irreducible mass)[cite: 1].

  • Ultrasound: Cheapest, dynamic (shows peristalsis), no radiation[cite: 1]. Can have false positives in thin individuals[cite: 1].
  • CT Scan / MRI: Static images. MRI is most sensitive and specific (95%, 96%) but expensive and time-consuming[cite: 1].

Treatment Options for Inguinal Hernias

Hernias are not an emergency unless complicated by incarceration or strangulation[cite: 1]. Surgery is the definitive treatment[cite: 1].

Approach Techniques Indications & Notes
Watchful Waiting Observation For asymptomatic patients[cite: 1]. Risk of incarceration is low[cite: 1].
Taxis (Manual Reduction) Analgesics, light sedatives (Diazepam), Trendelenburg position Attempted for incarcerated hernias WITHOUT sequelae of strangulation[cite: 1]. Never do this if strangulation is suspected[cite: 1].
Open Tissue Repair Bassini, Shouldice, McVay, Desarda Used when prosthetic material cannot be used safely due to infection or fecal contamination[cite: 1]. Shouldice has the least recurrence (1%) among tissue repairs[cite: 1].
Open Prosthetic (Mesh) Lichtenstein (Gold Standard), Plug and Patch Tension-free. Lichtenstein has lowest recurrence rate (~0.2%)[cite: 1]. Mesh reinforces the floor of Hesselbach's triangle[cite: 1].
Laparoscopic Repair TAPP, TEP, IPOM Minimizes recovery time[cite: 1]. Superior for recurrent or bilateral hernias[cite: 1]. Requires general anesthesia[cite: 1].

Key Surgical Principles

The repair of inguinal hernias has two primary aspects[cite: 1]:

  1. High ligation of the sac (at the level of internal oblique muscle before it becomes cremaster muscle)[cite: 1].
  2. Strengthening of the floor (posterior part of Hesselbach's triangle) via tissue or mesh[cite: 1].
⭐ TAPP vs TEP:
- TAPP (Transabdominal Preperitoneal): Enters the intra-abdominal cavity[cite: 1]. Associated with higher incidence of ileus because the peritoneal cavity is entered[cite: 1].
- TEP (Totally Extraperitoneal): Accesses preperitoneal space without entering the intraperitoneal cavity, usually via balloon dissection[cite: 1]. Minimizes injury to intra-abdominal organs[cite: 1].

Complications

  • Acute: Infection, Urinary retention (common in elderly with BPH; trial voiding, insert catheter)[cite: 1]. Hematoma/Seroma (observe, do NOT aspirate seromas unless severely symptomatic to avoid abscess)[cite: 1].
  • Chronic: Post-herniorrhaphy inguinodynia (chronic pain as high as 63%)[cite: 1]. Can be nociceptive, neuropathic (nerve entrapment), or visceral[cite: 1].
    • Nerve entrapment risk: Ilioinguinal and iliohypogastric (anterior repair), Genitofemoral and lateral femoral cutaneous (endoscopic)[cite: 1].
  • Vascular/Visceral (Lap specific): Iliac/femoral vessel injury is most severe (exsanguination)[cite: 1]. Inferior epigastric and external iliac arteries most commonly injured[cite: 1].
  • Testicular Atrophy/Ischemic Orchitis: Caused by injury to the pampiniform plexus rather than the testicular artery[cite: 1]. Do not deliver the testis out of the scrotum to prevent scrotal artery severing[cite: 1].

Abdominal Wall & Incisions

The goal of an efficacious incision is to provide adequate exposure with minimal perturbation of abdominal wall function[cite: 1].

  • Longitudinal (Midline): Most common[cite: 1].
  • Transverse/Oblique: Associated with lower incisional hernia rates but higher rates of wound infection[cite: 1].

Congenital Anomalies

Condition Pathology Covering
Omphalocele Failure of midgut to reenter abdomen at 12 wks AOG. Protrudes through umbilicus[cite: 1]. Covered with amniotic/peritoneal membrane[cite: 1].
Gastroschisis Viscera protrudes through a defect lateral to umbilicus (usually right)[cite: 1]. WITHOUT amniotic sac covering[cite: 1].

Acquired Abnormalities

  • Epigastric Hernias: Between umbilicus and xiphoid. Contains omentum or falciform ligament[cite: 1].
  • Umbilical Hernias: Congenital usually closes by 5 yrs[cite: 1]. Acquired adult form more common in females (pregnancy, obesity, ascites)[cite: 1].
  • Spigelian Hernias: Occurs along the arcuate line due to lack of posterior rectus sheath below it[cite: 1]. High incarceration rate (20%)[cite: 1].
  • Incisional Hernias: 20% of midline incisions develop a hernia. Sublay mesh technique is recommended. Simple suture repair has a 54% recurrence rate (unacceptable tension)[cite: 1].
⚠️ Rectus Abdominis Diastasis: Abnormal separation (>2cm) of rectus muscles[cite: 1]. It is NOT a true hernia because the midline fascia is intact[cite: 1]. Incarceration/strangulation do not occur[cite: 1]. Does not require surgery; improves with weight loss/exercise[cite: 1].

Rectus Sheath Hematoma

Disruption of the inferior epigastric artery, usually around the arcuate line[cite: 1]. Causes include trauma, vigorous coughing/sneezing[cite: 1].

Fothergill's Sign: A palpable abdominal mass that does NOT change with contraction of the rectus abdominis muscle[cite: 1].

Desmoid Tumors (Aggressive Fibromatosis)

Fibroblastic neoplasia with aggressive behavior but NO metastatic potential[cite: 1]. Gold standard treatment: margin-negative resection with immediate mesh reconstruction[cite: 1].

Omentum

A fibrous adipose apron. The Greater Omentum acts as the "Policeman of the abdominal cavity" (peritoneal defense organ)[cite: 1]. It migrates to areas of inflammation and forms a fibrin bridge to minimize risk of spread/perforation[cite: 1].

  • Graham Patch Repair: Uses this principle. A small omental patch is used to cover perforated ulcers in the stomach/duodenum[cite: 1].
  • Omental Infarction: Presents with right-sided abdominal pain/mass. CT shows streaking whirling pattern of fat[cite: 1].

Mesentery

Suspends bowel and houses neurovascular/lymphatic structures[cite: 1].

  • Sclerosing Mesenteritis: Idiopathic fibrosis. Diagnosed via CT (Fat ring sign - preserved fat near vessels crossing fibrosis). Aggressive surgery NOT indicated; use steroids/hormonal therapy[cite: 1].
  • Mesenteric Cyst: Fluid-filled mass. Tillaux's Sign: Mass is mobile only laterally (unlike omental cysts which move in all directions)[cite: 1].
  • Mesenteric Tumors: Lymphoma is the most common (appears as bulky lymphadenopathy, requires biopsy only, no resection)[cite: 1].
⚠️ Mesenteric Surgery Pitfall: When treating mesenteric problems, be cautious not to resect too much mesentery, as it contains the blood supply for the bowel. Excessive resection causes bowel ischemia and Short Bowel Syndrome[cite: 1].

Retroperitoneum

Bound anteriorly by peritoneum, posteriorly by iliopsoas/lumbar muscles[cite: 1]. Contains three spaces: Anterior pararenal, Perirenal, Posterior pararenal[cite: 1].

  • Retroperitoneal Infections: Can present with flank pain and erythema of umbilicus or flank (Grey Turner Sign)[cite: 1]. High morbidity (25%) due to delayed diagnosis. Treat with source control (drainage) and antibiotics[cite: 1].
  • Retroperitoneal Fibrosis: Chronic periaortitis causing encasement of ureters/IVC/aorta. Bilateral in 70%[cite: 1]. Presents with back pain, decreased urine output, or hydrocele/varicocele. Treat by relieving obstruction (medical or surgical lysis)[cite: 1].

High-Yield Comparisons

1. Direct vs. Indirect Inguinal Hernia[cite: 1]

Feature Indirect Inguinal Hernia Direct Inguinal Hernia
Prevalence Most common type Less common
Pathophysiology Patent processus vaginalis / congenital defect Weakness in the floor of the inguinal canal (Hesselbach's)
Landmark (Inf. Epigastric Artery) LATERAL to inferior epigastric artery MEDIAL to inferior epigastric artery
Route Passes through BOTH deep and superficial rings. Can enter scrotum. Passes ONLY through superficial ring. Does not usually enter scrotum.
Finger Test (Valsalva) Pulse felt at the TIP of the finger Pulse felt at the BASE / SIDE of the finger

2. Hernia Repair Techniques[cite: 1]

Category Specific Repair Key Characteristics
Tissue Repair (No mesh. Used for infected fields) Bassini Sutures conjoint tendon to inguinal ligament.
Shouldice Multi-layer native tissue repair. Least recurrence (1%) among tissue repairs.
McVay (Cooper's) Sutures transversalis fascia to Cooper's ligament.
Desarda Uses living flap of external oblique aponeurosis.
Prosthetic (Anterior) Lichtenstein Gold standard. Tension-free polypropylene mesh. ~0.2% recurrence.
Plug & Patch Cone-shaped mesh plug in defect + onlay patch.
Laparoscopic (Posterior) TAPP Enters abdominal cavity. Higher risk of ileus/visceral injury.
TEP Stays extraperitoneal (balloon dissection). Preferred to avoid visceral injury.
IPOM Mesh is in direct contact with bowel; requires expensive anti-adhesion mesh.
