Disorders of the Esophagus and Stomach

Comprehensive Interactive Reviewer for PLE (Reference: MED2 - LE 3 - V1)

Overview & Learning Objectives

This reviewer comprehensively covers the structural, motility, inflammatory, infectious, mechanical, and peptic ulcer disorders of the esophagus and stomach.


Major Categories of Esophageal Disease

  • Structural: Hiatal hernia, rings & webs, diverticula, tumors
  • Motility: Achalasia, DES, GERD
  • Inflammatory and Infectious: Eosinophilic Esophagitis (EoE), Candida, HSV, CMV
  • Mechanical Trauma/Iatrogenic: Perforation, Mallory-Weiss, radiation, corrosive, pill, foreign body impaction

Esophageal Anatomy

  • Length: 25cm long muscular hollow tube.
  • Mucosa: Non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium.
  • Submucosa: Glands and connective tissues.
  • Muscularis Propria: Upper 1/3 (skeletal), Middle 1/3 (mixed), Lower 1/3 (smooth).
  • Adventitia: Anchors to mediastinum. No true serosa.

Sphincters

  • UES: Main muscle is Cricopharyngeus. Prevents air entry during breathing.
  • LES: Physiologic high pressure zone at GEJ. Prevents reflux.

Structural Disorders

Hiatal Hernia

Herniation of viscera (usually stomach) into mediastinum via esophageal hiatus.

  • Type I (Sliding): 95% of cases. Cardia & GEJ displaced upwards. If asymptomatic -> no treatment. If reflux -> treat GERD.
  • Type II-IV (Paraesophageal): Fundus herniates. Type II (GEJ fixed), Type III (Combined GEJ + Fundus), Type IV (other viscera). High risk of strangulation/volvulus -> Surgery advocated.

Rings & Webs

  • Schatzki Rings (B Ring): Lower esophagus (<13mm). Associated with hiatal hernia and episodic solid food dysphagia ("Steakhouse syndrome").
  • Esophageal Webs: Upper esophagus, anterior. Intermittent dysphagia to solids.
  • Treatment: Balloon or Savary dilator.

Motility Disorders

Achalasia

Pathology Loss of myenteric plexus ganglion cells -> failure of LES relaxation & loss of peristalsis.

Diagnosis: Barium swallow (Bird's beak), Manometry (most sensitive: impaired LES relaxation + absent peristalsis).

Treatment: Pneumatic dilation, LES Myotomy (Heller, POEM). Pharmacologic (Nitrates/CCB) NOT very effective.

Diffuse Esophageal Spasm (DES)

Abnormal contractions with NORMAL deglutitive LES relaxation. Chest pain + dysphagia.

Diagnosis: 24-hr Manometry (spastic/uncoordinated). Barium: "Corkscrew" appearance.

GERD & Complications

Mechanisms: Transient LES relaxations (most common, 90%), LES hypotension, Hiatal hernia.

Complications: Erosive esophagitis, strictures, Barrett's Esophagus (squamous to columnar metaplasia -> Adenocarcinoma risk).

Treatment: PPIs (Mainstay), Lifestyle changes (weight, diet, elevation), Nissen fundoplication.

Barrett's TX: Endoscopic mucosal ablation (new gold standard) replacing esophagectomy.

Eosinophilic Esophagitis (EoE)

Chronic antigen-mediated disease. History of IgE allergies (eczema, asthma). Dysphagia to solid food, impaction.

Endoscopy: Loss of vascular markings, multiple rings, longitudinal furrows.

Histology: >15 eosinophils per HPF.

Treatment: PPIs, Swallowed topical glucocorticoids (Fluticasone), Elimination diet. Systemic steroids NOT recommended.

Infectious Esophagitis

Usually in immunocompromised (HIV, transplant). Most characteristic symptom: Odynophagia.

Mechanical Trauma & Iatrogenic Injury

  • Esophageal Perforation:
    Causes: Iatrogenic (endoscopy/dilation), Boerhaave's (retching/spontaneous rupture at GEJ).
    Exam Tip Mediastinitis Triad = Chest pain, vomiting, subcutaneous emphysema. Dx: CXR/CT (pneumomediastinum), water-soluble contrast.
  • Mallory-Weiss Tear: Non-transmural tear at GEJ from retching. Causes hematemesis. Resolves spontaneously.
  • Corrosive Esophagitis: Alkali > Acid. Early EGD (24-48 hrs) required. DO NOT scope after 48 hrs due to perforation risk.
  • Pill Esophagitis: Antibiotics (Tetracycline), NSAIDs, FeSO4 lodging in mid-esophagus. Self-limiting.

Peptic Ulcer Disease (PUD)

Disruption of mucosal integrity leading to local defect due to active inflammation. Imbalance of aggressive (Acid, Pepsin, H. pylori, NSAIDs) vs defensive (Mucus, Bicarb, Blood flow, Prostaglandins) factors.

H. Pylori

Gram-neg, spiral. #1 risk factor. 90% of DU, 70-90% of GU. Causes MALT Lymphoma & AdenoCA. Dx: Rapid urease test, Urea Breath Test, Stool Antigen.

NSAIDs

Inhibit COX-1 & COX-2 -> block prostaglandin production -> impaired mucosal defense. Tx: Stop NSAID or add PPI/Misoprostol.


Complications

  • Bleeding: Most common (15%). Melena/hematochezia.
  • Perforation: 2nd most common. Triad: acute abdominal pain, tachycardia, abdominal rigidity. Needs surgery.
  • Gastric Outlet Obstruction: Edema/scarring at pylorus. Early satiety, vomiting.

Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome (ZES)

Marked acid hypersecretion from gastrinoma (pancreas/duodenum). Rare (0.1-1% of PUD). PUD in atypical sites, refractory. 70% have diarrhea.

Pearl PUD + Diarrhea = Think ZES.

Diagnosis: Fasting gastrin >150-200 pg/mL -> BAO >15 mEq/hr -> Secretin Stimulation Test (highly sensitive/specific, gastrin inc >120pg).

Imaging: PET-CT with 68Ga-DOTATATE (functional), EUS (highest sensitivity for primary).

Stress-Related Mucosal Injury (SRMI)

Acute erosions/ulcers in fundus/body/proximal duodenum in severe physiologic stress (shock, sepsis, burns, trauma).

  • Cushing's ulcer: Head trauma.
  • Curling's ulcer: Severe burns.
  • Prophylaxis/Tx: PPIs are drug of choice in ICU.

Self-Assessment (50 MCQs)

Cram Sheet (Ultra-Condensed)

  • Anatomy: Upper 1/3 (Skeletal), Mid 1/3 (Mixed), Lower 1/3 (Smooth).
  • Schatzki Ring: "Steakhouse syndrome", intermittent solid dysphagia, <13mm.
  • Zenker's Diverticulum: False diverticulum at Killian's triangle (above UES).
  • Achalasia: Bird's beak, impaired LES relaxation, absent peristalsis. TX: Pneumatic dilation, Heller myotomy, POEM.
  • DES: Corkscrew esophagus, uncoordinated spastic contractions, NORMAL LES relaxation.
  • EoE: >15 eosinophils/HPF. Atopy history. TX: Fluticasone (topical), PPI, elimination diet. NOT systemic steroids.
  • Infectious Esophagitis: Candida (white plaques, Fluconazole), HSV-1 (vesicles, ground glass), CMV (serpiginous ulcers, owl's eye).
  • Boerhaave vs Mallory-Weiss: Boerhaave = Transmural rupture (Mediastinitis). M-W = Non-transmural mucosal tear (Hematemesis).
  • Caustic Ingestion: Early EGD within 24-48 hrs. NEVER >48 hrs due to fragilty.
  • H. pylori: 90% duodenal ulcers. 70-90% gastric. MALT lymphoma / AdenoCA risk.
  • NSAIDs: Block COX1/2 -> decrease prostaglandins.
  • Gastric Ulcers: Normal/Low acid. High malignancy risk -> ALWAYS biopsy. Repeat EGD in 8-12 weeks.
  • Duodenal Ulcers: High acid. Low malignancy risk -> Biopsy not needed.
  • Zollinger-Ellison (ZES): Gastrinoma. High gastrin + BAO >15. Secretin stim test (+). Diarrhea is common.
  • Forrest Classification: Ia (Spurting, highest rebleed 60-100%), III (Clean base, lowest 3-5%).
