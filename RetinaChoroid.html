1. Vitreous Humour Anatomy & Physiology

The vitreous body occupies 80% of total ocular volume and consists of a clear, solid gel that undergoes gradual liquefaction and collapse with aging (synchysis senilis).

Composition (99% Water, 1% Solids)

  • Collagen Fibrils: Provides structural matrix.
  • Hyaluronic Acid Molecules: Retains water molecules.
  • Hyalocytes: Mononuclear vitreous cells.

Topographic Division & Borders

  • Central Core: Central vitreous.
  • Cortical Vitreous: Peripheral outer shell.
  • Anterior Border: Retrolental space (behind lens).
  • Posterior Border: Posterior hyaloid adjacent to ILM (Vitreoretinal Interface).
⭐ Vitreous Attachment Sites (Strongest to Weakest)
  1. Vitreous Base (Ora Serrata): Strongest attachment point anteriorly.
  2. Optic Nerve Disc Margin
  3. Macula (Posterior Pole)
  4. Retinal Blood Vessels
💡 Primary Functions of the Vitreous

1. Ocular Shape Maintenance: Holds spherical globe form.
2. Visual Axis Clarity: Allows unhindered light transmission to retina.
3. Retinal Attachment Support: Internal pressure holds retina against Bruch's membrane & choroid.
4. Shock Absorber: Dampens blunt mechanical trauma to the globe.

2. Retina: Structural Architecture & The 10 Layers

The retina is the light-sensitive innermost neural layer continuous posteriorly with the optic nerve. Its dual blood supply consists of the Central Retinal Artery (supplying inner 2/3 layers) and the Ciliary Arteries/Choroid (supplying outer 1/3 layers: OPL, ONL, Photoreceptors, RPE).

Interactive Diagram: The 10 Layers of the Neurosensory Retina & RPE

Organized from Internal (Vitreous side) to External (Choroid side)

1. Internal Limiting Membrane (ILM)Boundary with vitreous hyaloid
2. Nerve Fiber Layer (NFL)Axons of Ganglion Cells → Optic Nerve
3. Ganglion Cell Layer (GCL)Cell bodies of Ganglion neurons
4. Inner Plexiform Layer (IPL)Synapses: Bipolar + Amacrine + Ganglion
5. Inner Nuclear Layer (INL)Bodies: Bipolar, Horizontal, Amacrine, Müller
6. Outer Plexiform Layer (OPL)Synapses: Photoreceptors + Bipolar + Horizontal
7. Outer Nuclear Layer (ONL)Cell bodies of Rods & Cones
8. External Limiting Membrane (ELM)Junctions between Photoreceptors & Müller cells
9. Photoreceptor Layer (Rods & Cones)Inner & Outer segments (Transduce light)
10. Retinal Pigment Epithelium (RPE)Monolayer, Outer Blood-Retina Barrier
--- Bruch's Membrane & Choroid ---Vascular coat (Ciliary arteries)
Clinical Significance: OCT uses light reflectivity variations to distinguish these exact 10 bands. Plexiform/fibrous layers (IPL, OPL, NFL) appear hyperreflective (bright), whereas nuclear layers (GCL, INL, ONL) appear hyporeflective (dark).
🧠 The 3-Neuron Relay System

Phototransduction light signal pathway:

Photoreceptors (ONL) → Hyperpolarize
⬇️
Bipolar Cells (INL) → Polarize
⬇️
Ganglion Cells (GCL) → Axons form NFL/Optic Nerve to Occipital Brain

⭐ Structural & Metabolic Support

Müller Cells: Span the entire thickness across all 10 retinal layers. Serve as the primary structural "scaffolding" and metabolic support network.

Subretinal Space: A potential space between Photoreceptors and RPE (site of Retinal Detachment).

3. Retinal Pigment Epithelium (RPE) & Choroid

Retinal Pigment Epithelium (RPE) - Deep Dive ▼
  • Outer Blood-Retina Barrier: Formed by tight junctions (zonula occludens) between RPE cells adjacent to Bruch's membrane.
  • Subretinal Space Dehydration: RPE actively pumps fluid and ions out of the subretinal space into the choroid. This creates a "suction effect" holding the neurosensory retina attached to Bruch's membrane without physical structural anatomical ties.
  • Metabolic Support: Serves the outer third of retinal layers (OPL, ONL, Photoreceptor layer). Recycles retinol (light transduction byproducts) and phagocytoses shed photoreceptor outer segment discs.
Choroid Physiology ▼
  • Vascular Coat: Middle coat of the eye wall originating from short and long posterior ciliary arteries. Arranged in lobules.
  • Bruch's Membrane: Extracellular matrix layer separating RPE from the choriocapillaris.
  • Nourishment: Delivers oxygen and nutrient supply to outer retinal layers, RPE, and the avascular foveal macula.

Diagnostic & Imaging Modalities Summary

Comprehensive evaluation requires systematic visual testing, clinical ophthalmoscopy, and cross-sectional or angiographic imaging.

Diagnostic Tool Field / View Scope Dimensionality & Optics Key Clinical Indications & Features
Visual Acuity (Snellen / Bailey-Lovie) Central visual function Functional rating (20/20 baseline) Bailey-Lovie chart preferred over Snellen due to standardized letter spacing/sizing.
Direct Ophthalmoscopy Monocular, central 15° restricted view 2D view (No depth perception) Upright image, high magnification. Sees disc, proximal vessels, macula. Unsuitable for peripheral retina.
Slit-lamp Biomicroscopy Posterior pole (with 70D or 90D condensing lens) Stereoscopic 3D view Magnification can be dynamically adjusted using slit lamp controls. Excellent for macular edema & vitreous.
Indirect Ophthalmoscopy Widest field view (Posterior pole + far periphery) Stereoscopic 3D inverted image Headgear + 20D condensing lens. Patient looks in 8 cardinal directions to inspect peripheral retinal breaks.
Fluorescein Angiography (FA) Retinal vasculature dynamic map 2D Blue-light excitation fundus photo IV sodium fluorescein dye. Shows hyperfluorescence (leakage/edema/MA) & hypofluorescence (ischemia/hemorrhage block).
Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT) Cross-sectional foveal & macular architecture High-resolution optical cross-section Near-IR laser beam. In vivo histology. Measures thickness, fluid (DME/SRF), and layer integrity.
B-Scan Ultrasonography 2D acoustic cross-section of posterior segment Sound waves (10 MHz) Indicated when posterior pole view is completely blocked by media opacities (e.g. dense Vitreous Hemorrhage).

OCT Optical Reflectivity Breakdown

OCT constructs images based on varying light reflectance indexes of individual layers:

⭐ Hyperreflective (Bright / White Bands)

Fibrous or Plexiform layers + RPE:

  • Retinal Nerve Fiber Layer (RNFL)
  • Inner Plexiform Layer (IPL)
  • Outer Plexiform Layer (OPL)
  • RPE Complex: Highly hyperreflective due to uniform cellular density.
  • Hard Exudates / Laser Scars / Hemorrhages
⚠️ Hyporeflective (Dark Bands)

Cellular / Nuclear layers + Fluid:

  • Ganglion Cell Layer (GCL)
  • Inner Nuclear Layer (INL)
  • Outer Nuclear Layer (ONL)
  • Subretinal Fluid / Cystoid Macular Edema: Fluid creates hyporeflective dark spaces.

Diabetic Retinopathy (DR) - Epidemiology & Pathogenesis

Diabetic Retinopathy is a leading cause of blindness among working-class adults. Primary cause: Chronic Hyperglycemia causing loss of capillary pericytes, endothelial damage, and capillary leakage leading to inner Blood-Retinal Barrier breakdown.

💡 Comprehensive DR Risk Factors
  • Duration of DM: Single most consistent relationship; frequency & severity increase with years of DM.
  • Age at Diagnosis: Younger age at diagnosis of Type 2 DM = significantly higher risk. (No age association in Type 1 DM).
  • Glycemic Control: Indirect relationship; strict HbA1c control (<7%) prevents development/progression.
  • Hypertension & Serum Lipids: High lipids strongly correlate with retinal hard exudates (lipoproteins) and Diabetic Macular Edema (DME).
  • Nephropathy & Anemia: Persistent microalbuminuria & low baseline hematocrit (low O2 capacity) worsen ischemia.
  • Pregnancy: High estrogen levels accelerate DR rapidly.
⚠️ Common Pitfall: Platelet Count vs DR

According to the Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS), baseline platelet counts and aspirin therapy have NO effect on the development or progression of Diabetic Retinopathy. Low hematocrit (not platelets) is the independent risk factor!

DR Classifications & Diagnostic Pathways

DR Stage Key Diagnostic Criteria & Features Pathophysiologic Stage
Mild NPDR Microaneurysms ONLY (count ≤ 20 red dots). Asymptomatic. Earliest clinical sign: Focal capillary outpouching.
Moderate NPDR Microaneurysms (>20), Dot/Blot hemorrhages, Hard exudates, Cotton-wool spots. Does NOT satisfy 4-2-1 Rule. Increasing capillary leakage & local ischemia.
Severe NPDR Fulfills 4-2-1 Rule (Any 1 of the following):
1. Microaneurysms / Hemorrhages in ALL 4 quadrants
2. Venous Beading in 2+ quadrants
3. Intraretinal Microvascular Abnormalities (IRMA) in 1+ quadrant		 Severe pre-proliferative capillary non-perfusion & widespread hypoxia.
Proliferative DR (PDR) Neovascularization (NVD on disc, NVE elsewhere). Complications: Vitreous Hemorrhage, Subhyaloid Hemorrhage, Fibrovascular Traction Retinal Detachment. Severe ischemia → HIF-1 upregulation → Excessive VEGF surge → Angiogenesis.
⭐ Diabetic Macular Edema (DME)

DME can occur at ANY stage of NPDR or PDR! Characterized by fluid accumulation at the macula leading to central visual loss. Hard exudates (waxy yellow lipid rings) indicate chronic fluid leakage.

DR Management & Screening Guidelines

Treatment Modalities

  • Intravitreal Anti-VEGF: First-line for DME & PDR. Stabilizes blood-retinal barrier.
    - Ranibizumab & Aflibercept: FDA approved.
    - Bevacizumab: Off-label, low-cost colorectal chemo agent.
  • Laser Photocoagulation (LP):
    - Panretinal (PRP): Ablates peripheral photoreceptors/RPE to reduce O2 demand & drop VEGF (for Severe NPDR / PDR).
    - Focal LP: Treats focal leakage in DME.
    - Grid LP: Treats diffuse leakage in DME.
  • Pars Plana Vitrectomy (23-Gauge PPV): Indicated for non-clearing vitreous hemorrhage or tractional RD.
    - Port 1: Infusion (maintains IOP)
    - Port 2: Endoillumination Light Source
    - Port 3: Vitrector (cuts & aspirates)

Recommended Eye Exam Screening Schedule

Pregnancy + DMBefore conception / 1st TrimesterNo/Mild DR: 3–12 mos
Severe NPDR+: 1–3 mos

Rationale: Type 2 DM patients often have subclinical hyperglycemia for ~5 years before clinical diagnosis, so DR may already be present at diagnosis!

Hypertensive Retinopathy (HR)

Systemic hypertension causes increased basal arteriolar muscle tone via sympathetic stimulation, RAAS overactivity, and vessel wall sclerosis. Normal retinal Arteriole-to-Vein (A:V) ratio is 2:3. Arteriosclerosis reduces this ratio to 1:2 or 1:3.

Keith-Wagener-Barker (KWB) Classification

KWB Grade Retinal Findings Systemic Features & Prognosis
Grade 1 Slight functional narrowing, sclerosis, and tortuosity of retinal arterioles. A:V ratio ~ 1:2. Mild, asymptomatic hypertension.
Grade 2 Definite focal constriction, sclerosis, and Arteriovenous (AV) Nicking at crossing points. Higher sustained BP; few systemic symptoms.
Grade 3 Retinopathy: Flame-shaped hemorrhages, Cotton-wool spots (RNFL microinfarcts), hard exudates. Significantly elevated BP; headaches, mild cardiac/renal/cerebral dysfunction.
Grade 4 Neuroretinal Edema + Papilledema (Optic Disc Edema) + Elschnig spots / Siegrist streaks. Malignant HTN / Pre-eclampsia. Strongly associated with increased mortality!
🧠 Board Exam Tip: AV Nicking Pathophysiology

Retinal arteries and veins share a common adventitial sheath at crossing points. In chronic HTN, the thickened, sclerotic arterial wall compresses the underlying softer vein within the rigid shared sheath, causing venous stasis and distal constriction (AV Nicking).

Retinal Vein & Artery Occlusions

⭐ Pathophysiologic Rule of Thumb

Vein Occlusions = THROMBOTIC Phenomenon (Venous stasis, hyperviscosity, AV compression).
Artery Occlusions = EMBOLIC Phenomenon (Calcified plaques from Carotid Artery or Heart).

⚠️ Emergency Rule

CRAO is an Absolute Ophthalmic Emergency! Irreversible retinal tissue necrosis occurs after 90 minutes of complete occlusion ("The Golden Period").

Vascular Disorder Site of Occlusion Fundus Findings Management Strategy
BRVO
(Branch Vein)		 AV crossing defect (arteriosclerotic artery compresses vein) Hemorrhages & exudates localized to ONE quadrant along the occluded venous branch. Anti-VEGF for macular edema; LP for capillary non-perfusion/ischemia.
CRVO
(Central Vein)		 Lamina Cribrosa (scleral opening) "Blood & Thunder": Dilated, tortuous veins & flame hemorrhages in ALL 4 quadrants; disc edema. Intravitreal Anti-VEGF (monthly x 3 then treat & extend); LP for neovascularization.
BRAO
(Branch Artery)		 Branch retinal arteriole bifurcation Sectoral retinal whitening/pallor corresponding to the territory of the blocked arteriole. Ocular massage; system workup for embolic origin (carotid Doppler, echo).
CRAO
(Central Artery)		 Central Retinal Artery at optic nerve entrance Diffuse ground-glass retinal pallor/edema with classic Cherry-Red Spot at fovea. Emergency (<90 min): Ocular massage, AC paracentesis, Carbogen, Anti-IOP meds.

Clinical Highlight: Cilioretinal Artery Sparing in CRAO

Anatomic variant present in ~20% of the population

Pathophysiology: The Cilioretinal Artery arises from the short posterior ciliary arteries (choroidal supply), NOT the Central Retinal Artery. In CRAO with cilioretinal sparing, the fovea/macula remains perfused, preserving central visual acuity (e.g. 20/20) despite total perifoveal retinal pallor!

Retinal Detachment (RD)

Retinal Detachment is the separation of the neurosensory retina from the underlying Retinal Pigment Epithelium (RPE) at the potential subretinal space.

💡 Classic Symptom Triad of Retinal Detachment

1. Photopsia: Flashes of light (due to mechanical vitreoretinal traction on neurosensory retina).
2. Floaters: Dark spots or cobwebs (vitreous condensation / RBCs in vitreous).
3. Expanding Scotoma: "Curtain-like" painless visual field shadow progressing from peripheral to central vision.

Major Types of Retinal Detachment

RD Classification Primary Mechanism Common Etiologies Key Diagnostic / Management Features
Rhegmatogenous RD Full-thickness retinal break/tear allows liquefied vitreous gel into subretinal space. High myopia, trauma, post-cataract surgery, aging vitreous collapse. Retinal folds, visible tear. Treatment: Surgery (PPV, Scleral Buckle) + Laser retinopexy to seal tear.
Tractional RD Fibrovascular membranes pull neurosensory retina away from RPE (no tear required). Proliferative Diabetic Retinopathy (PDR), Trauma, Ischemic retinopathies. Concave detachment contour, fixed traction bands. Treatment: PPV with membrane peeling.
Exudative (Serous) RD Subretinal fluid accumulation due to RPE pump failure or hyperpermeability without tear. Severe HTN / Pre-eclampsia, choroidal tumors, posterior uveitis / inflammation. Smooth, dome-shaped detachment with shifting fluid. Treatment: Treat underlying medical cause.
⭐ Surgical Principles of Rhegmatogenous RD Repair

1. Identify ALL retinal breaks.
2. Close and seal the breaks (create chorioretinal scars using laser photocoagulation or cryotherapy).
3. Relieve/neutralize vitreoretinal traction (via vitrectomy or scleral buckle).

💡 High-Yield Board Buzzwords & Numeric Rules
  • 80%: Volume of eye occupied by vitreous humour.
  • 99% vs 1%: Vitreous is 99% water, 1% collagen/hyaluronic acid/hyalocytes.
  • 2:3 vs 1:2: Normal retinal A:V ratio is 2:3; narrows to 1:2 or 1:3 in HTN/arteriosclerosis.
  • 90 Minutes: Maximum Golden Window to treat CRAO before permanent retinal cell necrosis.
  • 70D / 90D vs 20D: 70D/90D lens used for Slit-lamp biomicroscopy; 20D lens used for Indirect Ophthalmoscopy.
  • Port 1 / Port 2 / Port 3 in 23-Gauge PPV: Port 1 = Infusion, Port 2 = Light source, Port 3 = Vitrector.
  • Bevacizumab: Off-label colorectal chemo drug used as budget-friendly Anti-VEGF.
