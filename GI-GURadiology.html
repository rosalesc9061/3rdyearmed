Exam Tip

Ultrasound Specifics

  • Anechoic: Purely black (e.g., bile, simple fluid, urinary bladder, blood vessels).
  • Hypoechoic: Black-appearing (complex fluid or solid lesion reference to surrounding).
  • Hyperechoic: Echogenic / White (structures like stones).

Strengths & Weaknesses of Modalities

Modality Strengths Weaknesses
X-ray Bones (fractures), Air-containing structures (bowel gas pattern). Readily available. Cartilage, soft tissue.
CT Scan Good soft tissue contrast. Best for extent of lesions beyond organ involved. Preferred first for complex abdominal evaluation due to MRI cost. Cartilage. Ionizing radiation.
Ultrasound Solid organs, superficial small parts, real-time vascular (Doppler). No radiation. Best for ascites. Air (number one enemy). Cannot visualize bowel loops unless fluid-filled.
MRI Superior soft tissue contrast. Best option when CT is equivocal. Motion artifact (lesser in new models), High cost, bone encased structures.
PET-CT (Nuclear) Functional (metabolic/biochemical activity). Combines precise anatomic localization with functional imaging (Fusion image). Good for metastasis. Anatomic weakness of conventional scanner solved by PET-CT combo.
Common Pitfall

Do not request UTZ for bowel evaluation unless you suspect fluid-filled loops, as the air in the intestines completely impedes the sound waves.

Abdominal X-Ray & Common Signs

The plain abdominal X-ray is the primary study requested for abdominal pain/enlargement because it provides an immediate overview of the bowel gas pattern.

Routine Positions

  • Upright: Best for visualizing fluid levels (e.g., gastric bubble at left subdiaphragmatic region).
  • Supine: Shows radiolucency outlining the stomach due to gas shift to the non-dependent aspect.

Normal Bowel Calibers

  • Small Intestine: Up to 3 cm
  • Large Intestine: Up to 4 cm

Calcifications appear radiopaque. Their anatomical location infers the involved organ.

  • RUQ Calcification: Likely Gallbladder stones (cluster, rounded, outside renal shadow). Next step: Ultrasound to confirm.
  • Overlying Renal Outline: Likely Kidney stone.
  • RLQ (overlapping iliac wing): Appendicolith (stone within appendix).
  • Along Paraspinal/Sacral Wing: Ureteral stones.

Masses are identified by inference (displacement of bowel loops).

  • Upper abdominal density displacing bowels inferiorly: Enlarged liver/spleen.
  • Left hemi-abdominal density displacing bowels rightward: Mass from left kidney or spleen.
  • Mass displacing transverse colon superiorly: Pelvo-abdominal mass.

Bowel Gas Patterns: Ileus vs Obstruction

Pathophysiology

Adynamic (Paralytic) Ileus

  • Definition: "Lazy bowel", generalized lack of peristalsis. Common in sedentary lifestyle or post-op (should resolve by 4th post-op day).
  • X-ray Findings: Normal caliber gas-containing small and large bowel loops. Disorganized pattern.
  • Rectal Gas: Present.
  • Air-Fluid Levels: Non-differential (air fluid is on the same level in a loop due to absent peristalsis).
Pathology

Segmental Ileus (Sentinel Loop)

  • Definition: Focal, gas-filled, localized distended loop responding to acute intra-abdominal inflammation.
  • Location correlates to inflammation:
    • RLQ: Appendicitis
    • RUQ: Cholecystitis
    • Upper/Central: Pancreatitis (Hallmark by X-ray)
Must Know

Bowel Obstruction

Radiographic Features:

  • Disproportionate bowel loop dilatation: Proximal to obstruction is dilated (>3cm SB, >4cm LB), distal is normal/collapsed.
  • Organized Pattern: Helps locate obstruction site (always distal to the distended loops).
    • Obstruction at distal ileum → multiple dilated proximal small intestines.
    • Obstruction at sigmoid → proximal descending/transverse dilated.
  • Rectal Gas: Negative if complete; Present if incomplete.
  • Air-Fluid Level: Differential air-fluid level in a loop (due to intact peristalsis fighting the obstruction).

Specific Signs:

  • Stack of coins: Separation of plicae circulares.
  • Stepladder sign: Shows number of dilated loops (more loops = more distal obstruction).
  • String-of-beads sign: Lateral decubitus view. Large fluid in small intestine; gas accumulates in non-dependent separated plicae.

Contrast Studies & GIT Pathologies

Esophagogram / Barium Swallow

Investigates pharynx down to esophagus in real-time. Indications: dysphagia, sensation of lump, vomiting.

Normal: Smooth mucosal outline, visible esophageal peristalsis. Cervical osteophytes can cause anterior indentations.

UGIS with Small Bowel Series

Extends to Stomach & Small Intestine (down to ligament of Treitz). Feathery appearance = Jejunum. Less defined = Ileum. Imaginary line from RUQ to LLQ separates Jejunum (top left) from Ileum (bottom right).

Barium Enema

Infused retrogradely via rectum. Evaluates colon (rectum to cecum). Identifies haustrations.

Specific GIT Pathologies

ConditionDetails & Radiographic Findings
Esophageal Stricture Benign (Inflammation/GERD): Tapered, smooth, circumferential narrowing.
Malignant: Abrupt, concentric/eccentric narrowing, irregular nodular mucosa.
Achalasia Failure of LES relaxation (Auerbach plexus destruction). Bird's beak deformity: Tapered narrowing at LES with proximal dilatation.
Esophageal Carcinoma Best seen on CT. Eccentric, irregular wall thickening. Serosal irregularity indicates extension beyond muscularis.
  • Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumor (GIST): Most common mesenchymal tumor. Long asymptomatic growth. Submucosal/Extraluminal mass indenting gastric lumen. Well-defined border.
  • Gastric Carcinoma: 95% Adenocarcinoma. Types: Polypoid (filling defect), Ulcerative, Infiltrating. Linitis Plastica: Leather-bottle configuration, diffuse infiltration causing stiff, narrowed lumen.
  • Duodenal Ulcer: 95% H. pylori. Ulcer crater fills with contrast, thickened mucosal folds radiate from crater.
  • Duodenal Diverticulum: Common in 2nd segment. Saccular outpouching with visible stalk. Change in configuration during peristalsis.
  • Widened Duodenal C-Loop: Highly suggestive of Pancreatic head mass (Malignancy or Pancreatic Pseudocyst).
  • Colonic Carcinoma: Most common abdominal malignancy. 50% in rectosigmoid. Presents with changed bowel habits. Apple Core Appearance: Short abrupt narrowing with overhanging edges on barium enema. PET-CT shows "Light Bulb" sign (hypermetabolic).
  • Diverticulosis: Outward herniation of mucosa/submucosa due to smooth muscle weakness (low-residue diet). Multiple thin-walled air-containing outpouchings, most common in Sigmoid.
  • Diverticulitis: Inflamed diverticula. CT shows thickened dense walls, pericolic fat stranding. Can perforate into a paracolic abscess (intralesional gas, luminal fluid).
  • Large Bowel Obstruction (LBO):
    • Competent Ileocecal Valve (ICV): Colonic distension increases rapidly, limited backflow.
    • Incompetent ICV: Retrograde gas flow into small intestine, centralized normal-caliber SB loops.

Pneumoperitoneum: Free air in peritoneal cavity (bowel perforation, trauma). Resolves in 3-4 days post-op.

  • Upright CXR: Subdiaphragmatic gas (crescent lucency).
  • Left Lateral Decubitus: For bedridden. Parahepatic air.
  • Signs: Double wall (Rigler) sign, Football sign.
  • CT: Air collects at non-dependent anterior portion (patient is supine).

Hernias: Organ pushes through defect.

  • Diaphragmatic: Congenital/acquired. Bowel/stomach in thorax. Bochdalek (Left), Morgagni (Right).
  • Umbilical: Common post-lap cholecystectomy. Reducible mass with fat attenuation (mesenteric tear).

Solid Organs (Liver, GB, Pancreas, Spleen)

Evaluated best via Ultrasound and CT. MRI utilized when CT is equivocal.

Gallbladder (GB) & Biliary Pathologies

  • Cholelithiasis: GB Stones. UTZ: Hyperechoic focus with posterior acoustic shadowing. X-ray: Multifaceted RUQ calcifications.
  • Biliary Sludge: Medium-level echoes, mixture of particulate matter/bile.
  • Sludge Ball: Mobile, intraluminal, hyperechoic, non-shadowing, non-vascular focus.
  • GB Polyp: Isoechoic, non-shadowing, immobile soft tissue focus attached to wall. >1cm = premalignant risk.
  • Cholecystitis: GB wall >2mm, pericholecystic fluid, fat stranding on CT (subacute/chronic).
  • Gangrenous Cholecystitis: Necrosis due to ischemia. Striated thickened wall, intraluminal membranes.
  • Emphysematous Cholecystitis: Gas in GB wall. Hyperechoic with dirty shadowing (UTZ), hypodense gas (CT).
  • Choledocholithiasis: CBD stones causing dilated intrahepatic ducts. Filling defect on ERCP.
Triphasic CT of Liver

Hepatic Nodules & Masses

Used to differentiate benign vs malignant liver lesions. Normal liver gets 80% blood from portal vein; tumors get 100% from hepatic artery.

  • Arterial Phase (18 sec): Lesions begin to enhance.
  • Venous Phase (35 sec): Nodules enhance fully (solid nature, hypervascular).
  • Delayed/Equilibrium (5-10 min): Contrast washes out of tumors. (If retained = Hemangioma).
  • Hepatomegaly: Span >15.5cm (midclavicular). Rounded inferior edge extending past lower pole of right kidney. Normal variants: Riedel's Lobe (elongated right tip), Beaver Tail (elongated left lobe wrapping spleen).
  • Hepatic Steatosis (Fatty Liver): Excess fat. UTZ: Increased echogenicity obscures portal vein walls, far-field becomes hypoechoic. CT: Liver attenuation is lower than spleen (Normal: Liver ~ Spleen).
  • Hepatic Cysts: Well-defined, smooth. Anechoic on UTZ (posterior acoustic enhancement). Hypodense on CT.
  • Hepatic Abscess: Irregular complex fluid. Doppler shows perilesional (not intralesional) vascularity due to inflammation. CT shows septal/rim enhancement.
  • Metastases: Multiple nodularities (hypo/hyperechoic).
  • Liver Cirrhosis: Small liver, lobulated margin, coarse parenchyma, associated with ascites. Pathogenesis: repetitive injury → excess collagen → fibrosis → cirrhosis.
  • Ultrasound Elastography: Best non-invasive alternative to biopsy to measure tissue stiffness and stage hepatic fibrosis before it becomes cirrhosis.

Pancreas:

  • Pancreatitis: Enlarged due to edema. Heterogeneously hypoechoic (UTZ) / hypodense (CT). Sentinel loop on X-ray.
  • Pseudocyst: Common benign fluid collection, can widen duodenal loop.
  • Solid Mass: Markedly irregular, obstructs pancreatic duct, encroaches on SMA/SMV.

Spleen:

  • Splenomegaly: Length >12cm. Splenic index >480. Often due to hematopoietic abnormalities or portal hypertension (look for tortuous dilated splenic vessels).
  • Splenic Calcifications: Hyperechoic (UTZ), Hyperdense (CT). Post-infectious.
  • Splenic Abscess: Complex mass fluid collection.

Genitourinary Tract, Retroperitoneum & Others

Kidney & Ureter

  • Medical Renal Parenchymal Disease: Non-specific for nephritis. Cortex becomes hyperechoic relative to liver.
  • Acute Nephritis: CT shows perirenal soft tissue fat stranding.
  • Staghorn Calculus: Large stone conforming to pelvocaliceal system. Best seen on X-ray/CT (Not UTZ).
  • Nephrolithiasis:
    • Non-obstructing: Cortical, no calyx dilation.
    • Obstructing: Intraluminal, causes pelvocaliceal dilation (hydronephrosis).
  • Ureterolithiasis: Diagnosed via CT Stonogram (Modality of choice). Can cause severe upstream hydronephrosis.
  • Renal Mass: CT Urogram to assess enhancing/non-enhancing masses pre- and post-op.

Bladder & Prostate

  • Cystolithiasis (Bladder Stones): Radiopaque (X-ray), Echogenic with strong shadow (UTZ), Hyperdense (CT).
  • Cystitis: Bladder wall > 3mm. (UTZ requires >150cc fluid for eval). CT shows nodular thickening.
  • Bladder Mass: Presents with hematuria. If near UVJ, obstructs distal ureter. If at bladder neck, causes urinary retention.
  • Prostate:
    • Trans-abdominal UTZ: Requires full bladder. Pre/post-void to check retention.
    • Transrectal UTZ: Modality of choice for enlarged prostate (BPH vs nodules).
    • MRI: Definitive for prostate carcinoma staging and radiotherapy planning.
    • Prostatomegaly: Causes chronic bladder outlet obstruction → chronic cystitis.

Ascites: Fluid in peritoneal cavity. Best confirmed via UTZ (sensitive to small amounts in Morrison pouch / pelvis). X-ray shows diffuse haziness, central floating bowels.

  • Serous: Cirrhosis, CHF, Hypoproteinemia. Anechoic (UTZ), -10 to +10 HU (CT).
  • Exudative: Infection/inflammation. Low-level echoes (UTZ), ≥ +15 HU (CT).
  • Hemoperitoneum: Trauma/Surgery. Medium echoes (UTZ), ≥ +45 HU (CT).
  • Neoplastic: Intraperitoneal tumors. Bloody fluid.

Aortic Aneurysm: Strong pulsatile mid-abdomen mass. CT shows fusion dilatation proximal to common iliac bifurcation, calcifications.

Hodgkin's Lymphoma: CT shows matted retroperitoneal masses (multiple focal lymphadenopathies).

Abdominal Wall Lipoma: Hypodense fat-attenuating mass in muscular layer, non-enhancing on contrast.
