Core Concepts & Executive Summary

Thyroid and parathyroid surgical management relies heavily on understanding functional status (TFTs), sonographic features, cytopathological classification (Bethesda system), and surgical anatomy to prevent catastrophic nerve or metabolic complications.

1. Anatomic & Functional Evaluation

Anteroinferior neck mass moving with deglutition confirms thyroid origin. TSH is the most sensitive initial assay. Low TSH prompts Radionuclide Scan; Normal/High TSH prompts Neck Ultrasound + FNABC.

2. Goiter & Malignancy Spectrum

Toxic goiters (Graves', Toxic MNG) require pre-op antithyroid stabilization. Well-Differentiated Carcinomas (Papillary & Follicular) account for ~90% of cancers; age (<55 vs ≥55) dictates AJCC prognosis.

3. Surgical Hazards & Parathyroid

Recurrent Laryngeal Nerve (RLN) runs in the tracheoesophageal groove (bilateral injury = airway emergency). Post-op hypocalcemia presents with Chvostek/Trousseau signs.

Initial Evaluation: Neck Mass & HPT Axis

An anterior neck mass that moves up with deglutition (swallowing) confirms an origin in the THYROID GLAND because the thyroid is anchored to the larynx by Berry's ligament.

CLINICAL REASONING CUE: If you see an anterior neck mass moving vertically with swallowing → Think Thyroid origin → Order initial Thyroid Function Tests (TSH, FT4, FT3).

Thyroid Function Tests (TFTs)

  • Serum TSH: Single most sensitive and specific test for diagnosing thyroid metabolic status.
  • Serum Free T4 & Free T3 (FT4/FT3): Measures biologically active hormones; crucial for quantifying degree of dysfunction.

HPT Axis Feedback Regulation

Hypothalamus releases TRH → Pituitary releases TSH → Thyroid produces T3/T4. There is an inverse relationship between Thyroid Hormone (TH) and TSH levels.

Radionuclide Imaging (Thyroid Scan)

Indicated ONLY if TFTs reveal hyperthyroidism (Low TSH). Uses Iodine-123 ($I^{123}$), Iodine-131 ($I^{131}$), or Technetium-99m ($^{99m}\text{Tc}$).

⚠️ Diagnostic Pitfall
$I^{123}$ or $I^{131}$ is preferred over $^{99m}\text{Tc}$ pertechnetate because 3–8% of warm nodules appear cold on pertechnetate scan, leading to potential misdiagnosis!
  • Hot / Warm Nodule: Demonstrates hyperfunctioning/increased radioisotope uptake. Treated as a "toxic" nodule (rarely malignant).
  • Cold Nodule: Hypoactive area trapping less radioactivity than surrounding gland. Requires FNABC to rule out cancer!

Thyroid Neck Ultrasonography (USG)

Ordered when TSH is normal/high. Key uses: guides FNAB, measures nodule size, detects non-palpable multicentric nodules, checks cervical lymphadenopathy, and evaluates substernal goiters.

USG Features: Benign vs Carcinoma

Benign Characteristics Carcinoma Characteristics
• Spongiform: "Honeycomb" or "puff pastry" microcystic spaces separated by thin septa. • Hypoechogenic: Dark/grayish tone.
• Simple cyst: Fluid-filled with no solid component. • Taller-than-wide shape (anteroposterior > transverse diameter).
• Isoechogenic / Hyperechogenic smooth round nodule. • Irregular/microlobulated margins.
• Microcalcifications (white specks).

TIRADS & Bethesda Categorization Systems

TIRADS (USG Cancer Risk)

  • TIRADS 1: Normal thyroid
  • TIRADS 2: Benign (spongiform or simple cyst)
  • TIRADS 3: Probably benign (no suspicious features)
  • TIRADS 4: Suspicious for CA (≥1 suspicious feature)
  • TIRADS 5: Highly suggestive of CA (all suspicious features)

Fine Needle Aspiration Biopsy (FNABC)

Single most useful diagnostic modality for thyroid nodules in non-hyperthyroid patients.

⭐ Major FNABC Limitations: 1. Cannot differentiate Follicular Adenoma from Follicular Carcinoma (requires histopathology showing capsular/vascular invasion).
2. Contraindicated in hot/hyperthyroid nodules (causes bleeding & false positives due to hypertrophic cells).

Bethesda Criteria & Management Algorithm (Figure 11)

Bethesda Category Cytologic Interpretation Standard Management Action
Category I Non-diagnostic / Unsatisfactory Repeat FNAB with Ultrasound Guidance
Category II Benign (Colloid nodule / Simple cyst) Observe / Aspirate cyst (Surgery if >4 cm or recurs 3x)
Category III AUS / FLUS (Atypia / Follicular Lesion of Undetermined Significance) Repeat FNAB
Category IV FN / Suspicious for FN (Follicular Neoplasm) Thyroid Lobectomy
Category V Suspicious for Malignancy Lobectomy or Near-total / Total Thyroidectomy
Category VI Malignant (e.g., Papillary CA) Near-total or Total Thyroidectomy

Specialized Diagnostic Work-ups

Antibodies & Markers

  • Anti-TPO Antibodies: Order if Hashimoto's thyroiditis is suspected.
  • Thyroglobulin (Tg): Tumor marker used to monitor cancer recurrence after total thyroidectomy.
  • Calcitonin: Virtually diagnostic for Medullary Thyroid Cancer (MTC).

Imaging & Endoscopy

  • Neck CT Scan: Indicated for fixed lesions with borderline resectability or organ invasion.
  • Laryngoscopy: Essential to assess vocal cord mobility if hoarseness or compression occurs.

MEN Syndrome Work-up

  • If MTC or family history of MEN 2A/2B is present → check serum calcitonin + screen for pheochromocytoma and hyperparathyroidism!

Thyroid Cyst & Goiter Taxonomy

Thyroid Cyst

Etiology: Unknown (theories: iodine lack, autoimmune, radiation). Diagnosed by USG. 75% resolve with simple needle aspiration.

⚠️ Indications for Surgery (Thyroidectomy) in Cysts: 1. Recurrence after 3 needle aspirations.
2. Cyst diameter > 4 cm.

Goiter Classification & Compressive Signs

  • Diffuse vs Nodular: Diffuse has no discrete nodules; Nodular presents with ≥1 nodules.
  • Toxic vs Nontoxic: Toxic = Hyperthyroid state; Nontoxic = Euthyroid/Normal TH.
  • Compressive Symptoms: Dysphagia, orthopnea, dysphonia.
PEMBERTON SIGN: Facial flushing and cervical vein dilation when raising arms above head → indicates thoracic inlet venous obstruction from a substernal goiter.
Figure 14 & 15: Pathophysiology of Nontoxic vs Toxic Goiter
Nontoxic Goiter Mechanism

Low TH / Iodine Deficiency → High TSH secretion → Thyroid overcompensates → Gland Hypertrophy / Hyperplasia = Nontoxic Goiter.

Toxic Goiter Mechanism

Increased TRH/TSH (e.g. pituitary tumor) OR Autonomous Nodules → Excessive TH release → Hypertrophy = Toxic Goiter.

Hyperthyroidism & Diffuse Toxic Goiter (Graves' Disease)

Autoimmune condition (TSH receptor antibodies stimulate thyrocytes). Prevalent in females 40–60 yo. Triggers: postpartum, excess iodine, lithium, viral infections.

⭐ Clinical Manifestations of Thyrotoxicosis
Heat intolerance, weight loss, fine tremors, proximal muscle weakness, warm moist skin, amenorrhea, pretibial dermopathy, ophthalmopathy.
MOST RELIABLE CLINICAL SIGN: Resting Tachycardia.

Treatment Modalities for Graves' Disease

Treatment Type Mechanism / Agent Clinical Advantages Drawbacks & Exam Traps
Antithyroid Drugs (ATDs) Propylthiouracil (PTU 100-300mg TID) or Methimazole (10-30mg TID) Prepares patients for definitive surgery/RAI; control thyrotoxicosis High relapse rate if discontinued; risks of Agranulocytosis, vasculitis, hepatotoxicity.
Radioactive Iodine (RAI) $I^{131}$ ablation of hyperactive thyroid tissue Non-surgical; ideal for small goiters (<100 mL), high surgical risk Gradual effect (3-6 mos); Contraindicated in pregnancy / breastfeeding.
Thyroidectomy (Surgery) Subtotal or Total excision of thyroid gland Immediate cure; required for large goiters (>100 mL), compression, suspicious nodules Must render patient EUTHYROID with ATDs first to avoid intraoperative THYROID STORM!

Toxic Multinodular Goiter & Jod-Basedow

Occurs in patients >50 years old with a prior history of nontoxic multinodular goiter. Over years, nodules acquire autonomy.

JOD-BASEDOW PHENOMENON: Patient with nontoxic goiter given exogenous iodine (CT contrast, kelp supplements, amiodarone) → escapes negative feedback → Iodine-Induced Hyperthyroidism.

Diagnosis & Tx: Scan shows multiple hot nodules. Treatment is Thyroidectomy (after ATD prep).

Nontoxic Goiter (DNTG vs NNTG)

Results from iodine deficiency or TSH stimulation. Patients are euthyroid (Normal TSH/FT4/FT3).

TSH Suppression vs Surgery:

  • TSH Suppression: High-dose thyroxine suppresses TSH. Best for young patients with small nodules.
  • Contraindications to TSH Suppression: Postmenopausal women, age >60, osteoporosis, cardiovascular disease.
  • Indications for Surgery: Large goiter, compressive symptoms, or suspicious nodules.

Benign & Malignant Thyroid Neoplasms

Benign Adenomas

Follicular & Hurthle Cell Adenoma: CANNOT be distinguished from carcinoma by FNAB. Histology must show NO capsular/vascular invasion.
Toxic Adenoma (Plummer's): Single hot nodule in younger patients. Scan shows single hot spot with rest of gland suppressed.

Well-Differentiated CA (WDC)

Comprises ~90% of thyroid cancers.
• Papillary (PTC) - 80%: 90-95% cure rate, 30-45% lymph node metastasis.
• Follicular (FCA) - 10%: 70-80% cure rate, hematogenous spread (lungs/bones).

Rare & Aggressive Cancers

• Hurthle Cell (3%): Oxyphilic cells; Only ~5% take up RAI!
• Medullary (5%): Parafollicular C cells; secretes Calcitonin.
• Anaplastic (1%): Highly lethal; rapid growth; requires tracheostomy.

✅ Papillary Thyroid Carcinoma Histopathology Cues
Presence of two pathognomonic features confirms PTC: 1. Orphan Annie Nuclei (cells with clear intranuclear cytoplasmic inclusions).
2. Psammoma Bodies (concentric calcified microcalcification deposits).

WDC Risk Stratification & Surgical Algorithms

Prognostic scoring systems (AGES, MACIS, AMES, AJCC) classify low vs high risk. Under AJCC TNM 8th Edition, AGE (<55 vs ≥55 years) is the single most important factor.

Complications of Thyroidectomy

Complication Incidence Anatomical / Pathophysiologic Basis Clinical Presentation & Management
Post-op Hemorrhage 1% Arterial bleeding into closed neck compartment Emergency! Bulging neck mass, pale, hypotensive, airway compromise. Immediate bedside decompression.
Unilateral Recurrent Laryngeal Nerve (RLN) Injury 3–8% Nerve damaged in tracheoesophageal groove Hoarseness. Note: Edema can cause temporary hoarseness. Wait 3–6 mos; confirm by laryngoscopy.

Calcium Homeostasis & Parathyroid Disorders

PTH (Parathyroid Hormone)

Increases serum calcium by driving bone resorption, renal calcium reabsorption, and activating Vitamin D in kidneys.

Vitamin D

Stimulates active absorption of calcium and phosphate from the GI tract.

Calcitonin

Secreted by thyroid C cells; decreases serum calcium by inhibiting osteoclast activity.

⭐ Primary Hyperparathyroidism Clinical Presentation
Classic "Pentad" (Rare): Kidney stones, Painful bones, Abdominal groans, Psychic moans, Fatigue.
Most patients today are asymptomatic or present with non-specific weakness, polyuria, constipation, and memory loss.

Hypoparathyroidism & Hypocalcemia Signs

Most common cause: Inadvertent surgical removal or vascular injury during Total Thyroidectomy. Other causes: DiGeorge syndrome, neonatal hypoparathyroidism.

Chvostek Sign

Tapping on the facial nerve anterior to the ear elicits ipsilateral facial muscle contraction/twitching due to increased neuromuscular excitability from hypocalcemia.

Trousseau Sign

Occluding brachial artery blood flow with a BP cuff inflated to 20 mmHg above systolic BP for 2–3 minutes triggers carpopedal spasm (carpal spasm in hand/wrist).

MANAGEMENT OF HYPOCALCEMIA: Oral calcium tablets + Vitamin D supplements. If severe symptomatic tetany occurs → Administer IV Calcium Gluconate infusion immediately!

⚡ Ultra-Condensed Pre-Exam Cram Sheet

Designed to be read in under 10 minutes prior to entering the examination room. Bullet points and high-yield rapid tables only.

1. Diagnostic Work-up Rules

  • Neck mass moving up with deglutition = THYROID ORIGIN.
  • Initial test for all thyroid nodule suspects = Serum TSH (Most sensitive/specific).
  • If TSH is LOW (Hyperthyroid) → Order Radionuclide Scan ($I^{123}/I^{131}$ preferred over $^{99m}\text{Tc}$ because 3-8% warm nodules look cold on Tc).
  • If TSH is NORMAL/HIGH → Order Neck Ultrasound.
  • FNABC is single most useful test for non-hyperthyroid nodules, BUT cannot differentiate Follicular Adenoma from Follicular Carcinoma.
  • FNABC is CONTRAINDICATED in hot nodules due to bleeding and false positive hypertrophic cells.

2. Quick Sonographic Differentiators

  • Benign USG: Spongiform ("honeycomb/puff pastry") appearance or Simple cyst.
  • Malignant USG: Hypoechogenic, Taller-than-wide shape, Microcalcifications, Irregular margins.
  • Thyroid Cyst Surgery indications: Recurrence after 3 aspirations OR size > 4 cm.

5. Surgical Complications & Parathyroid Quick Rules

  • Unilateral RLN injury: Hoarseness. (Wait 3-6 mos for edema).
  • Bilateral RLN injury: Midline vocal cord paralysis → Emergency Tracheostomy.
  • SLN injury: Loss of high pitch / voice strength.
  • Chvostek Sign: Facial nerve tap → facial twitch = Hypocalcemia.
  • Trousseau Sign: BP cuff >20 mmHg SBP for 2-3 mins → Carpopedal spasm = Hypocalcemia.
  • Post-op Total Thyroidectomy Monitoring: Serum Thyroglobulin (Tg) for WDC recurrence.
